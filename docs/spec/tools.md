# Authoring tools

`RecursiveAI.OnePhysics.Tools` holds geometry processing used when content is made or loaded. Every tool is a pure function of its inputs and options: the same inputs give the same output bits on every platform, vector width and thread count. All arithmetic is IEEE `+ - * /` and `sqrt` (double inside, float32 at the boundary); the few trigonometric functions the test generators use come from onemath. No tool reads a clock, a hash order or a machine property. Errors are `GeometryToolException` (kinds: Empty, NonFinite, OutOfRange, BadIndex, Degenerate, OpenMesh, NonManifold, InvertedWinding, SelfIntersecting, InvalidOptions); the message names the input and the position of the offending value.

## Input limits

- Meshes: at least one triangle (cleaning accepts none), at most 2^24 vertices and 2^24 triangles, coordinates finite with magnitude at most 2^21 (the engine hull limit), indices in range, index count a multiple of 3.
- 2D polygons: 3 to 16384 points in total over outline and holes, finite, magnitude at most 2^20, nonzero coordinates not below 2^-80 (so the exact orientation predicates stay in range). Consecutive duplicate points and a repeated closing point are dropped.
- Tetrahedralization takes the mesh limits above (at most 2^24 vertices and triangles, coordinates within 2^21).
- Skeletons (ragdoll fitting): 1 to 1024 bones, parents -1 or a bone index (no cycle, at least one root), bone and skin coordinates finite and within 2^15 (so that fitted sizes stay within the engine's 65536 m limit), at most 2^24 skin points with 1 to 16 influences each, weights finite and not negative, influence bone indices in range wherever the weight is above zero. Bone names may be null or empty.
- Sprites: width and height 1 to 16384 and at most 2^26 pixels; alpha has exactly width x height bytes.

## Mesh weld and clean (`MeshCleaner`)

1. Weld tolerance `t` = option, or 10^-6 times the longest axis extent of the vertices. Vertices are sorted by (x, index); every pair at distance at most `t` is linked (a sweep along x; single linkage, so chains of close vertices merge). The lowest index of a group is kept, at its own position.
2. Triangles are remapped. Removed as degenerate: a repeated vertex, zero area, or smallest height (2 x area / longest edge) at most `t`. Of triangles with the same vertex set (either winding) only the first stays.
3. Surviving vertices keep their relative order; unreferenced ones are dropped. Triangles keep their order.
4. Edges are grouped by their sorted vertex pair. Components are found by breadth-first search from the lowest unvisited triangle across edges shared by exactly two triangles; a neighbour is flipped (indices 1 and 2 swapped) when it uses the shared edge in the same direction. A component whose triangles all have three such neighbours (closed) is flipped as a whole if its signed volume is negative. Open components keep the orientation of their lowest triangle.
5. Report: component count, edges with 3 or more triangles (sorted by vertex pair), boundary edge count, and holes = connected groups of boundary edges.

Cleaning is idempotent bit for bit. The input arrays are not modified.

## Mass properties (`MeshMass`)

Closed means every directed edge occurs once and its reverse once. Volume, centre of mass and inertia come from the signed tetrahedra (reference point, triangle) about the vertex mean, with the simplex second moments of Tonon (2004): the integral of x x^T over a tetrahedron is V/20 (sum p_k p_k^T + (sum p_k)(sum p_k)^T); inertia = trace(C) I - C about the centre of mass (Mirtich 1996; Eberly, "Polyhedral Mass Properties (Revisited)"). Unit density; `Mass` is the volume. Negative volume throws InvertedWinding, zero volume Degenerate. For a convex mesh the result equals `ConvexHull3D.MassProperties` to single precision rounding.

## Hull simplification (`HullSimplifier`)

Greedy vertex removal: rank vertices by distance from the centroid of their neighbours (ties by index), re-hull the rest for the first 12 candidates and keep the removal with the smallest measured error (first wins ties). Error = the largest distance from any original vertex to the current hull (exact point to polyhedron distance over every face triangle). The simplified hull is the hull of a subset of the original vertices, so it lies inside the original and this equals the Hausdorff distance. Vertex and face targets are always pursued; beyond them removal continues while the error stays at most `MaxError`. `TargetsMet` is false if four vertices or a degenerate result stops it.

## 3D convex decomposition (`ConvexDecomposer`)

After Mamou and Ghorbel (ICIP 2009), with Lien and Amato's distance-based concavity (2004) and the worst-part-first refinement of Wei et al. (SIGGRAPH 2022).

1. Clean the mesh with default options. Fail with Degenerate when no triangle survives or the smallest bounding-box extent is below 10^-5 of the largest.
2. Voxelize: cell `h` = longest extent / `Resolution`; the grid starts 1.5 cells before the mesh minimum and has `ceil(extent / h) + 4` cells per axis. A cell is a surface cell when a triangle meets it (separating-axis box test on the triangle's plane distance, then clipping to the box by Sutherland-Hodgman, keeping non-empty clips). Every clipped polygon is stored with its triangle's outward unit normal. Empty cells not reachable from the grid corner by 6-neighbour flood fill (queue in index order) through non-surface cells are interior.
3. A part is an axis-aligned box over the mesh. Its surface points are the stored polygons clipped to the box, except polygons that only touch the box along an edge or a point or lie exactly on a box face with their normal pointing out of the box, plus the corners of the cross-sections where the box face planes cut interior cells. The hull of these points (at most 128 vertices) is the part's hull; its **concavity** is the largest, over surface points, of the distance a ray from the point along its outward normal travels inside the hull before leaving it (faces whose normal opposes the ray are ignored), divided by the mesh's bounding-box diagonal.
4. Starting from the whole mesh, repeat: choose the splittable part of largest concavity above `MaxConcavity` (lowest index on ties) while fewer than `MaxHulls` parts exist. Candidate cuts per axis: 6 evenly spaced planes plus up to 6 planes at mesh vertex coordinates, at least a quarter cell from the part's faces and from each other. Each candidate cost is `max(c1, c2) + 0.5 min(c1, c2) + 0.02 |n1 - n2| / (n1 + n2)` where `n` counts solid cells whose centre is in the child. The lowest cost wins (candidate order on ties: axis, then plane position); candidates are evaluated into fixed slots, in parallel when `MaxThreads` > 1, and compared in index order, so the result does not depend on the thread count. A part with no valid cut stops being splittable.
5. Output hulls in creation order; a hull with more than `MaxVerticesPerHull` (default 64) vertices is reduced by `HullSimplifier`. `ThresholdMet` is false if a part above the threshold remains.

Defaults: 16 hulls, concavity 0.01, 64 vertices per hull, resolution 32. Hulls are in the mesh's own coordinates; `ToCompoundParts` places each at the origin.

Stated accuracy (own test meshes): a box is one hull; the L-block 2 hulls and the U-block 3 hulls at the exact volume; a torus at concavity 0.01 and a mug at 0.05 meet their thresholds with at most 40 and 32 hulls, the union covering over 90% of the torus. The voxel grid limits the smallest feature found to about one cell.

## 2D polygon decomposition (`PolygonDecomposer`)

1. Outline and holes are normalised (outline counter-clockwise, holes clockwise). Every edge pair is tested with onemath's exact segment predicates: crossing, touching or overlapping edges (other than neighbours of one loop meeting at their shared vertex) are SelfIntersecting. Each hole must lie inside the outline (even-odd test) and in no other hole.
2. Holes are joined by bridges in order of decreasing maximum x (ties by index): from the hole's rightmost vertex (lowest index on ties) to the nearest ring vertex (distance, then ring position) that is visible, whose interior wedge contains the bridge and that no edge of the ring or of an unmerged hole blocks. The ring then contains both bridge ends twice.
3. Ear clipping (Eberly): a vertex is an ear when convex and no non-convex vertex lies inside or on its triangle (vertices equal to a triangle corner ignored); zero-area vertices are removed without a triangle. If no ear exists the test relaxes to strict interior containment once before failing with SelfIntersecting.
4. Hertel-Mehlhorn (1983): in triangle then edge order, a diagonal is removed when the merged piece stays convex at the two junction vertices and has at most `MaxVerticesPerPiece` (default 8, 3 to 64) corners.
5. Output pieces counter-clockwise, collinear vertices dropped; the pieces share vertices exactly, sum to the polygon area, and do not overlap. The count is at most four times the minimum (Hertel-Mehlhorn).

## Sprite outlines (`SpriteOutliner`)

1. The mask is padded with a zero border. A sample is solid when alpha is at least the threshold (default 128). Marching squares over cells between sample centres: each edge whose samples straddle the threshold carries one crossing at the linearly interpolated position; the two ambiguous cells keep diagonal solids apart. Segments are directed so the solid is on the left in image coordinates, and loops are followed from the lowest unvisited crossing id (cell row-major, horizontal edge before vertical).
2. Loops below `MinArea` square pixels are dropped. Each loop is simplified by Douglas-Peucker split at its first point and the point furthest from it, with the pixel tolerance (default 1). If simplification makes any loops cross or touch, the tolerance is halved (up to 10 times, then 0 = the raw contour).
3. Outer loops and holes are told apart by winding; each hole belongs to the smallest-area outer loop containing it. Islands inside holes are outer loops of their own. Output is ordered by the creation order of the loops. Points map to `(x * PixelSize + Origin.X, (height - y) * PixelSize + Origin.Y)` (y up; `KeepImageRows` leaves y growing down); outers are counter-clockwise and holes clockwise as the numbers read.
4. `ToConvexPieces` decomposes each island with `PolygonDecomposer`.

## Tetrahedralization (`Tetrahedralizer`)

Fills a closed, consistently oriented triangle mesh with tetrahedra for soft bodies. The route is isosurface stuffing (Labelle and Shewchuk, SIGGRAPH 2007) reduced to its lattice and warping steps, with exact inside tests and Si's quality measures (TetGen, ACM TOMS 2015).

1. The mesh must be closed, manifold and positively oriented: `MeshMass.ComputeDouble` runs first, so an open mesh is OpenMesh, a mixed or reversed one NonManifold or InvertedWinding, a flat one Degenerate. Self-intersection is not detected.
2. The lattice cell `a` is `TargetEdgeLength`, or `cbrt(12 MaxVolume)` when only `MaxVolume` is set, or the smaller of the two when both are; with neither, the longest extent over 16. `extent / a` may not exceed 512, the lattice may not exceed 2^21 cells (InvalidOptions naming the limit).
3. The lattice is body-centred cubic: grid points at `O + (i, j, k) a` and cell centres at `O + (i + 1/2, j + 1/2, k + 1/2) a`, with `O = (min x - 1.818 a, min y - 1.641 a, min z - 1.777 a)` (an irregular fraction, so flat faces of the input rarely lie on lattice planes) and `ceil((max - O) / a) + 2` cells per axis. Its Delaunay tetrahedra are, for every grid edge (three per grid point, along x, y and z), the four tetrahedra made of the edge's two end points and two cell centres that are neighbours around the edge, taken in the cyclic order (-,-), (+,-), (+,+), (-,+) of the two cross-axes (offsets in cells from the edge's grid point; offsets -1 or 0). A tetrahedron whose centres lie outside the lattice is dropped; the vertex order of each is fixed by the sign of its determinant in half-cell integer coordinates (the last two vertices are swapped when negative), so every tetrahedron has positive volume. Interior tetrahedra have volume `a^3 / 12`, edges `a` (a third) and `0.866 a`, radius-edge ratio 0.79.
4. A tetrahedron is kept when its centroid is inside the surface. Inside is the parity of the crossings of a ray along +x with the triangles, found in a uniform grid of triangles (cell `a`, the lattice's own origin, triangles listed in every cell their bounding box touches, in triangle order). A ray through an edge or vertex is resolved by simulation of simplicity: the ray is moved by an infinitesimal (e, e^2) in (y, z), so each triangle is decided by onemath's exact `Orient2D` of the projected triangle (through `SafePredicates`: the exact stage of onemath's predicates refuses coordinates below 2^-140, and mesh coordinates can be float32 subnormals, so out-of-range input is multiplied by a power of two that brings its largest coordinate just under 2^200 — the sign of a determinant does not change under uniform scaling — and a coordinate more than 340 binades under the largest is taken as zero) and, where that is zero, by the sign of `e (az - bz) + e^2 (by - ay)` for the directed edge (a, b). Where the four cells around a tetrahedron's edge hold no triangle, the parity at the first such cell's centre decides all of them (computed once per cell).
5. Vertices used by kept tetrahedra are numbered in lattice order (grid points, then centres) and rounded to float32. Boundary vertices (those of faces used by one tetrahedron) are moved onto the surface, in vertex order, three sweeps: the target is the nearest surface point within 2 cells (nearest triangle by the grid, closest point after Ericson), and the move is tried at 1, 0.75, 0.5 and 0.25 of the way, rounded to float32, taking the first that is accepted. A move is accepted when every tetrahedron around the vertex keeps positive volume and either stays at or above a volume ratio of 0.3 (six volumes over the cube of the longest edge) or does not get worse than before, and either stays at or below the radius-edge bound (`MaxRadiusEdgeRatio`, default 2) or does not get worse than before. So the radius-edge ratio of every tetrahedron stays at most 2 (or the lattice's own 0.79), and the volume ratio at least 0.3 for every tetrahedron a move touched.
6. Pinches are removed: while a boundary edge has more than two boundary faces, the tetrahedron of highest index on it is dropped (at most 64 rounds); then unused vertices are dropped, preserving order.
7. Boundary faces are the faces used once, ordered by (tetrahedron, face), oriented outward: for a tetrahedron (v0 v1 v2 v3) of positive volume the faces are (1 2 3), (0 3 2), (0 1 3), (0 2 1). Each input vertex gets a `SurfaceBinding`: the closest point of the boundary (nearest face by a grid over the boundary, brute force if the rings find none), as barycentric weights on its tetrahedron's four vertices (the vertex opposite the face has weight 0).
8. Report: volume, mesh volume, worst radius-edge ratio, smallest volume ratio, the surface deviation (the largest distance from any input vertex to the boundary, and from any boundary vertex, edge midpoint and face centre to the input surface; NaN when skipped), the cell, and `QualityMet`.

Stated accuracy on the test meshes (sphere, torus, a generated character-like blob; 10 to 24 cells across): every tetrahedron has positive volume; radius-edge at most 2 (the default bound); volume ratio at least 0.3; total volume 94 to 100 % of the mesh volume (96 to 99.6 % from 12 cells across on); surface deviation at most 0.6 cell (0.32 to 0.55 measured). The deviation is not smaller because a vertex is not moved when that would break a quality bound. Soft bodies need enough cells across the thinnest feature (the engine's soft solver inverts the boundary tetrahedra of finer meshes under impact more often, and 2 to 3 cells across a limb is thin: see the scene below). Threads (`MaxThreads`): the per-cell classification, the per-tetrahedron classification and the measuring passes run in blocks of fixed size writing disjoint output, so the result does not depend on the count.

## 2D quality triangulation (`TriangleMesher`)

**Output resolution.** Triangles are oriented counter-clockwise exactly in double; the vertices are then rounded to float32, so a triangle narrower than that rounding (a few ulps of its largest coordinate times its edge lengths) may come out degenerate or marginally clockwise. Input outlines with vertices closer than that resolution are the usual cause.

Delaunay refinement of a polygon with holes: Ruppert (J. Algorithms 1995), Chew's size rule, Shewchuk's handling of encroached segments (2002).

1. The polygon is validated as for `PolygonDecomposer` (outline counter-clockwise, holes clockwise; simple, not touching; each hole inside the outline and in no other hole). The size bound is the area of an equilateral triangle of edge `TargetEdgeLength` (default extent over 16); the angle bound `MinAngleDegrees` (default 25, at most 34) gives the radius-edge bound `1 / (2 sin angle)`; `MaxVertices` (default 50 000) caps the output.
2. A triangle 2000 extents wide holds the points. Points are added by the Bowyer-Watson cavity: the triangle containing the point is found by a walk with exact `Orient2D` (falling back to a scan in index order), the cavity is the connected set of triangles whose circumcircle strictly holds the point (exact `InCircle`), starting there, not growing across boundary pieces unless a piece is being split; each new triangle takes the inside or outside label of the cavity triangle that owned its edge; a cavity not star-shaped about the point refuses the insertion.
3. The polygon's points go in in loop order (outline, then holes). Boundary pieces are the polygon's edges. Phase 1 splits every piece whose closed diametral disk holds another vertex at its midpoint (queue in creation order, every vertex and piece rechecked after a split), then splits any piece that is still not an edge of the triangulation, until neither occurs (at most 64 rounds). A piece shorter than 2e-5 of the extent is never split.
4. Triangles touching the enclosing triangle's corners are outside; labels spread across edges that are not boundary pieces, flipping at a piece: outside, inside, hole...
5. Phase 2 refines the inside triangles in creation order (queue, then every new triangle appended): a triangle whose circumradius over shortest edge exceeds the bound, or whose area exceeds the size bound, is bad; its circumcentre is computed; if it lies in the closed diametral disk of any boundary piece, those pieces (lowest index first) are split instead and the triangle is looked at again; otherwise the circumcentre is inserted if it falls inside an inside triangle. A skinny triangle whose shortest edge is under 2e-5 of the extent is left (this is where polygon corners narrower than the angle bound end the refinement). The run stops at `MaxVertices`; `QualityMet` is then false.
6. Output: inside triangles in creation order; the polygon's points first in input order, then the other vertices in creation order; coordinates rounded to float32.

Stated accuracy: on the test polygons (a rectangle, an annulus, a star, a square with two holes, an L) the smallest angle is at least 25 degrees at the default (25.1 to 31.7 measured), the triangles tile the polygon (area within 1e-5), every mesh boundary edge lies along a polygon edge, every polygon point is a vertex. The Ruppert guarantee holds for angles up to 20.7 degrees where the polygon's own corners are at least 90 degrees; a polygon with a narrower corner cannot get a better angle there.

## Ragdoll fitting (`AutoRagdoller`)

Makes a `RagdollDef` (3D) or `RagdollDef` (2D) from a bone hierarchy and, optionally, skinned points. The skeleton is in the engine's frame (y up; a person faces +z in 3D, whose left is +x; in 2D the figure faces `Facing` along x).

1. **Roles.** A bone's name is cut after its last ':' or '|', split into lower-case letter tokens at separators, digits and lower-to-upper changes ("mixamorig:LeftUpLeg" gives left, up, leg), and tested in this order against the token set and the concatenated tokens: Merge for twist, roll, helper, corrective, pole, nub, ctrl, jiggle, target, end, top, the token ik, and thumb, index, middle, ring, pinky, pinkie, finger; Toe (toe, toebase, toes, ball); Foot (foot, ankle); Hand (hand, wrist, palm); Forearm (forearm, lowerarm, lowarm, elbow, radius, ulna); Merge for clavicle, collar, shoulder, scapula; UpperArm (upperarm, uparm, humerus, or the token arm without fore or lower); Thigh (thigh, upleg, upperleg, femur); Shin (calf, shin, lowerleg, lowleg, knee, tibia); a bare leg token (Thigh below the hips or a spine, Shin below a thigh); Head; Neck; Chest (chest, thorax, ribcage, torso); Spine (spine, abdomen, waist, stomach, lumbar, belly); Pelvis (pelvis, the token hips, or the token hip with no side token); Thigh for hip with a side; Merge for root, armature, skeleton, origin, world, reference, bip, master; otherwise Generic. `RoleOverrides` (by exact name) replaces the result for the bones it names. A bone with no part of its own belongs to its nearest ancestor that has one.
2. **Humanoid.** A skeleton with a Pelvis bone, exactly two Thigh bones below it, each with a Shin and then a Foot below, and at least one Spine or Chest bone below the pelvis outside the legs, is a person. The head is the shallowest Head bone; Neck bones above it join its part; two UpperArm bones, each with a Forearm below (and a Hand below that, if any), give the arms (otherwise the arm bones go with the torso). Parts, parents first: pelvis (root), torso (one part holding every spine bone; `SplitTorso` with two or more spine bones makes an abdomen of the lower half and a chest of the rest), head, the left arm (upper arm, forearm, and hand with `IncludeHands`) then the right arm, the left leg (thigh, shin, foot) then the right. Left is the side of the larger x (ties: lower bone index). That is 13 parts, 15 with hands, 14 with a split torso, 16 with both. Anything else is a generic skeleton.
3. **Generic skeleton.** Bones in depth-first pre-order (children by index); every bone that is not Merge becomes a part unless its length (to its primary child, the one with the largest subtree, or half the distance to its parent for a leaf) is under `MinBoneLength` (default 2 % of the height or half the largest extent, whichever is larger) and a part exists already. A part's parent is the part of its nearest ancestor; a part with none goes under part 0. More than 64 parts is OutOfRange.
4. **Axes and shapes.** A part's axis runs from its joint (its first bone) to its end: the child part's joint for chains (torso to head, arm, forearm to wrist, thigh to knee, shin to ankle), the farthest bone of the part otherwise, a default stub when none. With skin points (each goes to the part that its bones weigh most on, summed per part, ties to the lower part), limbs are capsules along the axis: radius the median distance of the part's points from the axis (the 90th percentile in 2D, where a side view puts most points inside the silhouette), length from the extreme points along the axis; pelvis, torso, hands and feet are boxes on the world axes around their points, the head a sphere at the centre of the bounding box with the median distance as radius; a generic part whose points are flat (the two principal moments across the bone, from onemath's symmetric eigen decomposition of their covariance, differing by more than 4 to 1) is a box on the principal axes (long axis along the bone). Without skin, proportions of an adult scaled to the height: thigh, shin, upper arm, forearm radius 0.19, 0.143, 0.145, 0.148 of the segment; head sphere radius 0.0611 H; pelvis half sizes (0.0833, 0.0444, 0.05) H; torso 0.0944 H wide, 0.0556 H deep; foot (0.0333, 0.0222, 0.0833) H (2D 0.0778 long). Sizes are at least 0.002 H.
5. **Mass.** A humanoid shares `TotalMass` by Winter's segment table (Dempster): pelvis 0.142, trunk 0.355 (abdomen 0.139 and chest 0.216 when split), head with neck 0.081, upper arm 0.028, forearm 0.016 (0.022 with the hand folded in), hand 0.006, thigh 0.100, shank 0.0465, foot 0.0145, normalised to the parts present. Anything else shares it in proportion to shape volume (area in 2D) times density; `MassModel.Volume` forces that for a person.
6. **Joints (3D).** Frame: persons use the engine's own (twist axis up, first swing axis lateral) for the spine, head, legs and feet; arms, hands and generic parts take the end of the bone axis that points up as the twist axis with the first swing axis lateral (forward cross twist), because a twist axis pointing down made the elbow joint unstable. Limits and drive gains by role (radians; twist ±, swing half angles about y and z; stiffness, damping, torque cap at 75 kg and 1.66 m, scaled by mass over 75 and height over 1.66): torso 0.4, 0.5, 0.5 (split: 0.3, 0.4, 0.4); head 0.6, 0.6, 0.6; upper arm 1.0, 1.6, 1.6; forearm 0.3, 2.4, 0.1; hand 0.3, 1.3, 0.4; thigh 0.6, 1.2, 1.0; shin 0.1, 2.3, 0.08; foot 0.3, 0.6, 0.4; generic 0.5, 0.8, 0.8. Ranges of motion after the AAOS and Kapandji tables (shoulder up to 180 degrees of flexion and abduction, elbow flexion to 145, knee flexion to 135, hip flexion to 120, ankle dorsiflexion 20 and plantar flexion 50), given as symmetric cones like the engine's own humanoid. `MinSwingHalfAngle` raises every swing half angle to at least a value. **2D:** relative revolute limits (counter-clockwise positive, a figure facing +x; mirrored and swapped for -x) torso -0.5..0.5, head -0.6..0.6, upper arm -1.2..3.0, forearm -0.05..2.5, hand -1..1, thigh -0.7..1.5, shin -2.4..0.05, foot -0.7..0.4; capsules take the angle of the upward end of their axis.
7. **Output checks.** Every part's mass, sizes and position are checked against the engine's limits before the definition is returned (OutOfRange, naming the part): the mass is a positive float, sizes in 0 to 65536 m, and the mass times the smallest half size squared at least 1e-14 and the mass at most 1e12 (the engine refuses a body whose mass or inertia falls outside 1e-18 to 1e30).
8. **Biped mapping.** `Biped` names the pelvis, torso, head (or -1), thighs, shins and feet when the skeleton is a person without a split torso (with one, the head hangs from the chest, which the controllers' wiring check refuses).

**Joint reach (3D).** A fitted part must keep its joint within 40 times its own reach (sphere radius, capsule half length plus radius, box half-diagonal) plus 0.05 m of the centre of the part and of its parent, the limit `AddRagdoll` enforces; a skeleton that cannot (bones far longer than the shapes fitted to them) gets a `GeometryToolException` naming the bone, not a definition the engine refuses.

## Scenes and hash matrix (part 2)

Scenes (own thresholds; no other engine builds these): `tet-drop-3d` (`tests/Common/TetDropScene.cs`: a character-like blob (edge 0.14 m), a torus and a sphere, each tetrahedralized and made a Rubber soft body, dropped 0.3 m onto a floor, 600 steps at 60 Hz). Thresholds: no NaN, nothing escaping, maxSpeed at most 25 m/s, at most 40 tetrahedra (of 10 000) ever inverted during the impact and at most 10 at the end, volume 0.5 to 1.5 of rest, no particle more than 5 cm under the floor. Measured: maxSpeed 12.1, inverted 22 (blob) at most and 3 at the end, volume ratio 0.955 to 1.002. A Jelly body dropped the same way loses 30 to 60 % of its volume and inverts hundreds of tetrahedra (a regular grid of the engine's own box does the same), so the scene uses Rubber. `tri-drop-2d`: an annulus, a square with two holes and a star meshed at 0.07 m and dropped 0.3 m: maxSpeed at most 25, at most 20 triangles (of about 700) inverted during the impact and 12 at the end, area 0.5 to 1.5 of rest, smallest angle at least 24.5 degrees. `autoragdoll-pile-3d`: eight limp figures of different height (1.5 to 1.92 m), mass (50 to 99 kg) and layout (plain, with hands, split torso; left limbs first or last) made from the generated skeleton, dropped from 2.3 to 3.5 m with seeded orientations onto a floor, 600 steps, swing cones opened to at least 3.0 rad (see Findings): maxSpeed at most 30 m/s, joint error (the gap between the two anchors of a joint) at most 0.3 m, nothing escaping, the 95th percentile final speed at most 0.5 m/s, no pelvis below the floor. `autoragdoll-stand-3d`: one figure made with the default limits stands under `Stand` with the generated `BipedBones`, pushed 12 N s forward at step 120, 600 steps: no fall (pelvis never below 60 % of its standing height), pelvis within 0.2 m of its standing height, joint error at most 0.15 m. Measured: height error 0.014 m, maxSpeed 1.05 m/s. `autoragdoll-pile-2d`: four side-view figures dropped from 1.0 to 2.5 m. Hash-matrix scenarios `tools/tetrahedralize`, `tools/triangulate2d`, `tools/autoragdoll`, `tools/tet-drop-3d`, `tools/tri-drop-2d`, `tools/autoragdoll-pile-3d`, `tools/autoragdoll-stand-3d`, `tools/autoragdoll-pile-2d`.

**Findings.** (1) Limp swing-twist ragdolls used to explode in tumbling drops; fixed in the engine (see `world3d.md`, swing limit), so the pile runs at the fitted limits and `AutoRagdollOptions.MinSwingHalfAngle` is only a styling floor. (2) The 2D standing controller is fragile: the engine's own 2D humanoid falls when its head is 1.5 kg heavier or its pelvis 3 cm narrower, so a 2D figure fitted from a skeleton (Winter's table puts 6.1 kg on the head against the engine's 5) does not stand under `Stand`; 2D figures settle (the pile scene) and the 3D controller stands the fitted 3D figure.
