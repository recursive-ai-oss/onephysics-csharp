# The 3D world

Normative, language-neutral description of `src/OnePhysics/Physics3D/`: what a 3D world does in a step and which choices a port must reproduce to get the same bits. It sits on the shared core (`architecture.md`), the 3D collision code (`collision3d.md`) and the broadphase (`broadphase.md`). It mirrors `world2d.md`; this page lists what is the same in one line and spells out what differs. All arithmetic is IEEE `+ − × ÷ sqrt` on float32 (positions and broadphase boxes are double); no fused operations. The solver kernel is scalar, one constraint at a time, so lane width cannot matter.

## Bodies, shapes, mass

* Same body model as 2D: the core position is the centre of mass, the origin is `com − R·lc`, rotation is a unit quaternion `(x, y, z, w)`, `StartAsleep` puts a body to sleep when created. `BodyDef.Rotation` is normalized on creation (zero, NaN and infinite quaternions are rejected with `ArgumentException` naming the field).
* Mass data per shape and unit density comes from `collision3d.md` (`InertiaF.Combine` for several shapes): `m = Σ density·volume`, `lc` the density-weighted centroid, inertia tensor about the centre of mass stored as six floats `xx xy xz yy yz zz`. No mass at all gives mass 1, identity inertia. `FixedRotation` gives zero inverse inertia. Static and kinematic bodies have zero inverse mass and inertia. Meshes and height fields are static only (`StaticOnlyShapeException`).
* The inverse inertia in world axes `R·I⁻¹·Rᵀ` is recomputed (float, rows in the order of the product) whenever a body's rotation changes, at the start of a step and after every substep rotation.
* Per shape the box of the shape's bounds in the body frame (`ShapeBounds3D` of its pose; the local bounding box for compounds, meshes and height fields) gives: `maxExtent` = the largest over shapes of `sqrt(dx² + dy² + dz²)` with `d` the largest per-axis distance from the centre of mass to that box; `minExtent` = the smallest over shapes of half the smallest box side. A body without shapes has `maxExtent` 0 and `minExtent` `float.MaxValue`. The gyroscopic flag is set when the inertia tensor is not isotropic (off-diagonals above `1e-6·Ixx` or diagonal differences above that) and rotation is not fixed.

## Shape geometry and recipes

A shape is created from a **recipe**: a byte string that fully determines its geometry. Recipes are what snapshots store; geometry is rebuilt from them on restore. Identical recipes share one geometry object (looked up by the byte string; slots are reused lowest first). Little-endian; floats are IEEE bit patterns.

| kind byte | layout after the kind |
|---|---|
| 1 sphere | radius |
| 2 box | half extents x y z, convex radius |
| 3 capsule | half height, radius |
| 4 tapered capsule | half height, top radius, bottom radius |
| 5 cylinder | half height, radius, convex radius |
| 6 hull | convex radius, int32 point count, points (x y z each) |
| 7 compound | int32 part count; per part: rotation x y z w, position x y z, then the part's convex recipe |
| 8 mesh | double flat-edge tolerance, int32 vertex count, vertices, int32 index count, indices |
| 9 height field | double flat-edge tolerance, int32 nx, int32 nz, scale x y z, bool has-holes, nx·nz heights, then one bool per cell if has-holes |

A shape also has a placement in the body (local rotation and translation, each shape slot), a material (friction, restitution, rolling resistance), a density, filter bits (category, mask, group) and the owner body.

## Pair table

One entry per pair of shapes `(a, b)`, `a < b`, whose broadphase boxes overlap, exactly as in 2D (same proxy margins, same acceptance rules, same update rule, parallel narrowphase over fixed chunks of 64 updated pairs, serial apply in ascending pair index). Differences:

* The tight box of a shape is the shape's float bounds relative to the centre of mass, added to the double position.
* **One pair may own several constraints**, one per sub-shape pair that has points: compound children, mesh triangles and height-field triangles are sub-shapes. A pair's constraints form a chain sorted ascending by `(subShapeA, subShapeB)`; the pair's results are applied in that order, so constraint creation order, and therefore the color of each constraint, is fixed. The core reports contact events per constraint; the world collapses them to one event per shape pair (see Events).
* The pose of B in A's frame is built from the shape frames relative to each centre of mass, with the double centre-of-mass difference converted to float once. The speculative distance is `SpeculativeDistance` (default 0.02).
* **Triangle soups** (meshes, height fields): the candidate triangles for the other shape's bounds (grown by the speculative distance) come from `collision3d.md` (`GetCandidates`), each triangle is a degenerate convex shape, and each gives its own manifold. Soup against soup never collides.
* **Internal-edge rule.** For a triangle manifold whose normal differs from the triangle's face normal (`dot < 0.99999` and `≥ −0.1`): if any manifold point's closest triangle feature is an inactive edge, or a vertex with both of its edges inactive, the manifold is dropped. This keeps a body from bumping over the seams of a flat mesh. Replacing the normal by the face normal was tried and gave double contacts, so the manifold is dropped instead.
* **Manifold payload** per constraint: normal (3), mixed friction, restitution, rolling resistance, the rolling impulse (3, kept from the previous manifold), per point (up to 4) the arms, separation, normal impulse, two tangent impulses and the max normal impulse; warm starting copies impulses from the old point with the same feature id (0 if none). Layout in `architecture.md`.
* **Materials.** Friction `sqrt(fA·fB)`, restitution `max`, rolling resistance `max`; `MaterialMixer` replaces it. A shape or `Material3D` that does not set a rolling resistance gets `Material3D.DefaultRollingResistance` = 0.003 (the cap on the rolling torque is that fraction of the summed normal impulse times 1 m): without it a sphere or capsule resting at a point or along a line has nothing to stop a spin, and piles of jointed ragdoll parts never come to rest. Character shapes set 0.

## Step

The core pipeline (`architecture.md`) with these stage bodies; `h` is the substep time. Defaults: 4 substeps, 1 solve iteration and 1 relax iteration per substep, contact hertz 30, damping ratio 10, push-out velocity 3 m/s, contact slop 0, restitution threshold 1 m/s, maximum linear speed 400 m/s, maximum rotation per substep π/4, speculative distance 0.02, broadphase margin 0.03 (round 23; was 0.1).

* **Prepare** (per active constraint): `hertz = min(ContactHertz, 0.25/h)`; soft coefficients as in 2D with `hertz`, and with `2·hertz` when either body has zero inverse mass. Tangent basis from the normal `n`: if `|nx| ≥ 0.57735` then `t = (ny, −nx, 0)` else `t = (0, nz, −ny)`; `t1 = t/|t|`, `t2 = n × t1`. Per point: effective masses `1/(mA + mB + (rA×d)·IA(rA×d) + (rB×d)·IB(rB×d))` for `d` in `n, t1, t2`; adjusted separation `s − (rB − rA)·n`; relative normal velocity; max normal impulse reset. Rolling mass is the inverse of `IA + IB` (3×3, zero when singular).
* **IntegrateVelocities** (dynamic bodies): `v += h·(gravityScale·g + invMass·force)`; `w += h·I⁻¹·torque`; **gyroscopic term** for bodies flagged as non-isotropic: one Newton step on `I(ω − ω₀) + h·ω × Iω` in the body frame with the exact Jacobian `I + h(ω̃I − (Iω)~)` (skipped when the determinant is not finite or tiny); then damping `1/(1 + h·damping)` on `v` and `w`; `|v|` clamped to the maximum speed, `|w|` to `MaxRotationPerSubstep/h`.
* **IntegratePositions**: `Δp += h·v`, `position += (double)(h·v)`; if `w ≠ 0`, `Δq` and the body rotation advance by `q += h/2·(w, 0)·q` and are renormalized with `sqrt` and a divide (if the norm is zero or not finite the old quaternion is kept); the world inverse inertia is refreshed.
* **Kernel** (per constraint; the arithmetic below is exactly one constraint's, evaluated lane by lane, see Performance). Warm start: `p = n·λn + t1·λt1 + t2·λt2` per point at the arms, then the rolling impulse as an angular impulse. Solve (with bias) and Relax (without), in this order: rolling resistance (clamped to `rolling · Σλn`, projected perpendicular to the normal), friction (both tangent impulses accumulated and clamped to the disc of radius `friction·λn` by scaling), then the normal row of each point:
  `s = ((Δp_B − Δp_A) + (Δq_B·rB − Δq_A·rA))·n + adjustedSeparation`; if `s > 0`: `bias = s/h`, mass scale 1, impulse scale 0; else when biased `bias = max(biasRate·min(s + slop, 0), −pushOut)` with the soft scales; relax uses bias 0 and scales 1, 0. `λ = −normalMass·massScale·(vn + bias) − impulseScale·λold`, accumulated and clamped at zero. Restitution (after all substeps, constraints whose restitution is 0 are skipped): for points whose pre-solve relative normal velocity is `≤ −threshold` and whose max normal impulse is positive, `λ = −normalMass·(vn + e·vrel)` accumulated and clamped.
* **Sweep order.** Within a color the order cannot matter. The sequence of colors alternates: in even substeps Solve visits overflow, then colors 0 to 23 ascending, and Relax visits colors 23 to 0 descending and the overflow last; odd substeps swap the two. Warm start and restitution are order-independent in effect and use the forward order. With one iteration per phase a fixed forward sweep leaves a systematic bias along the constraint order that makes a tall stack of boxes creep sideways (a 18-layer pyramid's top moved 0.1 m in 2 s); the alternating sweep removes it. 2D keeps the forward order.
* **FinalizeBodies**: forces and torques cleared; continuous collision.
* **BodyMotion**: `float.MaxValue` if a touching partner is a moving kinematic body, else `|v| + |w|·maxExtent`. Sleeping is the core's, with one 3D setting: an island that has lost constraints is split as soon as any of its bodies has rested for the sleep time (instead of only when all of them have), the longest-resting such island first, one split per step; the resting part then sleeps while the moving part stays awake.

## Continuous collision against static shapes

For each dynamic body after the substeps: `m` = centre-of-mass displacement over the step, `spin = 2·|xyz of q_start⁻¹·q_end|·maxExtent`. If `|m| > 0` and `|m| + spin > 0.5·minExtent` (and all finite), a body with no static shape whose box overlaps the box of the whole sweep (the start and end positions grown by `1.01·maxExtent + 1e-4`, which contains every shape at both poses) is skipped at once, which changes no result; otherwise every non-soup shape (compound children one by one) is linearly cast along `m` at its start orientation against the static shapes whose boxes overlap the swept box (the union of the start and end bounds). A hit counts only if: the cast does not start overlapping; the body would end the step at least `0.25·minExtent` deep along the hit normal (`(1 − t)·approach ≥ 0.25·minExtent`, `approach` = `−m·n_out`); and the hit is farther than twice the speculative distance along the motion (`t·|m| ≥ 2·SpeculativeDistance`): nearer surfaces already have speculative contacts, and clamping against them pins a body that rests on or rolls over a surface. The smallest accepted fraction `t` moves the body to `t' = t − 0.005/|m|` (not below 0) along `m`, with the rotation interpolated by a normalized lerp along the shorter arc; velocity is unchanged and `Δp` is reset to the clamped displacement. Triangle soups are not cast (they are thin walls only for convex movers that the discrete contacts already handle); a fast sphere against a thin box or a mesh is stopped.

## Snapshot section

Section id `0x33445747` ("GW3D"), version 1 (version 2 adds the flagged blocks below), written by the engine after the core's sections in this order (little-endian, all through the core's state writer):

1. `uint32` version; floats `ContactHertz`, `ContactDampingRatio`, `PushOutVelocity`, `RestitutionThreshold`, `MaxLinearSpeed`, `MaxRotationPerSubstep`; bool `ContinuousEnabled`; floats `ContactSlop`, `SpeculativeDistance`.
2. `int32` body slot count `nb`, then arrays over `nb`: local centre (3), mass, inertia (6), maxExtent, minExtent, linear damping, angular damping, gravity scale, force (3), torque (3), flags (bytes), first shape, last shape, `Δp` (3).
3. `int32` geometry count; per slot a bool (present) and, if present, `int32` length and the recipe bytes.
4. `int32` shape slot count; per slot a bool (live) and, if live: geometry index, local rotation (4), local translation (3), proxy id, friction, restitution, rolling resistance, density, category, mask, group, next shape of the body.
5. `int32` pair count; per pair a bool (live) and, if live: shape a, shape b, `int32` constraint count and the constraint ids in chain order. Then `int32` free pair count and the free list.

The reader checks every count against the bytes that remain before allocating, rejects non-finite values, non-unit rotations (squared length outside `[0.999, 1.001]`), invalid materials and references to missing geometry, rebuilds geometry from the recipes (a recipe that fails validation rejects the snapshot), cross-checks the section against the core (`IsConsistentWithCore`: proxies, shape chains, unit body rotations, constraint chains sorted by sub-shape pair), and restores atomically: any failure leaves the world as it was. The world hash covers the core state in `architecture.md`'s order plus nothing from this section that the core does not already hold (impulses live in the payloads); the origin is part of the hash.

## Origin

`ShiftOrigin(offset)` subtracts the offset from every body position and every broadphase box and adds it to the accumulated origin; components must be multiples of 2^-31 (`ArgumentException` otherwise). Positions passed in are checked against ±2^21 at creation. At the start of each `Step`, a moving body whose stretched shape boxes are not finite or reach beyond ±2^22, or whose position, velocity or rotation is not finite, raises `BodyOutOfBoundsException` naming it, before anything changes (the hash is unchanged); destroying or moving the body, or shifting the origin, lets stepping continue.

## Joints

Twelve joint types (`JointType3D`): fixed, point, distance, hinge, slider, cone, swing-twist, six-degrees-of-freedom, path, gear, rack-and-pinion, pulley. A joint is a constraint of the same storage, colouring and batching as a contact: it takes colours like a contact between its two bodies, is solved in the same colour sweeps, and a static or kinematic body takes none. Both ends are bodies of this world; at least one must be dynamic, and a joint between a body and itself is refused (`InvalidJointException`, as for any value a type does not allow: non-finite, out of range, a zero-length axis, a non-unit frame rotation).

**Frames.** Each end has a frame (a point and a unit quaternion in the body's origin frame). The frame's x axis is the main axis: hinge axis, slider axis, cone and twist axis, path tangent. The happy-path helpers take a world anchor and axis and build frames from the axis and a perpendicular chosen by a fixed rule (`PathSpline3D.Perpendicular`: the component pair of the unit axis swapped and negated, depending on whether |x| is at least 0.57735), so the same call gives the same frames on every platform.

**Rows.** Every type is a set of scalar rows `Cdot = LA·vA + AA·wA + LB·vB + AB·wB` (`JointRow`). Equality rows of up to three are solved as one 3×3 (or 2×2, 1×1) block with the exact inverse of the small system, so coupled rows (a point constraint, a hinge's two perpendicular rotations) do not fight. Limits are one-sided rows: speculative while the limit is not reached (the allowed velocity closes the gap in one substep), soft when violated; accumulated impulses stay at least zero. Motors drive `Cdot` to the target with the accumulated impulse clamped to ±force·h. User springs use the soft coefficients of frequency and damping ratio on their row. Geometry (anchors, frames, axes) is rebuilt at every solve call from the start-of-step pose plus the displacement accumulated since, as the contact solver does, so substeps and relax passes see current directions.

**Soft step.** Rigid rows use the soft-step coefficients (Catto, "Solver2D" and the soft-step talk): `WorldOptions3D.JointHertz` (default 60) and `JointDampingRatio` (default 2), with the frequency capped at a quarter of the substep rate. The relax pass uses no bias.

**Point block in lanes.** The three-row point constraint (the anchors coincide) is solved by `JointKernel3D`, written over `ILane<T>` like the contact kernel: the scalar twin, 128-bit and 256-bit widths (`Engine3D.KernelWidth`) give the same bits. It solves the point joint itself and the last three rows of fixed, hinge, cone and swing-twist joints; the other rows of those joints (rotation locks, limits, motors, springs) stay in the scalar solver, which runs first for each joint, so a joint's rows keep their order. A color batch (at most eight constraints, no dynamic body in common) gets one joint group beside its contact group (field-major rows as in the contact kernel; layout `J3D`): per lane the start-of-step anchors from the centres of mass (`rA0`, `rB0`), the centre-of-mass offset `d0`, both inverse masses and the accumulated impulse of slots 0 to 2; `PrepareBatch` fills it once per step. Each solve call gathers the two bodies' velocities, accumulated displacement, accumulated rotation and world inverse inertia, then: `rA = qA·rA0`, `rB = qB·rB0` (accumulated rotations), `C = d0 + (dpB - dpA) + rB - rA`; the block `K = (mA + mB)·1 + [rA]^T·IA·[rA] + [rB]^T·IB·[rB]` (`[r]` the cross-product matrix; the inertia term of column `i` and `j` is `(r × e_i)·I·(r × e_j)`) with entries `k00, k01, k02, k11, k12, k22`, cofactors `c00 = k11·k22 - k12², c01 = k02·k12 - k01·k22, c02 = k01·k12 - k02·k11, c11 = k00·k22 - k02², c12 = k01·k02 - k00·k12, c22 = k00·k11 - k01²`, `det = k00·c00 + k01·c01 + k02·c02`, `inv = 1 / det` (zero when `det` is zero: no dynamic mass, no impulse); `dv = (vB + wB × rB) - (vA + wA × rA)`, `rhs = Ms·dv + Kk·C`, `x0 = inv·(c00·rhs0 + c01·rhs1 + c02·rhs2)`, `x1 = inv·(c01·rhs0 + c11·rhs1 + c12·rhs2)`, `x2 = inv·(c02·rhs0 + c12·rhs1 + c22·rhs2)`, `step = -x - Isc·accumulated`, accumulated `+= step`, and `step` is applied as `vA -= mA·step`, `wA -= IA·(rA × step)`, `vB += mB·step`, `wB += IB·(rB × step)`; the warm-start pass applies the stored impulse the same way (no `K`); the solve pass uses the soft-step coefficients of the rigid rows (mass scale `Ms`, `Kk` = mass scale · bias rate, impulse scale `Isc`), the relax pass `Ms = 1`, `Kk = 0`, `Isc = 0`; the restitution pass does nothing. Impulses live in the group during the step and `StoreResults` writes them back to payload slots 0 to 2, where the reaction force and the next warm start read them. The overflow set (bodies shared inside a batch) runs the same kernel one lane at a time in list order. `K` is rebuilt at every solve call from the current anchors and inertias: a version that computed `K^-1` once per step (as the soft-step talk prepares effective masses) was 0.08 ms faster on joint-chains-3d but made ragdoll-pile-3d explode (bodies spinning several radians per step change `K` too much), so it was dropped.

**Rows per type** (payload slots in the constraint's float payload, 64 floats in 3D, so no type is limited by row count; the largest, six-dof, uses 30):

| Type | Rigid rows | Optional rows |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed | point block (slots 0–2), angular block (3–5) | |
| Point | point block (0–2) | |
| Distance | one row along the anchor-to-anchor direction (0) | spring (10), lower and upper length limits (12, 13) |
| Hinge | point block (0–2), the two rotations perpendicular to the axis (3–4) | spring (10), motor (11), angle limits (12, 13) |
| Slider | two translations perpendicular to the axis (0–1), rotation lock (3–5) | spring (10), motor (11), limits (12, 13) |
| Cone | point block (0–2) | cone limit (12): the angle between the two x axes at most the half angle, about their cross product |
| Swing-twist | point block (0–2) | pose drive (3–5), twist limits (12, 13), swing limit (14) |
| Six-dof | locked translations as a compact block (0–2), locked rotations as a compact block (3–5) | per axis i: motor (6+4i), spring (7+4i), upper (8+4i), lower (9+4i) |
| Path | two translations perpendicular to the tangent (0–1), orientation per `PathRotation` (3–5) | motor along the tangent (11), end clamps (12, 13) |
| Gear | one velocity row `wA·axisA + ratio·wB·axisB = 0` (0) | |
| Rack and pinion | one velocity row `wA·axisA = ratio·vB·axisB` (0) | |
| Pulley | one row on `lengthA + ratio·lengthB` (0) | range limits instead of the equality (12, 13) |

**Swing-twist limits.** With the relative rotation `q = FA⁻¹·FB` (sign chosen so `w ≥ 0`), twist is `2·atan2(q.x, q.w)` (onemath's atan2) and `swing = q · conj(twist)`. The twist row is the exact gradient of the twist angle with respect to the relative angular velocity, `(1, (wz + xy)/(x² + w²), (xz − wy)/(x² + w²))` in frame A (from `q' = ½ ω q`); a row about frame A's x axis alone stops enforcing the limit once the swing is large, because the twist axis is then the swung one. The swing limit is the ellipse `(y/sin(hy/2))² + (z/sin(hz/2))² ≤ 1` on the swing quaternion's components (hy, hz the half angles); the row is `−L·∇ρ`, the gradient of the ellipse function ρ scaled by the swing angle `L` at which the current direction meets the ellipse, again from `q' = ½ ω q`, and the slack is `(1 − ρ)·L`. Near `x² + w² < 1e-4` (swing close to a half turn) the twist row falls back to frame A's x axis and the swing row is skipped. Limit angles are at most π.

**Cone** uses the true angle between the axes (onemath's atan2 of the cross and dot products), and about the cross product (frame A's y axis when the axes are parallel or opposed).

**Six-dof.** Each axis is `Locked`, `Limited` or `Free`; the three translations are along frame A's axes, the rotations are the twist about x and two swing components (twice the swing quaternion's y and z) which are limited independently; this is exact for small swings and an approximation for large ones. A free axis with a spring or motor gets those rows only. `Joint.SetAxisMode` changes the mode and clears the joint's accumulated impulses.

**Gear and rack-and-pinion** constrain velocities only: there is no drift correction on the angle or position, so the relation holds in rate and may wander by integration error over a long run. They name no other joint.

**Path** follows a uniform Catmull-Rom spline through at least 2 points (refused otherwise, and for non-finite points), given in world coordinates at creation and stored relative to the path body; `Loop` closes it. The follower's attachment is projected onto the nearest point of the curve with a coarse scan of 8 samples per segment (the lowest parameter wins a tie) and a bisection inside the best interval; the frame there is built from the tangent and `Up`.

**Pulley.** The fixed points are doubles in world coordinates and move with `ShiftOrigin`. With `MinLength`/`MaxLength` the equality becomes a range.

**Breaking.** `BreakForce` and `BreakTorque` (default infinity) compare against the reaction of the last substep (its accumulated impulse divided by the substep length); a joint that exceeds either is flagged during the step, destroyed after it, and reports one `JointBreak` event with the joint's handle, both bodies and the value, in the event order of the shared core (after contact and sensor events, before sleep and wake).

**Collision between the connected bodies** is off unless `CollideConnected`.

**Islands.** A joint links its two bodies in the island graph like a contact, so a sleeping island holds its joints and a joint wakes both bodies. Destroying a body destroys its joints (their `Joint` handles go stale; a stale handle throws `StaleHandleException` and a default or foreign one `InvalidHandleException`, each naming it).

**Snapshot.** The 3D section becomes version 2 when any joint or any non-default service state exists: after the version-1 body data comes a block flags word (bit 0 = joints, bit 1 = services; never 0), then the flagged blocks in that order. The joint block is `JointHertz` and `JointDampingRatio`, the joint slot count, and per slot a bool (live) and, if live, the joint record (type, flags, body-local frames, limits, springs, motors, break thresholds, the six-dof axis arrays, path points as a count and a list, pulley fixed points as doubles, the constraint payload with accumulated impulses). The reader accepts versions 1 and 2, checks every count against the remaining bytes, rejects non-finite values and values `JointData3D.Validate` refuses, and rebinds joints to constraints after the contacts section is read; a version-1 restore removes all joints. A world with neither joints nor service state writes exactly the version-1 bytes. The services block is described under Snapshot version 2: services. The world hash is the core's: the constraint payloads hold the accumulated joint impulses; joint definitions are not hashed separately.

**Known limits.** A swing-twist joint has a pose drive (see Ragdolls) but no velocity motor. Reaction force and torque are those of the last step in which the joint was solved and are not state: they are not in the snapshot.

## Layers and filters

The decision order is the 2D one (`world2d.md`, Layers and filters): sensor-sensor never collides; the 32 x 32 layer matrix (`SetLayerCollision`, `IgnoreLayerCollision`, default all ones, setting a pair sets both rows); the group index; category and mask; then the optional `WorldOptions3D.PairFilter` delegate and the optional struct `IContactFilter` (`SetContactFilter`), each called serially with the shapes in slot order. Re-filtering uses the same filter-dirty flag, with the same effects: pairs no longer allowed lose every constraint of their chain (contact end events, islands woken), and every proxy is touched so newly allowed pairs enter in sorted order. `Body.Layer` sets the layer of all the body's shapes. Delegates and filters are not saved.

## Sensors

**Sensor overlaps are marks, not constraints.** The overlap above is a mark on the pair table entry (`PairCons[p] = -2`, `SensorOverlap`); no constraint slot is taken, so a sensor never changes which slot, color or order a contact gets, and adding, moving or removing a sensor of any body type leaves every solid body bit-identical. Pairs that involve a sensor shape take their pair slots from their own free stack (the sensor free list); contact pairs use the contact free list, which keeps the slots of contact pairs the same relative to each other with or without sensors. Enter and exit events come from the apply stage and from pair removal (`EmitSensorEvent`): an overlap that ends because a pair is dropped, a shape or a body is destroyed, or the boxes stop overlapping reports exit exactly once. In the snapshot an overlapping sensor pair is a pair chain of length 1 holding -2, and the free-pair list is the contact free slots followed by the sensor free slots as bitwise complements; the pair's constraint id is -2 while it overlaps.

A shape created with `ShapeDef.IsSensor` contributes no mass or inertia, produces no contact response and is skipped by continuous collision. A pair with a sensor owns **one** sensor constraint (a chain of length 1, never solved, colored or linked into islands) while any manifold point of any of its sub-shape pairs has separation at most 0; it is added when the overlap begins (sensor enter) and removed when it ends, when the boxes stop overlapping, or when a shape or body is destroyed (sensor exit, delivered at the end of the next step). A sensor shape on a static or kinematic body also pairs with kinematic bodies and characters (and a kinematic sensor with static bodies); only a static-static pair never exists. Events for such pairs sort like any other (kind, then body slots). A sensor pair is evaluated when either body is awake and moving, and once in the step in which it is first found, so a sleeping body already inside a sensor gets its enter event. Sleeping and waking never end an overlap.

## Events

Kinds, their order and `ContactHit` are as in 2D. Differences in 3D:

* **One event per shape pair.** The core's contact events are per sub-shape constraint; the world builds the public list from them at the end of every step and restore. A pair gets a begin event if it had no contact constraint at the start of the step and has one now, an end event for the reverse, at most one persist, at most one hit (the largest approach speed over its constraints). A body over 40 mesh triangles produces one begin, not 40. The sub-shape ids of the reported event are those of the first sub-shape constraint of the chain. Everything is computed from chain lengths, so the list does not depend on the thread count.
* `Collision` carries 0 to 4 points (`PointCount`, `GetPoint`): the points of the pair's first sub-shape contact, world position of the anchor on A's centre of mass plus the arm, with `Separation`, the normal from A to B and `NormalImpulse` / `TangentImpulse` equal to the last substep's accumulated impulse times the number of substeps. `ApproachSpeed` is the hit speed. A contact that has ended has no points.
* Handlers are `OnCollisionEnter/Stay/Exit/Hit`, `OnTriggerEnter/Exit`, `OnSleep`, `OnWake`, fired after the step first on body A then on body B. Polled: `Events`, `GetEvents(kind)`, `GetCollision(event)`, `GetTrigger(event)`. Handlers belong to the handle and are muted inside `SuppressCallbacks()`; see `docs/guide/api-names.md`.

## Queries

The 2D rules (`world2d.md`, Queries) apply with 3D shapes: `Raycast`, `ShapeCast`, `SphereCast`, `BoxCast`, `CapsuleCast`, `OverlapShape`, `OverlapPoint`, `OverlapSphere`, `OverlapBox`, `OverlapCapsule` and `ClosestPoint`, each as a single (closest), `Any`, and `All` (caller span or new array) form, and `PhysicsQuery3D` for batches. Positions are double within +-2^22; directions, lengths and sizes are float; a ray or cast length of infinity means `MaxQueryDistance` (2^23). Rays and casts sort by distance (ties by shape slot), overlaps by shape slot, `OverlapShape` (single) returns the shape whose surface is nearest the query shape's origin, closest point sorts by distance. `Fraction` is the fraction of the cast length; `Distance` the distance travelled (overlap and closest point: from the query centre to the surface, 0 inside). `SubShapeId` is the compound child or mesh or height-field triangle (the id contact events use), 0 for a convex shape, -1 for overlaps. A ray starting inside a convex shape hits it at distance 0 with a zero normal; a cast starting overlapping reports a zero normal. Casts take a convex query shape (compound query shapes are not supported). A segment on a compound is clipped to half the compound's coordinate range before the compound sees it (the compound refuses longer reach), and fractions are mapped back. The candidate set comes from one broadphase tree per body type, padded by the largest gap between a shape's tight and stored box; the narrow phase works in each shape's own frame after one double subtraction. Candidates are tried cheapest first without changing any result. A convex candidate whose padded local box (kept with its geometry) cannot be reached by the ray, the sweep (the mover's box swept as a segment against the shape's box grown by the mover's half sizes) or the query shape is dropped before the exact test, with a slack of `1e-3 + scale / 4096` so float error never drops a touching shape. A sphere overlapping a sphere, box, capsule or hull is decided in closed form (distance from the centre to the core, less the radii; the hull by its face planes: beyond one plane by more than the radii it is clear, inside every plane it overlaps) whenever the answer is clear by the same slack, and goes to GJK when it is within the slack; the set of overlapped shapes is the one GJK alone gives. Rays and casts still take their fractions, normals and points from GJK, so those are unchanged. Per-thread scratch makes queries with caller buffers allocation-free in steady state; the triangle-soup cast reuses one thread-static scratch triangle. Errors are as in 2D (`ArgumentOutOfRangeException` / `ArgumentException` naming the argument, nothing changed). Batches run in fixed chunks of 64 queries on the world's executor and do not depend on the thread count.

## Debug geometry

`GetDebugGeometry(content, buffer)` as in 2D, with these 3D entries (flags: Shapes 1, Bounds 2, BroadphaseBounds 4, Contacts 8, Bodies 16, Joints 32, Characters 64, MeshTriangles 128; `All` is 255). Shapes by slot, a compound as one entry per child. Sphere: kind Circle, one point (centre) and the radius. Box: 8 corner points (bit 0 = +x, bit 1 = +y, bit 2 = +z of the core, rounding radius separate) and 12 triangles in `Indices` (`FirstIndex`, `IndexCount`). Capsule: the two axis ends and the radius. Cylinder and tapered capsule: bottom and top axis centres, `Radius` (top for the tapered capsule) and `BottomRadius`. Hull: its vertices and the faces fanned from each face's first vertex into triangles. Mesh and height field: the box corners, and with `MeshTriangles` the vertices and triangles. A joint entry adds the joint type, the reference axis in world coordinates, the limits and the cone half angle where the type has them. Points are `origin + (double)(R * v)`.

## Snapshot version 2: services

Block flag bit 1. Layout: the 32 layer-matrix rows (`uint32` each), `float32` `HitSpeedThreshold` (finite, at least 0), a bool filter-dirty flag, then for every shape slot in slot order a `uint8` shape flags (bit 0 = sensor) and a `uint8` layer (below 32); both are 0 on a dead slot, and the reader rejects any other value. ## Characters

The 3D character is the 2D character mover (`world2d.md`, "Character mover") with an up vector; everything not restated here is the same rule with 3D vectors. A character is an upright capsule (axis along `up`, centred on the body origin, a fixed rotation taking +y to `up`) on a **virtual** kinematic body or on a **rigid** dynamic body. `CharacterDef` adds to the 2D definition `Up` (normalized at creation, default +y), `Kind` (Virtual, Rigid), `Mass` (kg, default 70: the rigid body's density and the weight a virtual character presses on dynamic ground) and `MaxPushForce` (newtons, default 500, within [0, 1e9]). The limits are the 2D ones; positions stay within ±2^21. `Up`, `Kind` and the rigid preset belong to 3D only; `Mass`, `MaxPushForce` and the steep-slope ground state are still to be added to 2D.

**Planes.** One plane per direction around the capsule, from the manifold of the capsule against each gathered shape out to `2·skin`: a mesh or height field gives one plane per triangle, a compound one per child. Triangles go through the same internal-edge ghost filter as contacts, so a character crossing the seam of a mesh (a voxel chunk, neighbouring height-field cells) meets the face normal rather than the edge normal and does not snag. Planes whose normals have a dot product above 0.99995 and the same push limit are merged, keeping the smallest separation. A walkable plane (`n·up ≥ cos(slopeLimit)`) keeps no skin; the others hold `skin`. Push limits: `0.5·radius` per move for static and kinematic shapes, 0 for dynamic bodies and other characters, which are never ground. Candidates are gathered in ascending shape slot, then sub-shape id.

**Plane solver** (`Mover3D.SolvePlanes`): Dykstra's projection onto the half-spaces in plane order, as in 2D (at most 50 sweeps, tolerance 1e-5); an empty feasible set returns zero.

**Move** as in 2D with `up` for y: the displacement splits into a part along `up` and a horizontal part; when grounded the ground body's velocity at the character's position is added; the horizontal slide runs first (at most five rounds, each a shape cast of the capsule). The step-up probe reaches `radius + skin − 0.5·radius·sin(slopeLimit)` ahead and a step must gain at least `skin/2` to count; the second attempt moves `radius + skin` forward; the drop must land on walkable ground at most `stepOffset + 1e-3` above the start. Downward movement is absorbed when already resting on walkable ground; otherwise, when grounded last move and not moving up, it casts down by `max(stepOffset, movement)` and snaps to walkable ground; a steep surface within reach is slid along. Ground is a walkable plane with gap at most `2·skin`; **steep** ground (`0 < n·up < cos(slopeLimit)`, gap + skin at most `2·skin`) reports `GroundState.OnSteepGround` and is never stood on. `GroundState` is OnGround, OnSteepGround or InAir.

**Pushing.** A virtual character's body makes no solid contacts (the pair rule skips virtual character bodies; sensors still see them), so pushing is by impulse only: for every dynamic body hit through a non-walkable plane the wanted speed along the push direction is `displacement·(−n)/dt`; the impulse is `mass·(want − have)·pushStrength` at the contact point, limited to `MaxPushForce·dt`. A virtual character standing on an awake dynamic body presses `Mass·gravity·dt` of impulse down at its position.

**Rigid character.** A dynamic capsule with a friction-0 shape, fixed rotation, never sleeping. `Move` sets its velocity: the horizontal part of the displacement divided by `dt` (tangent to walkable ground when grounded, so it follows ramps) and a positive part along `up` as a jump velocity; its gravity scale is 0 while it stands on walkable non-dynamic ground. Ground detection and the slope limit are the virtual character's; steps are not climbed. Rigid characters take part in the solver and are obstacles for virtual ones; virtual characters pass through them.

**Characters and each other, layers.** As in 2D: another character is an obstacle for the cast and the planes, never ground and never pushed out of; give characters a layer that ignores itself to let them pass.

**State.** Per character: body slot and generation, definition, ground state, ground normal, ground body, realized velocity. All of it is in the snapshot and the hash. Destroying the body destroys the character.

**Characters block** (bit 4 of the section's block-flags word; written last, after joints and services): the handle table of characters, then per live character in slot order: body index, body generation (i32), radius, height, slope limit, step offset, skin (f32), layer mask (u32), push strength (f32), layer (i32), category, mask (u32), up x, y, z (f32), kind (u8), mass, max push force (f32), ground state (u8), ground normal x, y, z (f32), ground body index, generation (i32; −1 and 0 when none), velocity x, y, z (f32). Restore validates the definition like a new one, requires every character's body to be live with the named generation, and finite state. A world without characters writes no block and is byte-identical to before.

**Debug geometry.** Characters content (flag 64): per character the body, capsule centre, radius, height, grounded flag, unit ground normal and ground body, as in 2D.

## Vehicles

Wheeled vehicles (`world.AddVehicle(chassis, VehicleDef)`, presets `VehicleDef.Car`, `Truck`, `Kart`). A vehicle is a dynamic body plus a definition and a state record; it has no shape of its own, and its wheels are not bodies.

**Ordering.** Vehicles run as one serial stage at the start of `Step`, before the core step, in vehicle slot order and, within a vehicle, wheel order. Each applies its impulses to the chassis (and to a dynamic ground body) directly, so the solver then sees the changed velocities. It is not a coloured constraint batch: a fixed slot order makes it independent of thread count and vector width, and at about a hundred vehicles the stage is cheap next to the solve. If a scene shows that vehicles need to be solved together with contacts, that is a later change. Impulses are per step, not per substep.

**Per wheel, in this order.**
1. Drivetrain (below) gives the wheel's drive torque from the speeds at the end of the last step.
2. Wheel cast: the wheel's collision shape (a cylinder rounded by min(0.15 radius, half width), or a sphere when `CastShape` is `Sphere`) is swept with the public shape cast from the suspension top along the suspension direction for `SuspensionMaxLength` (plus 1e-4), the chassis excluded; the axle (steered by `Drive`'s steer times `MaxSteerAngle` about the steer axis) sets the cylinder's axis. A cast that starts overlapping has no normal and uses the opposite of the suspension direction. Contact needs the normal to be within about 87 degrees of the suspension line (cosine at least 0.05). The suspension length is the hit distance clamped to min..max. The ground body's cached world inertia is refreshed (it is not saved).
3. Suspension along the contact normal, pushing only. Compression is max length - length + preload. Spring impulse h k x, capped at m_eff x / h (what restores the wheel in one step), with k = sprung mass (2 pi f)^2 and sprung mass defaulting to chassis mass / wheel count; plus the anti-roll term bar stiffness times the compression difference of the two wheels; the damper is implicit in the effective mass (Catto, soft constraints, GDC 2011) and reads the closing speed corrected by gravity times h (S-1)/(2S) for S substeps, since the velocity at the start of a step carries the sawtooth of substepped gravity. Past the minimum length a bump stop adds an impulse that removes the closing speed and 20 % of the overshoot per step.
4. Tyre: wheel speed after drive torque, brakes (torque capped at what stops the wheel within the step) and angular damping; slip ratio (wheel speed times radius minus ground speed along the wheel's forward line) over max(|ground speed|, `LowSpeed`) and slip angle tangent (lateral speed over the same reference); Pacejka's magic formula sin(C atan(Bx - E (Bx - atan Bx))) for each direction; a friction ellipse (the pair of normalised forces is scaled to length 1); grip mu = sqrt(tyre friction x ground shape friction) times the normal load; each force limited so it cannot reverse the slip velocity it acts on within the step. A wheel held by its brake is locked: the tyre force then sees the whole chassis. Forces act at the contact point, along the normal, the wheel's forward line projected on the ground, and their cross product.

**Drivetrain.** Engine torque curve (samples over min..max rpm, default shape in `EngineDef`), engine braking, a clutch whose capacity ramps from idle to 1.5 times idle speed (no creep, no stall) with `ClutchStrength`, an automatic or manual gearbox (shift up above `ShiftUpRpm`, down below `ShiftDownRpm`, clutch open for `SwitchTime`; reverse when the driver asks for reverse while nearly stopped, braking first otherwise), and differentials: open, or limited slip as a torque-bias ratio that ramps in over 0.5 rad/s of speed difference. A wheel belongs to at most one differential; `EngineTorqueRatio` splits the engine between differentials.

**Defaults and limits.** Presets and field defaults are in the XML docs of `VehicleDef`, `WheelDef`, `TyreDef`, `EngineDef`, `TransmissionDef`, `DifferentialDef` and `AntiRollBarDef`; definitions are copied at creation and validated (1 to 32 wheels, at most 16 differentials and anti-roll bars, radius, width at most 100, positions within 10000, unit axes, spring at most 25 Hz) with an `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` naming the field. A body carries at most one vehicle (`PhysicsException` otherwise). Destroying the chassis destroys its vehicle.

**Snapshot.** Block flag 8, written last, after the character block when both exist. Handle table of vehicles; per live vehicle: chassis body index and generation (i32 each), the definition (wheel count; per wheel position, suspension, steer, axle and forward vectors, radius, width, min and max length, frequency, damping ratio, preload, sprung mass, inertia, angular damping, steer limit, brake and handbrake torques (f32 each), cast shape (u8), tyre curve parameters (8 x f32); engine scalars; torque curve length and samples; gearbox flags, reverse ratio, switch time, shift rpms, gear count and ratios; differentials; anti-roll bars), the driver inputs, engine speed, gear, shift timer, and per wheel the sprung mass, wheel speed, spin angle, steer angle, suspension length, spring force, slip ratio and angle, tyre forces, contact normal (f32 each), contact point (3 x f64), contact flag (u8), ground body index and generation (i32 each). Restore validates every field (finite, ranges, body and ground references live, the definition by `Validate`) and leaves the world unchanged on failure. The vehicle state is in the world hash. Debug geometry: `DebugContent.Vehicles` (256; `All` is 511) adds one wheel record per wheel (chassis, wheel index, suspension top, centre, radius, width, axle, contact flag, point and normal).

### Controller kinds: tracked vehicles and motorcycles

A vehicle has a kind (`VehicleKind`: Wheeled, Tracked, Motorcycle). All kinds share the wheel machinery above (cast, suspension, tyre, slot order, the same snapshot block); the kind chooses the drivetrain stage that fills the per-wheel drive torque and brake input, and two small hooks. `Vehicle.SetInput(throttle, steer, brake)` drives any kind. Wheeled vehicles take exactly the old code path.

**Tracked** (`AddVehicle(chassis, TrackedVehicleDef)`, `AddTank`; preset `TrackedVehicleDef.Tank`). Road wheels are the wheels: the left track's first, then the right's (`LeftTrackWheelCount`). Every road wheel uses the track's `Grip` as its tyre.
1. Commands: left = throttle + steering, right = throttle - steering, both divided by max(1, |left|, |right|), times (1 - brake). Positive steering turns towards +X (positive about +Y). A track whose command is exactly 0 is held by its brake (input 1), as a tank's steering brakes hold it on a slope; the tyre curve needs a little slip to carry the load, so a held tank creeps a few cm/s on a 30 % grade.
2. Drive: the belt's inertia is the sum over its wheels of inertia / radius^2 (seen along the belt, so wheel `Inertia` includes the belt, sprocket and drivetrain share); the sprocket applies the force that reaches the commanded belt speed (command times `MaxTrackSpeed`) within one step, capped at `MaxDriveTorque / SprocketRadius`, split over the wheels in proportion to their inertia.
3. Wheels are visited left 0, right 0, left 1, right 1 ... (extra wheels of the longer track last), because the impulses are applied one after the other and a side-by-side order would favour a track. Lateral tyre force is multiplied by `LateralFriction` (default 0.5) after the friction ellipse: skid steering needs sideways grip below longitudinal grip (Wong, Theory of Ground Vehicles, ch. 5).
4. After the tyres, the belt is rigid: all wheels of a track take the inertia-weighted mean of their surface speeds, limited to +-`MaxTrackSpeed`. The brake torque of a track is spread over its wheels in proportion to radius.

**Motorcycle** (`AddVehicle(chassis, MotorcycleDef)`, `AddMotorcycle`; preset `MotorcycleDef.Road`). Wheel 0 is the front (steered), wheel 1 the rear (driven by the usual engine, clutch and gearbox through one differential with `FinalDriveRatio`).
1. Steering limit: the front wheel turns by steering input times a limit that is `MaxSteerAngle` up to `SteerFadeStartSpeed`, `HighSpeedSteerAngle` from `SteerFadeEndSpeed` and linear between, by the speed along the front wheel's forward direction.
2. Balance assist, run before the wheels are cast (so it reads the lean rate before the tyres act): lean = atan2(up . right, up . worldUp) of the chassis' +Y about its heading (the horizontal part of the front wheel's forward direction), positive towards +X; wanted lean = atan(v^2 tan(delta) / (g L)) with v the forward speed (0 when not moving forward), delta the commanded steering angle, L the wheelbase along the forward direction, clamped to +-`MaxLeanAngle` (single-track model, Cossalter, Motorcycle Dynamics, ch. 3); the wanted lean moves towards it at most 1.5 rad/s (`LeanTarget`). A spring-damper with an integral term, in the lean rate: accel = k e + k_i integral(e) - c rate, with k = (2 pi f)^2, c = 2 zeta 2 pi f, k_i = 0.5 (2 pi f) k, the spring-damper implicit (Catto 2011) and the integral explicit and clamped, the change of lean rate in one step capped at step x `MaxLeanTorque` / I (I below). The assist turns the whole bike about the line through its contact patches: it changes the angular velocity about the heading by -d(rate) and the linear velocity by d(omega) x (up x h), so the patches do not slide; h is the height of the centre of mass above the patch line (from the wheel geometry) and I = roll inertia about the centre of mass + m h^2. It does nothing past `FallAngle` (the integral is cleared) or when `MaxLeanTorque` is 0.
3. Wheels as above. In addition, for Tracked and Motorcycle only, a braked wheel's tyre force is capped at brake torque / radius: a tyre force above that would spin the wheel forward. (Cars keep the old rule.)

**Defaults and limits.** Fields and presets are in the XML docs of `TrackedVehicleDef` and `MotorcycleDef`; a tracked vehicle has 1 to 16 road wheels a side, a motorcycle exactly two wheels; `ChassisSize` and `ChassisMass` are used only by `AddTank` and `AddMotorcycle`, which place the hull on the ground point `at` facing +Z. Invalid values raise `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` naming the field.

**Snapshot.** Block flag 64, after the vehicles block (flag 8, whose definition is the wheeled one the kind runs on) and before ragdolls; present only when a live vehicle is not wheeled, so worlds without such vehicles keep their bytes. Count (i32); per non-wheeled vehicle in slot order: slot (i32), kind (u8), then for Tracked sprocket radius, max track speed, max drive torque, max brake torque, lateral friction (f32 each) and the left wheel count (i32); for Motorcycle high-speed steer angle, fade start speed, fade end speed, max lean, fall angle, lean frequency, lean damping, max lean torque, lean, wanted lean and the lean integral (f32 each; the front steer angle and final drive ratio are in the wheeled definition). Restore rebuilds the kind's definition from the wheeled one plus these values and validates it. Lean and the integral are in the world hash.

Not yet: wheel contact begin and end events, vehicle events, wheel and track state as public raw-tier arrays, a user-supplied controller.

## Ragdolls

A ragdoll (`world.AddRagdoll(RagdollDef, at, rotation, pose)`, preset `RagdollDef.Humanoid`, `RagdollDef.CreateHumanoid(height, mass)`) is a set of dynamic bodies, one per part, joined by swing-twist joints, plus a record of the definition and of the drive. 3D only: there is no 2D ragdoll. It adds no stage to the step: the bodies and joints are ordinary and the drive is a row set of the joint solver.

**Definition.** Parts in an array, parents first: `Parent` is -1 for the root (exactly one) and otherwise an index below the part's own, so a skeleton cannot contain a cycle (a parent at or after the part is refused). At most 64 parts. Each part has one shape (capsule along the body's y axis, sphere or box), a mass (the density is mass over the shape's volume: sphere 4/3 pi r^3, capsule pi r^2 (2h) + 4/3 pi r^3, box 8abc), a position and orientation of its body in ragdoll space in the bind pose, a joint position and joint orientation in ragdoll space (the joint frame's x axis is the twist axis, y and z the swing axes), twist limits, swing half angles, and the drive gains: stiffness (N m/rad), damping (N m s/rad) and torque limit (N m) at full strength. Positions are within 2^20, all numbers finite, masses and sizes positive, limits as for swing-twist joints; a bad value raises `InvalidRagdollException` (skeleton shape) or `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` (a number), naming the part and the field, before anything is created.

**Building.** The ragdoll's space is placed in the world by `at` (a double position, within +-2^21) and `rotation`: part i's body goes to `at + rotation * Position_i` with orientation `rotation * Rotation_i` (normalized), at rest and at zero velocity. For a part with parent p the joint's frame A is `(conj(qbp) * (J - Pp), conj(qbp) * R)` and its frame B `(conj(qbi) * (J - Pi), conj(qbi) * R)` in the bodies' bind orientations qb with `R` the normalized joint orientation and `J` the joint position, so in the bind pose the two frames coincide. Order: bodies, shapes and joints are created part by part in index order, so their slots follow the part order. `SelfCollision` is `None` (every shape of the ragdoll gets group -(slot + 1), which never collides within the group), `NonAdjacent` (default: the joints are created with `CollideConnected` false, so only joined pairs are filtered) or `All` (`CollideConnected` true). A failure while building destroys what was made.

**Pose.** A pose is one rotation per part, `D_i`: part i turned relative to its parent in the bind pose's axes (so the identity is the bind pose, and a rotation about a bind-pose axis moves the limb about that axis whatever the parent is doing). The drive's target for a joint is the rotation of frame B in frame A, `rel = conj(R) * D_i * R`. The root's entry orients the whole figure in `SetPose` and `MoveToPose` and is ignored by the drive.

**Pose drive** (swing-twist joints with the drive flag, joint flag bit 5; the record then ends with the target quaternion, four floats, after the path points; the stiffness, damping and torque limit are the joint's `Length`, `Ratio` and `MaxMotor` fields, and slots 3 to 5 of the payload hold the accumulated angular impulse along frame A's axes). The solver adds three angular rows along frame A's axes, solved as one 3x3 block, before the limits, once per substep in the biased (solve) pass; the warm-start pass re-applies the stored impulse and the relax pass does nothing. With substep length `h`, stiffness `k` and damping `c`: error `e = 2 * vec(qe)` where `qe = rel * conj(target)` with its sign chosen so that `w >= 0` (the short way round); softness `gamma = 1 / (h (c + h k))`, bias rate `beta = k / (c + h k)`; the block solves `(K + gamma I) x = Cdot + beta e + gamma a` with `K` the 3x3 angular effective inverse mass of the rows (as in the contact and joint kernels: `K_ij = row_i . (IA + IB) row_j`), `a` the accumulated impulse, and the new accumulated impulse is `a - x`. This is Catto's soft constraint with an implicit spring-damper (Soft Constraints, GDC 2011): a torque stiffness and damping instead of the frequency form of the user springs, so a joint holds the same load whatever the masses around it. The accumulated impulse vector is then scaled down to length `h * maxTorque` when longer. Nothing is applied when `c + h k` is 0. The reaction torque readout and break check include the drive impulse in quadrature with the limit impulses.

**The Ragdoll API.** `DriveToPose(pose, strength)` sets every joint's drive: target `conj(R) * normalize(D_i) * R`, stiffness, damping and torque limit of the part times `strength` (0 to 1; 0 removes the drives). A joint whose drive record is unchanged is left alone (it does not wake the bodies), so calling it every frame with a still pose lets the figure sleep. `StopDriving` removes the drives. `SetPose(pose, at, rotation)` teleports: root orientation `rotation * normalize(D_0)`, root position `at + rotation * P_0`; with `delta_0 = rotation * D_0` and `delta_i = delta_p * D_i`, part i's orientation is `delta_i * qb_i` and its position is `pos_p + delta_p (J_i - P_p) - delta_i (J_i - P_i)`, so each joint anchor coincides; velocities are set to zero. `MoveToPose(pose, at, rotation, dt)` computes the same targets and sets each body's linear velocity to (target - position)/dt and its angular velocity to the axis-angle of `target * conj(current)` (short way round, angle by onemath's atan2) over dt; the bodies stay dynamic. `GetPoseError(pose)` is the largest over live joints of `2 atan2(|vec|, |w|)` of `rel * conj(target)`. The default `Ragdoll` handle and a removed one fail with `InvalidHandleException` and `StaleHandleException`; destroying one part body on its own ends the ragdoll record (the other bodies and joints stay).

**Solver settings.** The drive is a stiff soft constraint inside a long chain of rigid and soft rows, and a tall stack of them needs the solver to converge: with 4 substeps and 1 velocity iteration (the defaults) a standing humanoid creeps until it topples after about two seconds, with 4 iterations or with 8 substeps it holds (worst joint error 0.004 and 0.065 rad over 600 steps). `Ragdoll.ApplyRecommendedOptions(options)` raises the velocity iterations to at least 4.

**Humanoid preset.** Thirteen parts (pelvis, torso, head, two arms of two parts, two legs of thigh, shin and foot), 1.8 m, 75 kg, feet on y = 0, facing +z, joint twist axes along +y; the full table is in `RagdollDef.CreateHumanoid`. Gains and limits scale with `(mass / 75) * (height / 1.8)`; geometry scales with height. The knee and elbow limits allow flexion both ways (the swing limit is symmetric).

**Snapshot.** Block flag 16, written last. Handle table of ragdolls; per live ragdoll: part count (i32), self-collision (u8), angular damping (f32), category and mask bits (u32 each); per part: parent (i32), shape kind (u8), shape size (3 f32), position (3 f32), rotation (4 f32), mass, friction (f32), joint position (3 f32), joint rotation (4 f32), lower and upper twist, swing y and z (4 f32), drive stiffness, damping, torque limit (3 f32), body slot and generation, joint slot and generation (4 i32); then powered (bool) and strength (f32). A drive's target, gains and impulses are in the joint records and payloads. Restore refuses parts whose bodies are missing, shared between ragdolls or not dynamic, definitions that fail validation, and strengths outside 0..1. The hash covers it by covering the snapshot bytes.

## Buoyancy and drag

Fluids are 3D-only. A fluid (`world.AddWater(surfaceHeight, density, flow, damping)`, or `world.AddFluid(FluidDef)` in the middle tier, `world.ApplyBuoyancy(body, FluidDef, dt)` in the raw tier for a host with its own water) fills the half-space below a plane `dot(n, x) = offset` (`n` is stored as a unit vector: the definition's normal and offset are divided by the normal's length once, in double). Optionally (`LimitToRegion`) it acts only on bodies whose bounding sphere about the centre of mass touches a world-space box; the surface plane still decides what is submerged. `Body.BuoyancyFactor` (default 1, 0 to 10^6) scales a body's lift.

**Pre-solve stage.** Immediately before the core step (after vehicles), `StepFluids` visits bodies in ascending slot order and, for each awake dynamic body, fluids in ascending slot order, shapes in creation order and compound children in index order. Everything is float32 with IEEE-exact operations. A body is skipped when its centre of mass is higher above the surface than `MaxExtent`. Sleeping bodies are skipped (they rest on a bed or in equilibrium); adding, changing or removing a fluid wakes every dynamic body. Sensors, triangle meshes and height fields are skipped. Per submerged piece (a convex shape or a compound child) with submerged volume `V` and centroid `c` (relative to the centre of mass): `F += density * factor * V * (-gravity)` and `torque += c x (that force)`. The sums are added to the body's force and torque arrays, which the step applies in every substep and clears.

**Drag.** With `vr` the centre-of-mass velocity minus the flow, `A` the sum over pieces of the submerged silhouette area along `vr`, `Vsub` the submerged volume and `w` the angular velocity: linear force `-vr/|vr| * (0.5 * density * Cd * A * |vr|^2 + damping * density * Vsub * |vr|)` through the centre of mass; torque `-w/|w| * (0.5 * density * Ca * Vsub^(5/3) * |w|^2 + damping * density * Vsub^(5/3) * |w|)` (`Vsub^(1/3)` is `Math32.Cbrt`). Cd is `LinearDrag` (default 0.5), Ca `AngularDrag` (0.5), damping `ViscousDamping` (0 in `FluidDef`, 1 from `AddWater`). The quadratic part gives the analytic terminal velocity `sqrt(2 (rho_b - rho_f) V g / (rho_f Cd A))` (test `DragGivesTheAnalyticTerminalVelocity`); the linear part stands for viscosity and radiated waves and is what stops a floating body bobbing. Each force is capped so one step cannot reverse the relative motion (`|F| <= m |vr| / dt`; the torque is scaled so `|inverse inertia * torque * dt| <= |w|`).

**Submerged volume.** Piece by piece, in the frame of the centre of mass, with signed distance `d(p) = n . p + h0` (`h0` the double-precision height of the centre of mass above the plane, rounded once to float32; positive is above the surface). A sphere is the closed-form cap: with `s` the centre's height, `h = clamp(r - s, 0, 2r)`, volume `pi h^2 (3r - h) / 3`, centroid `3 (2r - h)^2 / (4 (3r - h))` below the centre along `n`, silhouette `pi r^2 (V / V_full)^(2/3)`. Every other convex shape is a closed polyhedron with outward counter-clockwise faces: a box (its convex radius added to the half extents), a hull (its faces; the convex radius is ignored), or a tessellation of a surface of revolution about y with 24 sides: cylinder (flat caps), capsule and tapered capsule (8 latitude rings per cap; the tapered cone is joined at the tangent elevation `asin((r_bottom - r_top) / (2 h))`, clamped to ±0.999). Ring radii are scaled by `sqrt(2 pi / (24 sin(2 pi / 24)))` so each ring has the circle's area; against a 96^3 brute force the volume error is under 2 % of the shape's volume for the shapes in the tests.

Method (Ericson, *Real-Time Collision Detection*, clipping a polygon against a plane; Catto, "Exact buoyancy for polyhedra", Game Programming Gems 6): each face polygon is clipped to `d <= 0` (Sutherland–Hodgman; the edge crossing is at `t = d_prev / (d_prev - d_cur)`), fanned from its first clipped vertex, and signed tetrahedra `(apex, p0, a, b)` are summed: `vol6 += dot(p0 - apex, cross(a - apex, b - apex))`, centroid `= apex + sum(vol6 * (p0 + a + b - 3 apex)) / (4 sum vol6)`. When the cut crosses the shape the apex is the plane point `-h0 n`, so the cap that closes the cut adds nothing and is never built; when the whole shape is below, the apex is the shape origin. A shape with no vertex at `d <= 0` contributes nothing. The silhouette along a unit direction `u` is `1/4 (sum |2 a_f . u| + |2 sum a_f . u|)` over the clipped faces (`2 a_f` the face area vector from the cross products; the cap's area vector is minus their sum). Polyhedra are built on first use and cached on the geometry (never saved). `Buoyancy3D.Submerged(shape, pose, normal, planeOffset, flowDirection)` exposes the same routine (it rebuilds the polyhedron on every call).

**Snapshot.** Block flag 32, written last, present when a fluid exists or any live body's factor is not 1 (so a world that uses neither keeps its bytes). A handle table of fluids; per live fluid: normal (3 × f32), surface offset (f64), density (f32), flow (3 × f32), linear drag, angular drag and viscous damping (f32 each), region flag (u8), region minimum then maximum (6 × f64); then a count (i32) of buoyancy factors and per factor the body slot (i32, ascending) and the factor (f32, never exactly 1). The reader rejects invalid definitions (`FluidDef.Validate`), normals whose squared length is outside `[0.998, 1.002]`, factors outside 0 to 10^6 or equal to 1, factor entries for missing bodies, and an empty block. `ShiftOrigin` moves the surface offsets by `dot(n, shift)` and the regions by the shift. The world hash is the core's: fluid state reaches the hash only through the bodies' state once a step has applied it.

## Performance

**Contact kernel.** Contacts are solved by one kernel written over lanes (`ILane<T>`, shared with 2D): the scalar twin (1 lane) and 128-bit (4) and 256-bit (8) vectors. A color batch (at most eight constraints, no dynamic body in common) is one group; `PrepareBatch` writes the group once per step in field-major rows (field `f` of lane `l` at `f·8 + l`; layout `W3D` in `ContactKernel3D.cs`): normal and tangents, material, inverse masses, soft coefficients, rolling mass, four point slots of arms, adjusted separation, normal, tangent-1 and tangent-2 masses, relative velocity, and the impulses that change while solving. The impulses live in the group during the step and `StoreResults` writes them back to the payloads. Each phase gathers the two bodies of every lane (velocity, angular velocity, accumulated translation and rotation, world inverse inertia), runs the arithmetic of the Step section for each lane with the same operations in the same order at every width, and scatters velocities of dynamic bodies. A lane with fewer points than the group's largest masks the unused slots with selects (a masked lane keeps its bits); slots past the group's largest point count are skipped. Joints in a batch share no dynamic body with the batch's contacts: point joints take the lanes of a separate joint group (see the joint kernel below), other joints solve one by one; contacts take the first lanes of the contact group. The overflow set (which may share bodies, joints included) runs in list order, one lane at a time. Square root is the IEEE operation, so every width gives the scalar answer; the world hash is the same at widths 1, 4 and 8 and equals the hash of the scalar solver that preceded the kernel. `Engine3D.KernelWidth` (default 8) only picks the width.

**Joint kernel.** Point joints and the point blocks of fixed, hinge, cone and swing-twist joints solve in lanes (see Joints, "Point block in lanes"); every width gives the same bits and the world hash is equal at widths 1, 4 and 8 and at every thread count (test `PointJointSceneIsIdenticalAcrossThreadsAndKernelWidths`, which includes a hub with more joints than there are colors, so the overflow path runs). Joint kinds other than those five (distance, slider, six-dof, path, gear, rack-and-pinion, pulley) and the rows of the five that are not their point block still run one joint at a time. The wide gather reads each body through scalar loads into staging rows (loading the packed body row with the 8x8 transpose of the 2D kernels measured slower, 0.72 ms against 0.58 ms of solve passes on joint-chains-3d).

**Continuous collision early-out.** `Continuous` first asks the static tree for any box overlapping the whole sweep of the body (see Continuous collision); on scenes of fast bodies far from static geometry (hanging chains, 672 bodies) this took the finalize stage from 0.20 ms to 0.03 ms per step with identical results.

**Bounding spheres.** A polyhedron pair whose shape-origin spheres (core extent plus radius) are farther apart than the speculative distance returns an empty manifold before any axis test.

**Islands.** A freed island keeps its body list for the next one and the split uses reusable scratch, so merges and splits allocate nothing once warm.

**Allocation.** After warm-up a step allocates nothing on large-pile-3d and, apart from rare growth of reusable buffers, nothing on mesh-terrain-3d and heightfield-terrain-3d (median 0 bytes per step; it was 9.8 KB and 7 KB). The garbage came from handle checks that built their message text before testing (`Resolve`), a fresh options object per option check, `Span.Sort`/`Array.Sort` with a comparer (a delegate per call; pairs and events now use `Core/Sorting.cs`, whose orders are total so the result is unchanged), and the list a continuous-collision cast against a mesh allocated per call (now one per thread). Island body lists that outgrow their island go to a small spare pool that the next growth draws from. What is left is growth of narrowphase result buffers (one per 64-pair chunk, doubling) and island lists when a bigger contact set than before appears.

**Tight-box rejection.** Each step the tight box of every shape of a moving body is kept. A pair whose boxes (this step's for a moving shape, the stored fat box for one that did not move) are farther apart than the speculative distance plus 0.005 has no contact points and returns an empty manifold before either shape is loaded. Before round 23, on large-pile-3d 32,100 pairs were live and 17,600 passed this test (fat boxes carried a 0.1 margin plus twice the last displacement); with the 0.03 margin 20,600 are live and the same 17,600 pass (round 23). Results are identical to running the narrowphase.

**Manifold reuse.** A touching convex-convex pair keeps its manifold while B has moved and turned relative to A by less than a bound: `|change of B's centre in A's frame| + 2|vector part of the change of B's rotation in A| * MaxExtent(B) <= 0.0035` (metres; Catto's contact reuse, Box2D 3.2 release notes), and every reused point stays within -0.02 and the speculative distance. The payload keeps, after the edge hint, a copy fixed to the bodies (`ManifoldLayout.RecycleOffset`, 40 floats): an owner flag (1 = the normal follows A, 2 = B), the normal in the owner's frame, B's centre in A's frame and B's rotation in A (3 + 4), and per point the arm on A in A's frame, the arm on B in B's frame and the separation when made. The owner is B when the normal is parallel (cosine > 0.99999) to a face normal of B and not of A (box axes, hull faces, cylinder caps); otherwise A (edge against edge, balls and capsules then follow A, which is right to within the allowed motion divided by the shapes' size). A reused manifold rebuilds arms from the stored locals with the current rotations, the normal from the owner's rotation, and each separation as the one when made plus the motion of B's point relative to A's point along the normal; feature ids, warm-start impulses and the hint stay. Materials are re-mixed. Pairs that are not convex-convex (compounds, meshes, height fields) and sensor pairs are never reused. The payload of `DimensionLayout.Spatial` grew from 64 to 104 floats, which changes snapshot bytes and every world hash that includes a 3D or core payload. On large-pile-3d about 99.7 % of touching pairs are reused once the pile has settled; collision time 14.2 to 7.2 ms per step at one thread, step time 37.8 to 26.3 ms (8 threads 10.9 to 9.5). The bound was picked from a sweep (0.0003 to 0.005): the ragdoll-pile-3d restKE and sleep-wake-3d escapes metrics are chaotic across it (restKE 0.33 to 0.78 against a limit of 0.5; escapes 0 to 17 against 10), 0.0035 passes both.

**Edge pairs.** The per-edge data of the second hull (negated face normals, their cross product, first vertex, direction and length) is computed once per query instead of once per edge pair; the arithmetic is the same operations, so the result is bit for bit the same. It is a small gain (EdgeQuery 11.6 % to 10.6 % of large-pile-3d at one thread); a dedicated box-box test was not written because the Gauss-map pruning already keeps only the pairs that can form a face of the Minkowski difference, and the edge pair chosen by it, not only the axis, decides the contact.

**Parallel prepare and manifold writes.** All batches of all colors are prepared in one parallel call (a call per color paid a fork and join for each of up to 24 small colors; prepare at 8 threads on large-pile-3d went from 2.2 to 1.1 ms). The serial pass of the contact stage now only adds, matches and removes constraints, which fixes the colors; the manifolds are written into the payloads afterwards in parallel, one chunk of pairs per task (serial part 2.9 to 0.7 ms at one thread). A user material mixer keeps the serial path, so a mixer is still called in pair order on the stepping thread. The kernel's gather reads displacement, accumulated rotation and world inverse inertia from one packed 16-float row per body (`Engine3D.Pk`) filled at the start of the contact stage and after each position integration.

**Sleeping worlds.** A world that has fallen asleep costs only what it must: `CheckBounds` skips sleeping bodies, the contact stage does not refresh inertia of bodies that stay asleep, scans no pairs when no body is awake or kinematic, and the active-constraint scan is skipped when no body is awake. pyramid-3d-30 (9455 boxes) went from 2.6 ms to 0.15 ms per step once asleep. It falls asleep at step 79 (it compresses for about 40 steps, then rests for the 30-step sleep time), so a timing window that starts at step 60 still contains 19 awake steps of about 320 ms each. The first step allocates about 250 MB: the constraint store, the wide groups and the per-chunk results grow by doubling to hold some 50,000 contacts; it is a one-time cost, and the narrowphase generator state is now kept per thread instead of per 64-pair chunk (it was 45 MB of that).

**Kernel width.** `Engine3D.KernelWidth` defaults to 8 lanes where `Vector256.IsHardwareAccelerated` and to 4 (two 128-bit halves of a group) otherwise, because 256-bit vectors are emulated on arm64. Every width gives the same bits.

**Temporal coherence for polyhedron pairs.** A full separating-axis query on two touching hulls or boxes searches all face axes and every edge pair that spans a face of the Minkowski difference; the edge search is about a third of the query. After a full query the pair keeps seven floats in the spare tail of its contact payload (`ManifoldLayout.HintOffset`; they are part of snapshots and world hashes): the margin by which the face axes led the best edge axis (threshold-adjusted, capped at the sum of the shapes' radii, zero if an edge axis was used) and the pose of B in A at that query (translation and the vector part of the rotation with a non-negative scalar part). On the next step the edge search is skipped while `margin > 8 · (translation change + 2·|vector part of the relative rotation| · (radiusA + radiusB))`, measured against the pose of the last full query (a skipped step carries the old hint forward, so motion accumulates and a drifting pair is re-queried once it has moved an eighth of its margin). Face queries, clipping and the GJK fallback for separated cores always run, so a skipped edge search can only change a result where an edge axis would have overtaken the face axes by more than the margin within that motion. This follows Gregorius, "Robust Contact Creation for Physics Simulations" (GDC 2015), on reusing the separating feature, and Catto's persistent manifolds (GDC 2007). It changes contact points rarely and the world hashes (the payload tail is hashed); a pair with no previous contact, a changed sub-shape, or a pose beyond the threshold gets the full query. The hint is read in the parallel narrowphase and written with the manifold, so it cannot depend on thread count.

**Sleeping on terrain.** The 3D world splits an island as soon as any of its bodies has rested for the sleep time (Step section). Awake bodies on mesh-terrain-3d at steps 120, 240, 360, 480, 600: before 1295, 1149, 1144, 1169, 1176 (all but a few of them motionless but joined to a rolling body); after 1294, 926, 753, 577, 507. Reference engines on the same scene (black-box dumps, awake flag): Jolt 1251, 615, 476, 235, 149; Box3D 1288, 859, 685, 571, 484; Rapier3D 1297, 888, 686, 571, 479. Rolling spheres and capsules have no rolling resistance by default here, as in the references' documented defaults (Jolt documents linear and angular damping of 0.05 per second and no rolling resistance; Rapier documents no damping by default), so rolling bodies stay awake on slopes until they reach a basin.

**Remaining gap** (before round 6, described at the end of this section) at one thread against the fastest reference (ms): large-pile-3d 42.0 vs 5.2 (Rapier3D), mesh-terrain-3d 9.5 vs 0.91 (Jolt), heightfield-terrain-3d 8.7 vs 0.89 (Jolt), sleep-wake-3d 9.1 vs 1.51 (Rapier3D), queries-3d settle 39.7 vs 5.7 (Rapier3D). Causes: the gather and scatter of body state per lane is scalar (nineteen floats per body per phase from five arrays; a packed per-step body row would turn it into one row load); the polyhedron axis test runs all edge pairs for every touching pair (a cached separating feature per pair does not help touching pairs, only an incremental manifold would); the group's lanes are masked for the unused point slots rather than sorted by point count; steady-state allocation is not zero on soup scenes (about 6.7 KB per step in pair churn and 2.8 KB in continuous collision's mesh casts on mesh-terrain-3d; 0.7 KB on large-pile-3d). Dedicated box-box code and the per-pair caching of the separating feature were not done. None of this is determinism-bound: no row is blocked by determinism rules.

**Contact speed, round 6.** Four changes, none of which changes a hash on the pinned scenes (the world3d manifest lines are unchanged):

- *Batches sorted by point count.* Before the batches are prepared, each color's constraints are reordered stably by contact point count (four points first, then three, two, one, none; joints last). A group runs as many point passes as its largest lane needs, and a pile is mostly one-point contacts with a few four-point ones, so unsorted batches ran about three point passes where one-point contacts need one (average largest count per batch 1.4 after sorting). The constraints of one color share no dynamic body, so the order they are solved in cannot change a result; the order depends only on the constraint data. Solve and relax passes on large-pile-3d at one thread: 3.8 ms to 2.2 ms each (locally measured, indicative).
- *Whole-row body gathers and smaller kernel frames.* The kernel gathers linear and angular velocity and the packed rows with the same 8x8 transposes the 2D kernel uses (`GatherRows`); the velocity arrays carry eight spare floats so an eight-float window can be read from any body's slot (only the first three are used; the spare floats are never written). The kernel's loads of body state, per-point state and the rolling-resistance block moved out of the main kernel method, which cut its stack frame from 10 KB (zeroed on every call) to 4.6 KB; a group in which no lane has rolling resistance skips the rolling block (every lane would keep its bits). A warm-start pass dropped from 1.9 ms to 1.0 ms.
- *Pairs proven apart.* A convex pair without a constraint that the polyhedron test (or the bounding spheres) found empty remembers the pose of B in A and by how much it was apart beyond the speculative distance (`ManifoldGenerator3D.ClearanceOut`: the face-plane separation, or the distance of the bounding spheres). It skips the query until the relative motion (change of the centre-of-mass offset in A's frame, plus the chord of the relative turn times B's farthest point, plus a millimetre and a float-rounding allowance) has used that margin up. The record is a cache: it is not in snapshots and a restored world recomputes it, giving the same answer. On large-pile-3d about 3100 of 8700 empty full queries per step are skipped; the rest are pairs only a few millimetres beyond the speculative distance, which no motion bound can hold for more than a step or two.
- *Triangle-soup contact reuse.* A convex shape resting on a mesh or height field keeps the manifold of every triangle contact of the pair while it has moved less than the convex-pair tolerance relative to the soup's body (the stored relative pose is the mover's in the soup body's frame, and the turn is multiplied by the mover's farthest point, not the terrain's). If any one contact of the pair has moved too far, the whole pair runs the soup query again. mesh-terrain-3d: collide 3.2 ms to 1.8 ms per step at one thread (locally measured, indicative).

Where large-pile-3d spends a step now (one thread, local, indicative, ms): contact stage 11 (collide 8: 6600 full queries of which 5500 find nothing within speculative range, 7700 recycled manifolds, 14,000 pairs rejected by tight boxes; tight boxes and broadphase moves 0.8; pair list 0.9; payload writes 0.6; apply 0.4), batch preparation 2.0 (effective masses and tangents per point, scalar), solve passes 6.8 of which integration 1.0, warm start 1.0, solve 2.2, relax 2.1, restitution 0.25; everything else 0.6.

**Contact stage, after round 6.** Three changes, none of which changes a hash (the world3d manifest lines are unchanged):

- *Constraint storage grows geometrically.* `Reserve` used to resize every constraint array to exactly the requested size, so each step in which the pair table grew by one copied all of them, payloads included (tens of megabytes in a big pile): 0.7 to 0.9 ms of a large-pile-3d step at one thread. It now grows by at least half again.
- *Reuse before the shape frames.* A touching convex pair tries to reuse its manifold before the shape-frame quaternions, the arms and the relative pose are built; the reuse does not read them.
- *Pair list in fixed chunks.* The per-body active flag is computed once per step, and the list of pairs needing fresh manifolds is built per fixed chunk of 1024 pairs in parallel and packed in chunk order, so it is the same list at any thread count.

Where the rest of the polyhedron time goes (one thread, local, indicative): about 800 box/hull pairs per step reach the separating axis test, at roughly 2.1 us each: loading both polyhedra 0.37, face axes 0.28, edge axes 1.07 (the edge-axis search runs for 5 of 6 queries in a settling pile because the cached margin rarely holds), clipping 0.2. They total 1.7 ms of a step; the other convex queries (spheres and capsules against anything) total about 1.5 ms. Tight-box rejection, manifold reuse (about 0.25 us each) and the serial apply pass remain the other collide costs.

Further contact-stage changes (hash-neutral; every world3d manifest line is unchanged):

- *Tight boxes in parallel.* The tight boxes of the awake bodies' shapes are pure functions of body state, so they are computed in fixed chunks of 32 bodies in parallel and the broadphase tree is updated serially from them in list order. At one thread they were 0.9 ms of large-pile-3d, the largest serial piece at eight.
- *Edge-axis search in four lanes.* The edge-pair search of the polyhedron test (Gregorius, GDC 2013) runs four edge pairs of the second polyhedron at a time: both arc tests, the crossing test, the axis and its separation are evaluated per lane with the arithmetic of the one-pair code, and candidates are taken in ascending pair order (strict `>`), so ties still go to the lowest pair. Per touching box/hull query 2.15 to 1.85 us.
- *Capsule against box or hull: face-plane early exit.* If a face plane of the polyhedron separates the capsule's core segment from the polyhedron's core by more than the radii and the speculative distance, the pair is empty (and the gap is reported as the clearance for query skipping). Apart pairs: 520 to 230 ns (box) and 420 to 110 ns (hull). Checked against GJK distance in `CapsulePolyApartTests`.
- *Batch preparation in lanes.* The effective masses along the normal and both tangents, the adjusted separations and the relative normal velocities of a group are computed for its eight lanes together by `ContactKernel3D.PrepareMasses`, in the kernel width of the machine; the scalar pass only stages inputs. Every lane performs the scalar sequence of operations, so all widths give the same bits. Batch preparation at one thread on large-pile-3d: 1.86 to 1.23 ms (best of five, minimum over steps).

Measured with in-process stage timers on a loaded host (minimum over steps, best of five runs, ms), main before / after: large-pile-3d one thread total 17.2 / 15.3 (contacts 7.3 / 6.4, prepare 1.86 / 1.23, solve 5.8 / 5.2), eight threads 4.7 / 4.0 (contacts 2.3 / 1.6); mesh-terrain-3d 2.80 / 2.62 and 1.67 / 1.35.

Left as measured not worth doing now: manifold reuse costs 265 ns per reused pair (6,650 a step, 1.7 ms); counting the arithmetic it does, rotation matrices instead of quaternion rotations would save at most a third of it and move every hash. The contact kernel's gather and scatter are about a tenth of a solve pass, so a packed velocity layout cannot return more than about 0.3 ms of 5.

**Contact stage, round 8.**

- *Tight boxes in closed form.* For a sphere, box, capsule or cylinder (all symmetric about their origin) the half extent along world axis i is the support function along row i of the rotation: `|R| * half sizes + convex radius` for a box (Ericson, Real-Time Collision Detection, 4.2.6), `|R_i,y| * half height + r` for a capsule, `sqrt(R_ix^2 + R_iz^2) * radius + |R_iy| * half height + r` for a cylinder, `r` for a sphere. The same 2^-16 relative padding applies. This replaces six support queries per shape (each normalising its direction when the shape is rounded). The boxes differ from the support-based ones in the last bits, so world hashes of scenes with these shapes moved. Hulls and tapered capsules keep the support queries. large-pile-3d boxes stage 1.0 to 0.5 ms, ragdoll-pile-3d 0.6 to 0.15 ms (one thread, local, indicative).
- *Apply skips empty pairs.* A pair with no new points and no constraint has nothing to add, match or remove, for sensor pairs as well; the serial apply pass steps over it.
- *Seams.* A contact point in a triangle's face region but within 5 mm of an inactive edge counts as on that edge. A point on a seam falls on either side of the edge depending on float rounding, and which one depends on the exact pose, so a spinning sphere crossing the same seam kept a leaning normal at some phases and was kicked up (0.57 m/s at 20 rad/s against 0.012 without spin). Tests: spinning spheres on a triangle mesh and on a height field.
- *Where the narrowphase time goes* (large-pile-3d, one thread, instrumented, indicative): about 27,000 pairs a step, of which 46% are rejected by the tight boxes (about 20 ns each), 24% reuse their manifold (220 ns each, 1.7 ms), 16% run the full query (4,500 a step, 3 ms of which box/hull pairs take half at about 2 us each), the rest are skipped as still apart.
- `ShiftOrigin` leaves the position of a destroyed body slot at zero (a snapshot with a non-zero dead slot is refused by the reader).

### Round 9: step cost (no result changes)

None of these changes a world hash; every `world3d` and `collision3d` hash-matrix line is as before.

- *Kernel frames are not cleared.* The contact and joint solver kernels carry kilobytes of vector-struct locals (a batch's bodies and points); the runtime cleared about 3.7 KB at every call. They are marked `SkipLocalsInit` (the project file allows unsafe blocks only so that the attribute compiles; the source scan still rejects the keyword). Every local is assigned before it is read, and the one stack buffer (gather offsets) is fully written before use. About 8% of a pile step.
- *Joint kernel gathers like the contact kernel.* Bodies come in through the same whole-row loads and transposes, and velocities go out through the same unchecked scatter (`Mem` accessors, bounded by the prepared body slots; bounds-checked in `Checked`).
- *Contact preparation gathers bodies itself.* The inverse inertias and velocities of a group are gathered in `PrepareMasses` instead of being copied lane by lane into scratch rows; the scratch rows are gone from the group.
- *Restitution pass skipped* for a group where no lane has restitution (the pass would change nothing).
- *Clearance for more pair kinds.* Sphere and capsule pairs, sphere against polyhedron and GJK-decided pairs report how far apart they are, so the pair is not queried again until the relative motion has used the distance up (the same rule as polyhedron pairs). In large-pile-3d full queries fell from about 3,900 to 430 a step.
- *Box A reuse in the SAT:* shape A's vertices, normals and plane offsets are kept between consecutive queries with the same box.
- *Bounds check:* a dynamic body well inside the range (position under 1e6, sizes under 1e5) skips the exact box test; anything else takes it.
- *Parallel glue:* the per-body start-of-step pass runs in chunks of 128 bodies; the serial constraint pass visits only the pairs the narrowphase tasks listed.

### Round 10: kernel inlining and the pair table

Correction to earlier notes: the polyhedron separating-axis test exists and is a small part of a step; the gap to the leaders is per-pair overhead around it and the solver.

* **Kernel methods stay small enough to be inlined into.** The runtime inlines only a few hundred calls into one method; after that every vector operation of a lane type is left as a call that returns its 32-byte result through memory. The point-joint block, the contact normal row (separation, then impulse) and the friction row had passed that limit (over a hundred un-inlined operations per batch in the joint block). They are now split into methods of their own (`SolveBlock`, `Block`, `Matrix`, `Separation`, `NormalImpulse`, `FrictionImpulse`). Arithmetic is unchanged for contacts (hashes equal). Per pass over the pile's contacts, microseconds (one thread, warm, best of 300): solve 535 to 309, relax 532 to 306; joint-chains point joints: solve 58 to 19.
* **Point-joint inertia term** written out entry by entry (the six products of the anchor are shared), which changes the rounding of joint scenes: the `world3d/joints` and `world3d/ragdolls` lines moved, all others are equal.
* **Pair table** is the open-addressing `PairTable` of 2D; the broadphase pair filter drops pairs that already have an entry.
* `IntegrateVelocities` reads through unchecked accessors (bounds checked in the Checked build).
* Not worth it (measured): a transposed three-float scatter of velocities, inlining the row methods back, and removing the zeroing of unused point slots.

Every hash-matrix line is unchanged.
* Triangle-soup pairs look up the per-thread buffers once per pair instead of once per triangle.

### Round 12: box against box (results change)

* **Where the time was** (stage timers and a sampled profile, one thread, loaded shared host). sleep-wake-3d: narrowphase 2.5 of 4.7 ms, of which the polyhedron test ran 1,200 pairs a step at about 1.1 µs each (loading B's corners, both face queries, the edge-pair search, GJK to confirm a gap). ragdoll-pile-3d: all 1,500 bodies awake, 3,500 constraints in 446 groups of eight (7.9 on average), about 47 ns per constraint per pass; narrowphase 0.9 ms, 11% of the step in GJK/EPA for capsule against box; the solve is flat (contact kernel 30%, integration 13%, joint kernel 11%). large-pile-3d is 40% contact kernel and gather. Fewer substeps are not an option (3 fail the joint scenes), and Bepu's 1-substep, 8-iteration solve does about two thirds of our passes plus three more integrations.
* **Dedicated box–box separating axis test** (`ManifoldBoxBox3D.cs`; Ericson, *Real-Time Collision Detection* 4.4.1; Gregorius GDC 2013). Face separations come from the half extents and the relative rotation's absolute values; the nine edge axes are the cross products of axes, kept only when the axis lies strictly inside the normal cones of both supporting edges (a margin of 1e-4 on the unit axis; closer to the rim it is a face axis already measured); the supporting edges are picked by the signs of the axis components and numbered like the shared unit cube's edges. Neither box is expanded into corners unless the test finds a contact to build (about 40% of pairs end before that). Face and edge numbering, feature ids, tolerances and the edge-skip hint are unchanged; a placed box's corners are the centre plus signed columns.
* **Closest pair without GJK.** For a separated pair whose best axis is a face axis, when the other core's nearest corner projects inside that face, that corner and its projection are the closest points (the axis value is a lower bound and this pair attains it), so they replace GJK's answer directly. Other cases (a corner or edge past the face's rim) still ask GJK.
* **Zero damping skips the two divisions** per body per substep (a factor of exactly one).
* Checked against the same boxes given as hulls (general route) and EPA over 40,000 random poses (`BoxBoxTests`): same touching decision except within 2 mm of the speculative distance, normals within 0.995 cosine, depth within 3 mm; known face, edge, stacked, pressed and hair-separated cases. The cached polyhedron of A is never touched by a pair that skips loading it (radii and centre distance are locals): an earlier version wrote them into the cache and a restored world no longer matched the uninterrupted one.
* Measured (local, shared host at load 18 to 28, taskset, interleaved best of three, median ms): sleep-wake-3d 4.54 to 3.74 at one thread (1.22×) and 1.78 to 1.60 at eight pinned (1.1×); large-pile-3d, ragdoll-pile-3d, mesh-terrain-3d, heightfield-terrain-3d, joint-chains-3d and queries-3d within 0 to 7% of the base. Stability: every 3D scene within its thresholds; moved metrics: sleep-wake-3d maxPenetration 0.47 to 1.05 (threshold 1.5; one frame of the thrown ball's debris), ragdoll-pile-3d maxPenetration 0.12 to 0.20 and jointError 0.033 to 0.059 (thresholds 0.4 and 0.3).
* Open: capsule against box still goes through GJK/EPA; a dedicated segment-against-box manifold would move ragdoll-pile-3d by about a tenth. The solve is flat, so the larger factors need fewer passes per substep, not tuning of one kernel.

### Round 13: pass structure (no result changes; measured dead ends)

Every candidate below was built, run through all fifteen 3D stability scenes at one thread and dropped. Nothing in `src/` changed. `bar run` takes `BAR_SUBSTEPS`, `BAR_RELAX` and `BAR_VELITERS` to repeat the sweeps.

* **Fewer substeps.** Per-step cost, one thread, local shared host (median ms, 4 against 3 substeps): large-pile-3d 9.8 to 9.0, mesh-terrain-3d 2.48 to 2.27, heightfield-terrain-3d 2.77 to 2.58, sleep-wake-3d 3.70 to 3.28, ragdoll-pile-3d 3.35 to 2.79 (8 to 17%: the solver is about half of a step). Three substeps keep every contact scene inside its thresholds (pyramid-3d-30 maxSpeed 0.05 to 0.38 of 1) but fail joint-chains-3d (jointError 0.23 to 1.94 of 1.0, maxSpeed 28 to 81 of 45) and ragdoll-powered-3d (50 fallen parts against none). Raising the joint frequency or damping does not help: the joint frequency is already at its cap for the substep rate, and a damping ratio of 5 makes joint-chains-3d worse (2.79). Two substeps with two solve passes fail the same two scenes by more (jointError 7.1).
* **Warm start fused into the first solve pass** (one gather and scatter per batch instead of two, colour by colour). The first solve of a colour then sees the warm start of earlier colours only, and with one iteration that is not enough: pyramid-3d-10 finalPenetration 0.0004 to 0.041, pyramid-3d-30 topDrop 0.02 to 10.7 (the pile collapses), joint-chains-3d jointError 3.5, ragdoll-powered-3d 50 fallen.
* **Contacts relaxed only in the last substep** (joints relax every substep): pyramids fail (pyramid-3d-30 maxPenetration 0.0017 to 0.41, topLateral 2.8), ragdoll-powered-3d 35 fallen.
* **Warm-start gather trimmed** (inverse inertia first in the packed body row, so the contact warm start reads eight floats of it instead of sixteen): bits unchanged, whole-step time unchanged within 1% on ragdoll-pile-3d and large-pile-3d. The gather is bound by the lane transposes, not the bytes.
* **Kernel width 4 on x64** instead of 8: 15 to 18% slower (large-pile-3d 10.3 to 11.8, ragdoll-pile-3d 3.53 to 4.08).
* **Where a step goes now** (stage timers, one thread, median ms): large-pile-3d 10.4 = broadphase 0.56, narrowphase 3.40, prepare 0.83, solve and integrate 4.96, finalize and store 0.18, sleep 0.13, other 0.36; ragdoll-pile-3d 3.50 = broadphase 0.15, narrowphase 0.87, prepare 0.25, solve and integrate 1.92, finalize 0.10, sleep 0.05, other 0.15. In large-pile-3d the contact kernel with its gather is 23% of the step and the pair driver (`Collide`, `CollideTask.Run`, `TryRecycle`) 20%; the rest is flat.
* **Conclusion.** The soft-step pass structure (four substeps, warm start, solve, relax) is at its stability minimum for the joint scenes and for single-iteration contact stacks; the fused variants trade stability for at most the 17% a third substep gives. A world that has no joints could run three substeps (8 to 12% faster, thresholds held), but then the same scene would step differently depending on whether it holds a joint, which is a product decision.

### Round 14: eight-thread scaling and the capsule–box contact (results change)

* **Why the terrain scenes did not scale.** In a settled terrain or sleep-wake scene a step has about 160 solve stages (four substeps, each with two integrations and a pass over every colour for warm start, solve and relax), and the average stage holds four to six tasks of four batches. The executor takes only as many threads as there is one task each in the average stage (it was two tasks each), so a stage of five tasks used one worker, and every stage boundary was crossed by all of them. Stage-level synchronisation, not work, set the 8-thread time: mesh-terrain-3d spent 0.67 ms of 1.24 ms in the solve stage at 8 threads against 1.17 ms of 3.08 ms at one (1.75× on eight cores), while its narrowphase scaled 6.7×.
* **What changed.** A solve stage now has one task per batch of eight constraints (it was one per four batches), and `TaskExecutor.ForStages` takes as many workers as there are tasks in the average stage. Which thread runs a batch never matters (batches of a colour share no dynamic body), so results do not depend on it. A single-threaded run decodes a stage once and loops over its batches (`IStagedBody.RunRange`), so one thread pays no per-task cost. Tried and rejected: tasks of 2 or of 4 batches for large colours (no faster on large-pile-3d or the 2D scenes, slower on terrain), and a task per batch with the old two-task divisor (the stage count stays high but only two threads join).
* **Capsule against box** no longer runs GJK when the core segment and the core box are apart. The nearest pair is one of: a segment end against the box (a clamp), or the segment against one of the twelve box edges (Ericson 5.1.9 for the segment pair); a segment crossing the box or ending inside it takes the GJK/EPA route as before. The pair gives GJK's normal and points to float rounding, and the supporting-feature clipping that follows is unchanged, so a capsule lying along a face still gets both ends. Results of worlds with capsules on boxes change in the last bits (the hash-matrix lines for collision3d/compound, collision3d/manifold, world3d/buoyancy, characters, host-probe, pile, ragdolls, restore-and-shift and terrain moved; every column is still equal).
* **Terrain pairs.** The inverse pose of the shape and the triangle's normal are computed once per pair and once per triangle instead of per triangle and again per emitted contact. With about 2.4 candidate triangles per soup pair, 58% of them touching, and about 530 soup pairs a step, the narrowphase of mesh-terrain-3d is about 0.85 µs per candidate triangle; the cost is the general convex–triangle test and the manifold clipping, and a dedicated triangle test per shape kind is the next step if the terrain rows need it. The pair driver showed no hotspot above the noise of a loaded machine, so it is unchanged.
* **Measured** (A/B against the commit before this round, one build each, interleaved, best of five medians, pinned with `taskset`, indicative: the host load was 20–30 throughout): at 8 threads mesh-terrain-3d 1.27 → 0.72 ms, heightfield-terrain-3d 1.29 → 0.84, sleep-wake-3d 1.61 → 1.11, ragdoll-pile-3d 1.45 → 1.03, large-pile-3d 2.70 → 2.61 (flat); at one thread every scene within ±5% (noise).

**Where a ragdoll-pile-3d step goes** (one thread, stage timers plus timers around the kernels, 4.6 ms): the solve stage is 2.7 ms: constraint kernels 1.3–1.7 (12 passes over about 3,400 constraints, 31–40 ns each), integration 0.75–0.85 (velocities 0.40, with the gyroscopic term of every non-isotropic body each substep; positions 0.35), the rest staging. **Not done, and why** (ideas that would move the per-pass cost, none built):
* *Joint K and its inverse once per substep.* The solve and relax passes each rebuild the 3×3 block and its closed-form inverse from the rotated anchors (about a third of a joint pass). Reusing the warm-start pass's block in the relax pass changes the arithmetic (the rotation differs by one integration) and saves one of two builds per substep; the joint scenes are the stability limit, so it needs a sweep of its own.
* *One velocity row per body.* Velocities live in two stride-3 arrays and the packed row holds the rest, so a gather is four 8×8 transposes per body side. A 24-float row (velocity, displacement and rotation, inertia) would be three. The velocity arrays are the public `Velocity` storage, so this touches the shared core.
* *Lane-wide integration.* The gyroscopic step and the rotation integration are scalar per body (about 70 and 60 ns); eight bodies per vector would be 3–4× cheaper, with a scalar twin.
* *Single friction anchor per manifold* would remove rows per pass but changes the contact model.

### Round 16: joint block built once per substep (results change)

* The 3×3 block K of a ball-socket joint and its inverse are built in the warm start pass of each substep and stored (six entries, `J3D.KInv`); the solve passes read them, and so does the relax pass, whose anchors and inertias are one integration newer than the ones K was built from. The solve passes of a substep run before positions integrate, so for them K is exactly what the pass would have built; only the relax pass uses a slightly stale effective mass. The cofactor products are folded into the stored inverse, which changes rounding. Per pass the kernel now loads six fields instead of rebuilding K (about 50 multiplications, a division).
* Marginal cost of a solve pass, one thread (slope over 1 to 17 passes, loaded host, indicative): joint-chains-3d 26.4 to 21.4 ns per joint; contacts unchanged (31.7 and 33.6 per manifold in two runs of the same code, i.e. the noise).
* Stability (all 15 3D scenes, one thread, `bar run --mode stability`): joint-chains-3d jointError 0.2337 to 0.2337, maxSpeed 28.42 to 28.42; ragdoll-powered-3d fallen 0, jointError 0.00998 to 0.00998; ragdoll-pile-3d maxPenetration 0.202 to 0.115, maxSpeed 24.1 to 20.2, finalPenetration 0.0031 to 0.0041; everything else within 15%.
* Hash matrix (jit, threads8, noavx2 all scenarios; scalar world3d only): equal; moved lines `world3d/joints` and `world3d/ragdolls` (manifest b72a26143fd5b14a to 705408cbb55a2036).

### Round 17: body integration eight bodies at a time (no result changes)

* `IntegrateKernel3D` runs both integration passes of a substep over `ILane<T>`: a group of eight bodies of the awake list is copied into a staging block (one row of eight floats per quantity), the arithmetic runs over lanes, and the results are written back body by body (the double position is still added in scalar). The branches of the scalar code are selects: a body at rest keeps its rotations and inertia, a zero or non-finite quaternion magnitude keeps the last orientation, a singular or non-finite gyroscopic Jacobian leaves the angular velocity, the speed limits scale only the lanes over the limit, and damping multiplies every lane (a damping of zero multiplies by exactly one). Every width, including the one-lane instantiation, gives the same bits, and they are the bits of the scalar code it replaced: the hash matrix is unchanged by this round (manifest 705408cbb55a2036 in the jit, threads8, scalar, noavx2 columns).
* The first version, with the passes as single methods, was 15 to 30% slower than the scalar code: the gyroscopic step and the position pass each held 150 to 250 lane operations, past the runtime's inlining budget, so every operation became a call (see round 10). Split into `GyroSetup`/`GyroSolve` and `Displace`/`Rotations`/`WorldInertia`, with the intermediate rows passed through the staging block, they inline.
* Whole-step A/B against round 16 (one thread, 20th percentile of 600 steps, best of 3, interleaved, shared host at load 20 to 30, indicative): joint-chains-3d 0.64 to 0.51 ms (1.26×), ragdoll-pile-3d 3.24 to 2.95 (1.10×), sleep-wake-3d 2.40 to 2.15 (1.12×), heightfield-terrain-3d 2.13 to 1.94 (1.10×), large-pile-3d 1.02×, mesh-terrain-3d 1.05×; eight threads 0.9 to 1.17× (noise about ±8%).
* What is left in the pass: about half of the time is the scalar copy into and out of the staging block (about 60 loads and 30 stores a body); the lane arithmetic is a small part. A body-major layout read by whole-row gathers, as the contact kernel does for velocities, would remove it but changes the public velocity storage.

**Built and dropped** (all bit-identical on large-pile-3d, ragdoll-pile-3d, joint-chains-3d and pyramid-3d-10 in the per-step dumps; patches kept outside the repository):

### Round 19: fixed costs of ragdoll-pile-3d and sleep-wake-3d (no result changes)

**Where the step goes.** Self-time profile at one thread (sampled, share of the run): ragdoll-pile-3d: contact kernel 11%, pair driver `CollideTask.Run` 7% and `Collide` 5%, kernel body gather 5%, position integration 4%, joint kernel 4% plus its gather 3%, batch preparation 4%, velocity integration 3%, the batch scan in `SolveBatch` 3%, capsule–box contact 3%, recycle test 3%, segment–segment 1%. sleep-wake-3d is as flat (contact kernel 8%, pair driver 6% and 6%, polyhedron loading 4%, positions 3.5%, then a long tail of 1–2.5% items). Nothing owned by the solver or the primitive manifold routines is bigger than a tenth of the step, so a 1.4× cut would need roughly two thirds of all of them to disappear.

**Built and kept.** The group number, contact count, point-joint count and the "has joints solved one by one" flag of each batch are worked out when the batch is prepared (`Engine3D.BatchInfo`, at four ints per batch, indexed by the batch's first constraint) and the solve passes read them instead of scanning every constraint of the batch in each of the twelve passes. Per-step dumps are bit-identical to before on joint-chains-3d, ragdoll-pile-3d, sleep-wake-3d and pyramid-3d-10, and the hash-matrix manifest is unchanged (c3c8c0dfc7d5fa51 on jit, threads8, threads-all, noavx512, noavx2, tc0 and aot; d9c3005a3cc070f0 scalar). Worker, one thread, pinned, interleaved, four runs, p20 of 600 steps: ragdoll-pile-3d 2.82 to 2.73 ms (3%), sleep-wake-3d, large-pile-3d and joint-chains-3d within noise.

**Looked at and dropped (each moves a step by under 2%):**
* *Integration staging.* Position integration reads 30 values and writes about 40 per body; the displacement, accumulated rotation and world inertia are kept in both their own arrays and the packed row. Reading them from the packed row alone would save a third of the loads, but those arrays are also read by the snapshot writer, the joint solver and the broadphase move, so it is a wider change than the 1% it buys.
* *Three-field velocity scatter.* The velocity arrays are three floats a body, so a four-float row store would overwrite the next body's x.
* *Scalar rolling-resistance mass in batch preparation.* It runs for every contact (default rolling resistance is not zero) but is one fifth of a preparation that is 4% of the step.
* *Primitive manifold routines.* All of `ManifoldGenerator3D.Collide` is 10% of the ragdoll step; capsule–box is 3% and segment–segment 1%. Halving them would be about 2%, and the pair driver around them (12%) belongs to the narrowphase work.

**Result.** The 1.4× aim for these two scenes is not reachable from the solver, the integration passes, or the primitive manifold routines: the profile has no item to cut. What would move them is fewer pairs reaching the narrowphase and a cheaper pair driver, which is outside this round.

### Round 21: pair stage, terrain and overlap queries (no result changes)

* **Mesh pairs keep the convex shape loaded.** A convex shape against the triangles of a mesh or height field is tested against the same shape in the same pose for every triangle, but the polyhedron of that shape was rebuilt (every vertex transformed, every face plane recomputed) for each one. `ManifoldGenerator3D.BeginPin(side)`/`EndPin()` declare that one side of the following calls is fixed; the first call loads it, the rest reuse it. The polyhedron's edge tables (`PrepareEdges`) are likewise kept for as long as the polyhedron stays loaded. Nothing else is cached across calls, so a pair's result is the same bits as before.
* **Height-field box walk.** A leaf block (4 × 4 cells) used to test all 32 triangles against the query box. It now clips the cell range to the box's x and z extent (widened by one cell for rounding; Ericson, *Real-Time Collision Detection*, 7.1.2, on walking only the cells a box covers), builds each cell's four corners once for both triangles, and skips a cell whose heights lie entirely above or below the box. The set of triangles returned is the same, in the same order.
* **Mesh candidates.** `TriangleSoup3D.CollectCandidates` is the engine's own candidate query: no argument checks, bounds read off the rotation matrix, ids sorted as a span. `Overlap` also sorts as a span.
* **Pairs that match their constraints skip the serial pass.** After the narrowphase, a pair whose new contacts are exactly its present constraints (same sub-shape keys in the same order, no body asleep, not a sensor) adds, removes and wakes nothing, so it cannot change constraint order or colours. The parallel narrowphase now records its constraints for the payload write, and the serial apply visits only pairs that add, remove or wake. This applies when the payload write is deferred (64 or more pairs, no custom material mixer), as before.
* **Tree update only for shapes that left their box.** The test "is the moved shape's tight box still inside the proxy's stored box" is a read of the tree, so it runs in the parallel tight-box task; the serial tree update skips the shapes that passed. A NaN box or a non-finite displacement never counts as inside, so those still reach the update that rejects them.
* **Overlap counting and sphere overlaps.** `OverlapSphereCount` runs in a count-only mode that increments a counter instead of storing and ordering a hit. A sphere overlap also tests the distance from its centre to the candidate's stored box (grown by the same lag the tree query used, plus a millimetre) before the exact test; large spheres let many candidates through the tree's box overlap at its corners. Results are the same. Overlap throughput on queries-3d: +8%; ray and cast unchanged.
* **Measured and dropped.** A plane test per candidate triangle (the triangle's own face axis, run before loading it) rejected only 7% of candidates, because the neighbours of a contact triangle are nearly coplanar with it; and a bounds-only candidate walk (no exact box–triangle test) raised the number of triangles taking the full manifold test from 2.5 to 3.4 per pair and was slower overall. Neither is in the code.
* **Where a mesh step goes** (mesh-terrain-3d, one thread, a whole run with a Stopwatch around each stage of the convex-against-soup path, indicative): the candidate query costs about as much as all the triangle tests together; the triangle tests cost 150 ns (sphere), 1.25 µs (box), 1 µs (capsule) and 1.45 µs (hull) each, 2.5 triangles per fresh soup pair; 49% of soup pairs reuse their manifolds. ### Round 24: would a lane-major constraint layout make the pass cheaper? (no library change; measured)

Round 18 judged a lane-major layout by whole-step time and by a per-pass figure that round 22 showed to be mismeasured, so the question is settled here with a microbenchmark: `Micro --solve-layout [steps] [passes] [repeats]` (`bench/Micro/SolveLayoutBench.cs`). It builds a pile of 3000 mixed bodies (spheres, capsules, boxes, tilted) in a walled box, steps it 400 times and takes the batches of that step: 943 groups, 7492 manifolds, 11,457 points (the largest point count per group averages 1.54), 3005 bodies, no rolling resistance. One pass is friction rows then normal rows with the soft-step bias, in two layouts, at one thread.

* **A** is the library's kernel, called as the solver calls it: group rows plus a per-pass gather of both bodies' velocity (two arrays) and of their packed pose and inertia rows, with the separation worked out from the pose each pass.
* **B** is a lane-major layout written by a prepare step: the group also carries both bodies' world inverse inertia (12 rows) and the current separation of every point, and the pass gathers one eight-float velocity row per body (linear, angular, two spare) through precomputed offsets and scatters six floats back. Same arithmetic in the same order; the pass is written in the same style as A (separate non-inlined friction and normal methods).
* **Equality.** After three passes from the same state every body velocity and every point impulse of B equals A's bit for bit (64,350 values compared, 0 differ).

Measured (worker, one pinned core, tiered compilation off, load 5.0 before, 4.9 after, an unrelated job elsewhere on the host; A and B interleaved in one process, best of 15 repeats of 20 passes; three processes):

| | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| ms per pass | 0.237 / 0.247 / 0.257 | 0.170 / 0.174 / 0.174 |
| ns per manifold | 31.7 / 32.9 / 34.3 | 22.7 / 23.2 / 23.2 |
| ns per point | 20.7 / 21.5 / 22.4 | 14.8 / 15.2 / 15.2 |
| ns per group of eight | 252 / 261 / 272 | 180 / 184 / 184 |

A / B is 1.40, 1.42, 1.48 for the pass alone. But B's separations and inertia rows depend on the bodies' poses, which change once per substep (position integration), so B must refresh them each substep: 0.069 ms for all groups (best of 15). A substep's warm start, solve and relax all read the refreshed rows, so charging the refresh to two passes gives A / B of 1.16 to 1.24, and charging it to one 1.0 to 1.06. The prestep rows that do not depend on the pose (arms, masses, normals) are written once per step in both layouts. So the layout is **not** 1.5 times cheaper per pass once the work it moves into prepare is counted; the best case is the pass alone, 1.4 to 1.5 times, which is 0.07 ms of a 4 ms solve stage per pass, 0.8 ms over its 12 passes at most.

**What binds the pass** (knockouts of B, ns per group, full 180 to 184): velocity gathers pointing at one hot row 178 to 183 (the gathers cost about 2 ns: not binding); scatters off 144 to 146 (the scalar scatter of six floats per lane costs about 38 ns, a fifth of the pass); both off 141 to 143. The group's rows stream from L3 at about 14 GB/s (2.5 MB in 0.17 ms), far under what the cache supplies, so it is not bandwidth. What remains, about 141 ns for 1.54 point slots, is the arithmetic itself: some 250 vector operations per point (cross products, two inertia applications, the clamps) with a dependency chain through the two bodies' velocities from one point to the next, and a square root and a division in each friction row. At the machine's throughput that many operations would take about a quarter of the measured time, so the pass is latency-bound on that chain, and neither the layout nor the gathers shorten it. The two things that could still move it are a cheaper scatter (about 38 ns of 184 in B; in the library's kernel the same scalar scatter is part of the 252) and interleaving two independent groups per call to hide the latency of the chain, which changes no arithmetic; neither was built.

**Decision.** No migration of Core storage, prepare, the joint kernel and the snapshot is justified by this measurement: the layout buys at most 1.4 to 1.5 times on the pass alone and 1.1 to 1.2 times net, and the solve stage's gap to the reference (12 passes, 4 integrations and a preparation against 8 passes and 1) is not closed by it. Prestep data is derived, so a layout change would not have touched the snapshot either way. Caveat: the pile has 1.5 points per group on average (a settled pile of mixed shapes), not four; a scene of stacked boxes (four points per manifold) has more arithmetic per gather and would favour the layout even less.

### Round 25: the two bit-neutral levers on the pass (measured; nothing kept)

Both levers named in round 24 were built and measured; neither is kept, and no library file changed. Worker with light load (12 to 13), one pinned core, tiered compilation off.

* **Lane-wide velocity scatter.** The contact kernel stored the twelve velocity rows to a staging block and copied three floats per lane per array with scalar moves. The replacement transposed the rows in registers and stored each body's three floats as eight bytes and four (a four-float row store would overwrite the next body's x, because the velocity arrays are packed three to a body), batch lanes being distinct bodies. Per-step dumps were bit-identical on ragdoll-pile-3d, large-pile-3d, sleep-wake-3d and joint-chains-3d. Whole step, interleaved A/B, 5 runs, p20 of 600 steps: large-pile-3d 8.83 to 9.12 ms (3% slower), ragdoll-pile-3d 2.72 to 2.77 (2% slower). The stores are cheap; the extra shuffles and the offset arrays cost what the scalar copies did.
* **Two independent groups per call, interleaved** (point routines alternated between the two groups so that one group's dependency chain overlaps the other's work). Tried in the layout microbenchmark (`Micro --solve-layout`, 471 pairs of adjacent groups sharing no dynamic body, results equal to the sequential order bit for bit): 0.90, 0.95 and 0.99 times the speed of running the groups one at a time, i.e. slower. The out-of-order core already overlaps the end of one group with the start of the next across the call boundary, and the second group's live vectors spill, so explicit interleaving adds stack traffic and buys no overlap. It was not built into the library.

So the pass is at the cost this arithmetic and these memory moves give; the remaining solve-stage gap is the pass count and the fixed costs, not the per-pass code.

### Round 26: exact origin shifting (results change)

**Exact shifting.** A world shifted by a valid offset steps through exactly the states of the unshifted world, translated: every position differs from the unshifted one by exactly the offset, and every velocity, rotation and constraint payload is bit-identical, for as long as the world runs. The rule has four parts, all in both engines:

* The shift moves every stored broadphase box by the offset in place (bounds are clamped to ±2^22; the clamp is monotone, so containment holds). Tree shape, enlarged flags, refit counts, the move buffer and the pair table stay as they are, so pair creation, ordering and timing do not change. Before this round the shift rebuilt every proxy with plain margins and the shifted world drifted from the unshifted one.
* A body's position increment of a step is rounded to the nearest multiple of 2^-32 (ties to even) before it is added to the double position. A position on that grid plus an increment on it is exact below 2^21, so integration never rounds and the difference of two positions is the same number at any origin. The step of 2.3e-10 is far below float32's resolution at any distance a body travels in one step; a body moving slower than about 1.4e-8 m/s at 60 Hz does not move.
* A shape's tight box is its body's position plus the shape's bounds, each bound rounded outward to a multiple of 2^-32 (down for a lower bound, up for an upper one); the broadphase margin option is rounded to the same grid when the world is built, and the displacement stretch is formed from the rounded step increment. Box bounds are therefore exact sums, and no comparison between boxes depends on the origin.
* The narrowphase forms the offset between two shape frames as the difference of the body positions (exact) plus the difference of the shape arms, instead of the sum of each position and its arm.

The guarantee holds while every coordinate stays within ±2^21 and bodies were created at positions on the 2^-32 grid (every 31.32 fixed-point value is); a position handed in off the grid is shifted exactly, but its later increments add a rounding of its own. It covers rigid bodies, contacts and continuous collision; characters, vehicles, fluids and joint anchors in world coordinates are shifted by the same offset but are not covered by the bit-identity test. The tests step a pile of mixed shapes for 600 steps (3D) and 400 steps (2D) and compare positions, velocities and rotations after every step.

Hash matrix lines changed by this round: every `world2d/*` and `world3d/*` scenario that steps a body (position increments are rounded, boxes and margins sit on the grid), and the broadphase hash is unchanged.

**Box stage.** A shape placed at its body's centre of mass with no turn of its own (a single-shape body made by the common constructors) takes the body's rotation as its pose and skips the composition with the local pose; the result is the same bits. Profile configuration, one thread, indicative, host load 5: the parallel bounding-box task of sleep-wake-3d went from 0.083 to 0.064 ms per step; ragdoll-pile-3d (capsule limbs with local offsets) went from 0.084 to 0.078 ms and large-pile-3d stayed at 0.226 ms. The 2x aim for this stage is not met: what is left per shape (about 45 ns) is the pose from the body rotation, the bounds from the rotation matrix, six rounded bounds, the tree read for the "still inside its stored box" test and the stores.

**Where a sleep-wake-3d step goes** (sampled call stacks, one thread, whole run including scene construction, indicative; inclusive shares): narrowphase `Collide` 32%, of which the polyhedron pair test 22% (loading both boxes 4.3%, GJK/EPA for separated cores 4.3%, clipping and manifold reduction 4.2%, face axes 3.4%); recording the reuse frame of a fresh manifold 3.5%; contact kernels 13%; batch preparation 6%; pair search 4%; integration 8%. No single piece is larger than about 4% of the step, so a 1.3x step needs a different box-box manifold, not a tuned one; that is left open.

### Round 27: box against box in face contact (results change)

When the separating axis test between two boxes is won by a face (not by a pair of edges), the manifold is built by `Collision3D/ManifoldBoxFace3D.cs` without loading either box as a polyhedron:

* The reference face is the face that won the test (A's unless the winner belongs to B). Earlier versions chose the reference again by comparing the areas of the two faces; this is the usual rule (Gregorius, GDC 2015, contact manifolds) and it follows the axis the test already found.
* The work is done in the reference box's axes. The other box's axes and centre become a 3x3 matrix and a vector in that frame; the incident face is the face of the other box with the smallest dot product against the reference normal (the first of equals, in the face table order of `BoxTopology`); its four corners are clipped in loop order against the four side planes of the reference face (`|u| <= half extent`, sides in the reference face's edge loop order); each surviving point's separation is the signed distance to the reference plane minus the sum of the two rounding radii; points are placed midway between the two surfaces and carried to A's frame when B is the reference.
* Feature ids keep the convention of the general route: surviving corners pair the reference face with the corner, and a point where a clipped edge meets a side pairs the two edges. When A is the reference the id is `Pack(reference, incident)`, otherwise the codes are swapped so the high half still names A's feature.
* When the cores are apart and the nearest corner of the incident face is not over the reference face, or no point survives, the general route (GJK for the distance) runs as before. Edge-axis winners use the general route unchanged. The derived GJK point that the general route adds when clipping drops the closest pair is not added: the nearest corner survives by construction.

Measured by the box-box micro-benchmark (`bar micro-boxbox`, pairs taken from a running sleep-wake-3d, public `Collide` only, one pinned core, host load about 25, three interleaved repetitions): 241 ns per call before and 194 ns after, 1090 and 845 ns per manifold made. At the first step sampled, where both runs see the same pairs, the digest of point counts and separations is identical; later steps see different pairs because the worlds have diverged. The micro tool is `bar micro-boxbox --scene <sleep-wake-3d.scene> --reps 40 --steps 150,250,300,340`.

### Round 29: closed-form ray and sphere-sweep leaves (results change)

A 3D ray or sphere sweep that reaches a convex shape is first tried in closed form (`Collision3D/ConvexSweep3D.cs`), in the shape's own frame, in IEEE double on the float inputs; only when that cannot settle the answer does the GJK route of `Gjk3D.RayCast` / `Gjk3D.ShapeCast` run as before.

* **Sweep as a ray.** A sphere of radius `r` swept along the segment `o + t d`, `t` in `[0, 1]`, is a ray against the shape grown by `r` (Ericson, Real-Time Collision Detection, 5.3 and 5.5). A ray is `r = 0`.
* **Shapes.** A sphere is a capsule with half height 0, grown to radius `R + r`. A capsule is the cylinder around the Y axis between `y = -h` and `y = h` plus the two end spheres: the infinite cylinder is entered first and counts when the entry has `|y| <= h`; otherwise the entry is the end sphere on that side (both end spheres are tried when the segment is parallel to the axis). A box or hull (core radius 0 or more) is clipped against its face planes moved out by `R + r` (Cyrus and Beck, the slab method): the entry fraction is the largest `dist / (n . d)` over planes the segment enters through, the exit fraction the smallest over planes it leaves through; the hit face is the plane that gave the entry. Cylinders, tapered capsules and hulls without a table go to the general route.
* **Face regions.** A grown polytope is sharp where the grown shape is round, so with `R + r > 0` the contact is only accepted when the point of the core nearest the contact (the contact minus `(R + r)` along the face normal) lies inside every other face plane (to 1e-6 relative); otherwise the general route decides. A pure ray (`R + r = 0`) needs no such check.
* **What is decided.** A miss, or a hit entering from outside at a fraction in `(1e-9, 1 - 1e-9)`. Everything else is left to the general route: a start inside or within 1e-6 (relative) of the shape (the general route reports the overlap semantics: fraction 0, zero normal), a grazing segment (discriminant within 1e-12 relative of 0, or an entry and exit fraction within 1e-9), an entry within 1e-9 of either end of the segment, a capsule segment nearly along its axis (the cylinder term below 1e-6 of the squared length), the seam between cylinder and end sphere (within 1e-6 relative), and an axis-parallel slab with the start within 1e-6 of the plane.
* **What is reported.** The entry fraction (rounded to float32) and the shape's outward unit normal at the contact; for a sweep, the contact point is the mover's centre at the entry fraction minus `r` along the normal. The caller adds the shape's frame as for the general route.

The general route's own accuracy (about 2e-4 of the segment, see collision3d.md) is the looser of the two, so a closed-form answer can differ from the old result by up to that, and the exact bits of `Fraction`, `Distance`, `Point` and `Normal` of ray and sphere-cast hits change; which shape is hit does not (the test `ClosedFormLeavesAgreeWithTheGeneralRoute` compares 30,000 random rays and sphere casts over a world of every shape kind with the test hook `ClosedFormLeaves` off and on; `ConvexSweepTests` compares 6 shape kinds and 4 random hulls, 4,000 segments each, against `Gjk3D` directly: no closed-form miss where GJK hits, no hit where GJK misses, fractions within 2e-3 of a metre, normals within 8 degrees). Box casts and other movers, overlaps, closest point, compounds and meshes are unchanged. Vehicles' wheel rays use these queries, so vehicle worlds move by those bits; the stability metrics of `vehicles-3d`, `characters-3d`, `tracked-3d`, `motorcycle-3d` and `buoyancy-3d` are identical to the printed digits.

**Result list insertion.** `QueryScratch.Insert` finds the place of a new hit by bisection (upper bound over `(key, slot)`, so equal entries keep arrival order as before) and moves the tail with one block copy; ordering and capacity behaviour are unchanged.

**Measured** (queries-3d, 3000 queries of each kind, one thread, best of four interleaved runs against main at 4f222e5, tiered compilation off, host load 16 to 27 from other jobs, indicative): ray 1.59 to 0.93 microseconds per query (0.59x), sphere cast 1.96 to 1.55 (0.79x), `OverlapSphereAll` 6.1 to 5.1 (0.84x), batch counting overlaps unchanged (3.9 to 4.0). Batch forms at eight pinned threads: ray 0.46 to 0.27, cast 0.56 to 0.45, overlap 2.04 to 1.71 microseconds. Hit counts are unchanged (2941 rays, 35011 overlaps, 2264 casts). Where a ray's time goes now (one thread, per query): the tree walk about 0.35 (23 box tests over the three trees), the leaf about 0.4, the rest the call.

The queries-3d step-time row of the speed gate measures the 300 settling steps of its 3000-body pile, not the queries (Bepu and Jitter2 are not driven for queries), so these changes do not move it.

Hash matrix lines changed by this round (manifest 50754db0777962ec to 706b503084d3ac3f on the 37-scenario set; scalar 100215fd5b6a51bc on its 19): `world3d/host-probe`, `world3d/services` and `world3d/vehicle-kinds`, which issue ray and sphere-cast queries. Every worker column (jit, threads 8, threads at 8 (light-work cap), scalar, no AVX-512, no AVX2, tiered compilation off, NativeAOT) is equal.

### Round 28: a schedule per island (results change)

The world-wide schedule of 4 substeps with one solve and one relax pass is needed only where joints are (round 20). Islands never share a dynamic body inside a step, so each island can pick its schedule from its own content.

* **Rule, decided at the start of the solve from the islands as they are then:** an island runs the world's schedule (`Substeps`, `VelocityIterations`, `RelaxIterations`) when any of its active constraints is a joint, when any contact of it touches a kinematic body, or when it holds a vehicle chassis (the wheel forces are timed for the world's substep count). Every other island runs the contact-only schedule: `ContactSubsteps` substeps (default 2) of `ContactVelocityIterations` solve passes (default 1) and `ContactRelaxIterations` relax passes (default 2). Kinematic bodies move on the world's schedule. Ragdolls are joints, so their islands keep the world's schedule; rigid characters, buoyancy and continuous collision do not read the substep count and follow their island. `ContactSubsteps = 0`, or a value not below `Substeps`, puts every island on the world's schedule (the old behaviour). The three options are saved in the snapshot beside `Substeps`, `VelocityIterations` and `RelaxIterations` and so are covered by the world hash; a restore range-checks them (0..256, 1..256, 0..256) and a world restored from a snapshot steps as the saved one did whatever options it was built with.
* **Order:** the step runs the world-schedule program first (its bodies, then its constraints, as one staged call), then the contact-only program. Bodies of each program are listed in ascending index; the constraints of each color keep their order and are split stably, world-schedule constraints first, so batches never mix programs. Batch numbers run over the first program's overflow set and colors, then the second's. Programs touch disjoint dynamic bodies and share only static ones (never written), so the order cannot change a result. A program with no bodies and no constraints is skipped. When no island is on the contact-only schedule the step is one program exactly as before.
* **Per program:** the substep length, the soft-contact coefficients and the speculative-separation rate come from the program's own substep count. Joint rows are always in the first program. The warm-start impulses stored with a contact are per substep of its program; an island that changes schedule (a joint appears, an island merges) warm starts one step with the other program's scale. Reported contact and hit impulses are multiplied by the contact's own substep count.
* **Why these defaults:** among contact-only schedules, 2/2/1, 2/3/1, 2/4/1, 3/1/1, 3/2/1 and 2/1/2 keep every jointless scene inside its thresholds at the default contact frequency. 2/2/1 failed pyramid-3d-30 once (restKE 2.2e-4 against 1e-4; the pyramid then stays awake and costs 60 ms a step); 2/2/2 tunnelled once on mesh-terrain-3d. 2/1/2 has the fewest passes of the schedules that passed everything (8 against 12), and passed the two contact-frequency scales tried (0.7 and 0.85 of the default).
* **Cost:** indicative, one thread, host load about 29, interleaved, best of 3: large-pile-3d 11.37 to 9.77 ms (1.16×), mesh-terrain-3d 2.59 to 1.88 (1.37×), heightfield-terrain-3d 2.24 to 1.94 (1.15×), sleep-wake-3d 1.02×, ragdoll-pile-3d and the joint scenes unchanged (their islands hold joints). A free body on the contact-only schedule loses accuracy in the gyroscopic term (angular momentum drift over 10 s of tumbling 2.9% against 1.5%).
* **Tests:** adding a joint to one pile leaves the other pile bit-identical for 240 steps at 1 and 4 threads; snapshot, restore and continue are bit-equal across a schedule change in both directions; thread counts 1, 3 and 8 give one result; a warm step allocates nothing.
* **2D takes the same options and the same rule** (`WorldOptions2D.ContactSubsteps`, `ContactVelocityIterations`, `ContactRelaxIterations`, same ranges, same snapshot fields): the schedule path is the core's, so a 2D island without a joint or a kinematic contact can run the contact-only schedule. **The 2D default is off** (`ContactSubsteps` 0, 1 solve pass, 0 relax passes), so every 2D world, hash and speed is as before; 3D defaults to 2 / 1 / 2. This is a choice, not a finding: 2D's rows already pass and its stability thresholds were set at 4 substeps. A 2D joint island steps exactly as with the feature off (tested), and a 2D world with the option at its default is bit-identical to one with it at 0.

### Round 33: mild joints on the contact-only schedule (results change)

An island with more joints than bodies (a lattice or ladder: redundant rows) also keeps the world's schedule. The decision is made at the start of every step from the islands as they are, as in round 28; the constants are not options. Every other jointed island runs the contact-only schedule (`ContactSubsteps`, `ContactVelocityIterations`, `ContactRelaxIterations`; 2 / 1 / 2 by default), with the joint rows solved in the same colour sweeps as the island's contacts. With `ContactSubsteps` 0 nothing changes from the world's schedule at all.
* **Per program:** a joint on the contact-only schedule takes its soft coefficients (`JointHertz` capped at a quarter of that schedule's substep rate, `JointDampingRatio`) from the contact-only substep length; the reaction force and torque it reports, and the break test, use its own substep length. Joint rows and contact rows of one island are never split across programs. This replaces round 28's "joint rows are always in the first program".
* **Why 50:** with the threshold at 40 or 100 the ragdoll pile (150 humanoids) is on the contact-only schedule and passes; at 1000 the 500:1 chain is too and fails (jointError 11.8 against 1.0, maxSpeed 685). The chain's own ratio is 500, so 50 sits between the two with a margin of 10 on that side; the humanoids' largest ratio is below 40 (the same result at 40 and at 100). A scene with a mass ratio between 50 and 500 on a long chain has not been measured; it takes the world's schedule, the safe side.
* **No new persistent state:** the schedule of an island is derived from its constraints each step, so the snapshot layout is unchanged; snapshot, restore and continue is bit-equal for a mild-jointed chain (test).
* **Stability** (`tools/bench/stab3d.sh`, one thread, all 15 scenes): every scene passes. Only ragdoll-pile-3d moved: maxSpeed 17.9 → 21.5 (limit 30), maxPenetration 0.121 → 0.139 (0.4), jointError 0.116 → 0.118 (0.3), restKE 3.7e-5 → 2.0e-5, finalPenetration 0.00417 → 0.00417. joint-chains-3d (0.234), joint-grid-3d (0.0057, still asleep), ragdoll-powered-3d (poseErrorP95 0.058, fallen 0), vehicles, characters, tracked, motorcycle and buoyancy are identical to the bit in the metrics.
* **Cost** (local, indicative, host load about 23, one pinned core, tiered compilation off, interleaved): ragdoll-pile-3d step 3.4–3.75 → 2.7–3.0 ms (best of 3 each), and by the profile build 3.9 → 3.1 ms, with solve+integrate 1.96 → 1.32 ms (0.67×). Other scenes have no mild joint islands and are unchanged.
* **Tests:** a chain of boxes with point joints (also at a mass ratio of 20) steps differently with the contact-only schedule on than off, i.e. it is on it; a ratio of 500, hinge motors, hinge springs and a ladder of point joints step identically with it on or off, i.e. they are not.
* **Not tried:** a different joint formulation that holds the 500:1 chain and the powered ragdolls at fewer substeps (position-level correction between substeps, shock propagation). The rule only moves the islands that already pass.
