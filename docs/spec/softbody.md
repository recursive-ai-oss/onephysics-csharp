# Soft bodies (3D)

Normative for `PhysicsWorld3D` soft bodies: what a body is, the order of a step, the constraints, the contact and coupling rules, the snapshot section and the scenes that measure them. The 3D surface is `[Experimental("OPHYS0002")]`.

## What a soft body is

A mesh of particles (`SoftBodyMesh`: vertices with either a closed or open triangle surface, or tetrahedra) with a mass spread over the particles in proportion to the rest volume (tetrahedra) or area (triangles) they carry. Each particle is a sphere of radius `ParticleRadius` for collision (default a quarter of the mean rest edge length). Particles may be pinned (inverse mass 0). Meshes are validated on construction: counts, finite coordinates inside ±2^21, indices, degenerate and non-manifold elements, unused vertices; errors are `SoftBodyException` with a `SoftBodyError` code naming the field or element.

**Storage.** Soft bodies do not live in the rigid body store. Particles have their own struct-of-arrays store (positions as double between steps, velocities and inverse masses as float), solved after the rigid step. The rigid islands, colouring, contact stage and solver passes do not see them, so a world with soft bodies steps its rigid part exactly as one without (the rigid state and hashes are identical until a rigid body touches a soft one). Reasons: the rigid solve is a velocity-level soft-step loop on 104-float contact payloads, and XPBD is position-level, so particles would not share a pass with it; a payload per particle contact would cost far more than the contact needs; and the block-ordered Gauss–Seidel below is deterministic at any thread count and vector width by construction.

## A step

`Step(dt)` runs the rigid step, then the soft step (`StepSoft`), when any soft body is awake:

1. **Awake set.** Soft bodies in slot order. A sleeping body wakes when an awake rigid body's bounds, grown by the particle radius and the speculative distance, reach its bounds. Candidate rigid shapes are those whose bounds, swept over the step, meet an awake body's bounds (layer matrix, group and mask rules as for shape pairs; sensors never collide). A sleeping rigid body is static for soft bodies.
1a. **Working positions.** For the step every awake body gets an origin, the middle of its bounds (0.5 * (min + max) in double, per axis), and float32 working positions `x = (float)(position - origin)` (and `xp`, the substep start). All of the substeps below run on these; at the end of the step `position = origin + x` is written back as double (a pinned particle keeps its stored position). The result depends on the body's state alone.
2. **Substeps.** `SoftSubsteps` (default 4) substeps of `h = dt / SoftSubsteps`, each with `SoftIterations` (default 3) Gauss–Seidel passes (Macklin et al. 2019, small steps; Müller et al. 2007; XPBD, Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016).
3. **Rigid bodies in the substep.** Each shape's pose at the end of substep k is the linear interpolation (position) and normalised linear interpolation (rotation, shortest arc) between its poses before and after the rigid step at t = (k+1)/SoftSubsteps, plus the displacement that soft reactions have already given the body.
4. **Predict.** `v += g · GravityScale · h`, `v /= 1 + LinearDamping · h`, `x* = x + v h` (float32); pinned particles stay.
5. **Detect contacts.** (Tasks of 32 particles in parallel; each task makes its contacts in particle order and the tasks' lists are joined in task order, then the rigid bodies that take a reaction are registered in that order, so the list is the one a single pass would make.) Each particle sphere against every candidate shape (sphere–convex manifold routine; compounds per child; triangle soups per candidate triangle; a particle that moved more than half its radius is swept against the shape's motion). Contacts are made for a distance of the speculative distance plus twice the particle's motion in the substep. At most four contacts per particle: the deepest are kept, ties keep the earlier. A particle further than 2·10^6 m from a shape makes no contact (the next step reports it out of bounds).
6. **Particle pairs and surfaces.** (Surface contacts, below, are listed at the start of the step or of every substep instead.) Particles of different bodies, and of one body when `SelfCollision` is set, collide as spheres, except pairs that share an edge or a bend hinge or rest closer than their spheres. The pairs are found **once per step**, from the start-of-step positions, with the same margin as the candidate shapes: a pair is listed when the particles are closer than `1.5 (r_a + r_b) + 2 travel`, where `travel` is the step's candidate margin (the fastest particle's speed times dt, half g dt^2, the speculative distance). Each substep tests the listed pairs again by their actual distance (`r_a + r_b`). A sorted cell grid finds them (cell size `6 r_max + 4 travel`, `r_max` the largest radius; keys = hashed cell << 24 | particle number, sorted ascending; a particle looks at the 2 x 2 x 2 cells nearest to it), the particle chunks of 32 are searched in parallel, and the chunks are joined in order, so the pair order is fixed: ascending first particle, then the cell visit order, then ascending second particle.
7. **Soften push-out.** A contact whose particle starts the substep inside the surface raises its constant term so that at most 1 m/s · h of the overlap is removed per substep (split-impulse style); the removed amount is excluded from the particle's velocity afterwards, for contacts not coupled to a dynamic rigid body.
8. **Iterate.** `SoftIterations` passes. Per pass: the constraints of every awake soft body, block by block and then the boundary (see Solver); then all shape contacts in the order detected; then all particle pairs in the order listed.
9. **Finish.** `v = (x - x_prev) / h`, clamped to `MaxLinearSpeed`; approach velocity against surfaces not coupled to a dynamic body is removed (restitution 0).
10. **After the last substep.** The net speed of every awake body is updated, then plasticity and the detection of tears (from the float working positions), then the positions are written back, then reactions on rigid bodies are applied in ascending body index: the accumulated velocity impulse (linear and angular, through the world inverse inertia) and the accumulated pose displacement, rounded to the position grid; the body's sleep timer is reset. Then the step's tears are applied (see Tearing), the passive bodies get their masses back, and soft sleeping is updated (see Sleeping of soft bodies).

## Constraints

XPBD with compliance α̃ = α / h² and damping γ = β / h per constraint family (`EdgeCompliance`, `BendCompliance`, `VolumeCompliance`, units m/N, and the matching `*Damping` in seconds); multipliers are zeroed at the start of each substep.

* **Edge:** distance to the rest length, one per distinct edge of the triangles and tetrahedra, edges sorted by (low, high) particle number.
* **Bend:** isometric bending (Bergou et al. 2006; Bender et al. 2014): for each pair of triangles sharing an edge, the quadratic bending energy of the rest shape, evaluated through the four-particle gradient. Distance across the hinge was tried first and rejected: it locks shear and exploded on a draped cloth.
* **Volume:** per tetrahedron, the signed volume (6V form) against its rest value; or, for a closed triangle surface with `InternalPressure` > 0, one constraint on the enclosed volume against `InternalPressure` times the rest volume (Müller, Heidelberger, Teschner, Gross 2005, pressure).

## Solver

The constraints of a body are **blocked once, when the body is created or restored**, from the mesh alone:

1. **Blocks.** The particles are cut by recursive bisection of the rest positions: a set larger than the block size is sorted along the axis of its largest extent (ties to the lower axis; equal coordinates by particle number) and split into a lower half of `ceil(n/2)` particles and the rest, and the lower half is cut first, until every set holds at most `max(80, ceil(N/32))` particles. Blocks are numbered in the order the cuts end. So a body has at most 32 blocks, and a block holds at most 80 particles unless the body has more than 2560.
2. **Interior and boundary.** A constraint whose particles all lie in one block is *interior* to it; every other constraint belongs to the *boundary* set (region number `BlockCount`). Edges, bends and tetrahedra are classified by all their particles.
3. **Groups.** Within each region the constraints of each kind are kept in index order (edges sorted by (low, high) particle number, bends in hinge order, tetrahedra in mesh order) and packed into groups of four that share no particle: take the first constraint not yet taken, then look through at most 48 not yet taken constraints in index order and add each that shares no particle with those already in the group; a group that reaches four is stored (in the order the groups were made), otherwise its first constraint goes to the *singles*, which are stored after the groups, in index order. The solver copies of the constraints are stored by region, kind, groups, singles; the public raw arrays keep their documented order.

One pass over a body solves, for each block in block order, the edge groups, the edge singles, the bend groups, the bend singles, the tetrahedron groups and singles; then the same for the boundary set; then the pressure constraint. A block's interior constraints touch no particle of another block, so the blocks are independent and can run in any order or at once; a group of four is solved in `Vector128` lanes or by the scalar loop with the same float32 operations per constraint (selects stand in for early returns), so the result is the same on any thread count and vector width. The singles and the boundary set are solved in stored order.

Tasks (the only part that looks at sizes; none of it enters a result): stage 0 of a pass has one task per block of every awake body, stage 1 one task per body for its boundary set and pressure constraint. The contacts with shapes follow as tasks of whole particles (about 16 contacts each; a particle's contacts stay together, in detection order; contacts that move a rigid body form one serial task, in detection order, because they share the rigid body's accumulators), then the particle pairs as one task in the order listed (skipped when there are none), and then the skin limits (see Skinning) as one task per skinned body, so they stay last in every pass. A substep is one staged call over all passes; with one thread, or with fewer than four tasks in every stage, it runs on the calling thread.

*Why this shape:* a stage per colour of every phase cost more in synchronisation than it saved on a cloth of 600 particles (about 25 colours per pass, twelve passes per step). Blocks need one stage for all their interior constraints, and the boundary, about a sixth of the constraints at 8 blocks, is cheaper to solve serially than to colour (a boundary colour has tens of constraints, about as long as one wait). Block size trades the two: 4 blocks halve the boundary and halve the parallelism. Measured on `cloth-drape-3d` (625 particles, 3456 constraints, one thread, indicative): interior 33 us and boundary 6.5 us per pass at 8 blocks (10 at 16 blocks, 3.6 at 4).

## Contacts, friction, coupling

* **Normal:** a position correction along the shape normal, shared between the particle and a coupled rigid body by inverse effective mass; the body's share is `wb = 1/m + (r×n)ᵀ I⁻¹ (r×n)` at the contact point. Later passes add the body's displacement (`wb` times the multiplier applied so far) to the gap, so more than one pass does not over-correct.
* **Friction:** Coulomb, as a position correction (Müller et al. 2007; Macklin et al. 2014): the tangential displacement relative to the surface is removed up to μ times the normal multiplier. μ is the geometric mean of the body's `Friction` and the shape's.
* **Two-way coupling:** a rigid body is *coupled* when it is dynamic, awake and has non-zero inverse mass; it receives the reaction. Kinematic and static bodies are moving or fixed obstacles. Momentum is conserved up to float rounding (tests: a ball into a jelly with no gravity within 2 %).

## Snapshot section

Section id `0x534F4654` ("SOFT"), **optional**: written, hashed and counted only once the world has created a soft body, so worlds without soft bodies keep their bytes and hashes (the core's section rule: a snapshot lacking an optional section resets it; a world that does not register the id rejects a snapshot that has it with a `SnapshotFormatException` naming the section count). Layout: i32 version (3 since tearing and plasticity; readers accept 1 and 2, which have no skin flag before 2 and no tearing block, net speed or plastic values before 3; see Skinning and the sections below); i32 SoftSubsteps; i32 SoftIterations; the handle table; then per live body in slot order: bool tetrahedra, i32 vertex count, vertices (3 × f32), i32 element count, indices (i32 each), the definition (position 3 × f64, rotation 4 × f32, scale, velocity 3 × f32, mass, radius, edge/bend/volume compliance and damping, pressure, friction, linear damping, gravity scale, bools self-collision and allow-sleep, category, mask, group, layer), bool asleep, f32 sleep time, then positions (f64), previous positions (f64), velocities (f32), pinned bytes. The constraints are rebuilt from the mesh on load. Restore validates counts, finiteness and ranges before touching the world.

## Out of bounds

A particle outside ±2^22 m or not finite raises `SoftBodyOutOfBoundsException` at the start of `Step`, world unchanged. `ShiftOrigin` shifts soft particles by the same offset.

Float32 positions near a body's own origin carry the precision of the body's extent (about 1e-7 of it), as the constraint error is measured relative to rest lengths of the same magnitude; a particle that moves less than that per substep does not move.

## Skinning

A body made with `SoftBodyDef.Skin` follows a skeleton. Data (`SoftSkin`, immutable): J joints (1 to 256) with parents before children and joint 0 the only root; the bind position of each joint in mesh coordinates (scaled by the definition's `Scale` together with the vertices); per particle four joint numbers and four weights (non-negative, normalised to sum to 1 in float32 by division; a weight of 0 skips its influence); a maximum distance D, a back-stop distance b and a back-stop radius R (0 to 10^6 m; R at most b); and a rest normal in mesh coordinates (given, or the area-weighted vertex normal of the mesh's triangles, normalised; none for a tetrahedral mesh; R > 0 needs one). Errors are `SoftBodyException` naming the array and entry.

**Pose.** State per joint: a world position (double) and a unit rotation (float32 x y z w), current and as of the end of the previous step. A body starts in its bind pose placed at `Position` and `Rotation`: joint j at `Position + Rotation * bind_j`, rotated by `Rotation`; the particles start exactly on their skinned positions. `SetSkeletonTransforms` stores the given positions and normalised rotations. `SetSkeletonPose(pose, at, rotation)` takes one rotation D_j per joint, relative to the parent in bind-pose axes (the ragdoll convention): the root's frame is qRoot = `rotation` (identity when zero); joint 0: rot = qRoot * D_0, position = at + qRoot * bind_0; joint j with parent p: rot_j = normalize(rot_p * D_j), position = pos_p + rot_p * (bind_j - bind_p). A pose that differs from the stored one wakes the body; `teleport` puts the particles on the targets (positions and previous positions), zeroes their velocities and makes the previous pose the current one.

**Linear blend skinning.** For particle i with influences (j_k, w_k), k = 0..3 in order, skipping w_k = 0: target = sum over k, accumulated in double in that order, of (double)w_k * (pos[j_k] + (double)(rot[j_k] * (rest_i - bind[j_k]))) with the rotation and the difference in float32; normal = the float32 sum of w_k * (rot[j_k] * restNormal_i), divided by its length (zero if the length is at most 1e-6 or not finite). Targets are computed twice at the start of a step, from the previous and the current pose; a substep k (of S) uses the target at fraction t = (k+1)/S, previous + (current - previous) * t in double, and exactly the current target at the last substep. The skinned normal always comes from the current pose.

**In the step.** The skin adds three things to the steps above:

1. *After Predict* (step 4), each kinematic particle (D = 0, not pinned) is set to its target at the end of the substep. Kinematic particles and pinned ones have effective inverse mass 0 (as for pins, they never collide with shapes and take no velocity). Bodies' bounds, for candidate shapes, are widened to the current targets.
2. *At the end of every pass* (after step 8's pair contacts) each free particle is limited, with d = position - target in float32 and c = -normal * b: if R > 0 and a normal exists, and |d - c| < R, d is moved radially out to |d - c| = R (to c + normal * R when d = c); then if |d| > D, d is scaled to length D; if that put d back inside the back-stop (and b > 0), d is replaced by the nearest point of the circle where the two spheres meet, at height a = (R^2 - D^2 - b^2) / (2b) along the normal and radius sqrt(D^2 - a^2) (0 if negative), taking the direction from the normal's perpendicular part of d (any perpendicular if on the axis). The position becomes target + d only when a limit acted. Because the limits come last in the last pass, the position at the end of the substep satisfies both to float32 rounding (a few ulp of D; the tests allow 1e-4 relative).
3. *At the end of the step* the current pose becomes the previous one. A body whose current pose differs from the previous one this step does not accumulate sleep time.

Contacts lose against the limits: a particle pushed by a shape beyond D comes back. The limits act as the long-range attachments of Kim, Chentanez and Müller (2012) and the attachments of Müller et al. (2007), with the back-stop as an exclusion sphere; the sphere definition is this library's own, written from the published description (a distance behind the skinned surface along its normal, and a radius).

**Snapshot.** The section version is 2 when any body is skinned and 1 otherwise, so worlds without skinned bodies keep their bytes and hashes; a reader accepts both. In version 2 each body has a bool after the definition's `Layer` field; when set, the skin follows: i32 J; parents (i32 x J); bind positions (3 x f32 x J); joint numbers (i32 x 4N); weights (f32 x 4N); maximum distances, back-stop distances and radii (f32 x N each); normals (3 x f32 x N). After the pin flags of such a body: current joint positions (3 x f64 x J), rotations (4 x f32 x J), then the same for the previous pose. The reader checks parents, indices, weights (non-negative, sum within 1e-3 of 1), limits, normals (zero or unit within 1e-3), rotations (unit within 1e-3) and positions (within 2^22) and does not renormalise anything, so a restored body writes the same bytes back. The world hash covers all of it, being the snapshot's state in the same order. `ShiftOrigin` shifts the joint positions with the particles.

**Debug.** `DebugSoftBody.FirstTargetPoint` indexes the N skinned targets (for the current pose) appended to the points after the particles; -1 for an unskinned body.

## Surface contacts

Particle spheres alone leave a body porous between its surface particles, and a cloth can pass through itself between them. Each body therefore also has a **surface**: its triangles (a cloth), or the boundary faces of its tetrahedra (`SoftBodyMesh.Triangles`). Surface contacts are position constraints solved in the pair stage of every pass, after the particle pairs and in this order: all vertex-triangle pairs, then all edge-edge pairs, each in the order listed. They need `SurfaceCollision` (**default false in 3D**: the search costs a pile or a draped cloth 1.5 to 3 times its step; **default true in 2D**, where the porous pile is the defect it closes) on both bodies, the pair filters of the particle pairs (layer matrix, group, category and mask), and for pairs inside one body `SelfCollision`.

1. **Listing**, from the positions a listing starts at (Bridson, Fedkiw and Anderson 2002 keep the side of a surface a vertex started on; Harmon et al. 2008 give the pair types). It is made **once at the start of the step** when no surface body's fastest particle can carry a feature, in the whole step, farther than half its particle radius (`1.5 (v_max dt + g dt^2 / 2) <= r / 2` for every awake surface body), and otherwise **at the start of every substep** (a step's travel enlarges the set of reachable pairs with its square: a falling 625-particle cloth listed 12 000 to 650 000 edge pairs per step). A listing's travel `t` is `v_max T + g T^2 / 2` over its span `T` (the step or the substep; its fastest particle's speed, the fall under gravity; 0 for a passive body). Every surface triangle and edge gets a box at the start positions, grown by the body's particle radius plus `1.5 t`. Each body that has a surface neighbour (its grown bounds meet another body's, at least one of the two awake) or `SelfCollision` is searched as a **target**, in body order, in its own `SoftCellGrid` (cell size twice the target's largest grown box extent, key = cell hash << 24 | item number, sorted ascending, runs found through an open-addressing table; a grid per target keeps a small body from taking the cell size of a large one). Into the target's triangle grid, in body order, come the surface particles (ascending) of every awake body that meets it (and of the target itself with `SelfCollision`), those of other bodies only when they lie inside the target's bounds; each looks at every cell that can hold the minimum corner of a box it meets. Into the target's edge grid come the edges of the lower-numbered bodies that meet it, then the target's own with `SelfCollision` (higher edge numbers only), each pair of edges once. Hits are taken in cell visit order (z, y, x), then item number. A pair is listed when the grown boxes meet and the exact distance between the features at the start (point to triangle, segment to segment, double precision, Ericson 5.1) is below `r_a + r_b + 1.5 (t_a + t_b)`. Triangles and edges of bodies that are asleep but touched by an awake body are part of the lists (see Sleeping). The 3D search is serial.
2. **Exclusions.** Within one body: a vertex that belongs to the triangle, edges that share a vertex, and any pair in which a vertex (triangle corner, edge end) rests closer than `2.1 (r_a + r_b)` to the other feature's vertices in the rest shape. Pairs in which every particle has inverse mass 0 are dropped.
3. **Side.** The pair, listed afresh each substep, stores `s` = +1 or -1, the side of the triangle (the right-handed normal of its corners) or the sign of `n . (q1 - q2)` for two edges (`n` the normalised cross product of the edge directions at the closest points) the pair started on. It is 0 (open) when the feature was not inside the triangle or the two edges at the start; the first substep that finds it inside sets it from where it is then. Fixing it from the plane side while the particle was outside the triangle made particles tunnel.
4. **The constraint** `C = s d - T`, `d` the signed distance of the particle from the triangle plane (or of the two closest points along `n`), `T = r_a + r_b` the two bodies' particle radii, active when the projection lies inside the triangle (barycentric coordinates at least -0.05) or both closest-point parameters lie strictly inside the edges, and `C < 0`. The particle moves along `s n` by its inverse-mass share, the triangle's corners (or the edges' ends) the other way by their barycentric shares, `lambda = -C / (w_p + sum b_i^2 w_i)`; the normal is that of the current positions. `C` is clamped to at least `-T` per pass, so a pair that is already deep on the wrong side is brought back by at most the thickness per pass instead of being launched (measured: unclamped, single corrections of 0.12 to 0.47 m and a particle speed of 65 m/s in `soft-pile-2d`). A particle that tunnels within one substep is therefore held on the wrong side only until the next listing; the substep's travel is below the contact thickness at the speeds of the scenes (`cloth-fold-3d`: 0 crossings).
5. **Friction** is Coulomb as a position correction with mu the geometric mean of the two bodies' `Friction`: the displacement over the substep of the particle relative to the point of the triangle (or edge) under it, in the plane of the surface, is removed up to `mu lambda` (Macklin et al. 2014).
6. A pair with a vertex of the same body, a skinned body's kinematic particle or a pin that gives no inverse mass is dropped or moves nothing, like the particle pairs.

Cost: the search runs only while at least two awake or touched bodies have a surface, or one has `SelfCollision`; a single body without `SelfCollision` pays nothing. Measured self-collision: `cloth-fold-3d` (a 24 x 24 cloth stood on end that topples and crumples) has 0 triangle crossings at every 10th step over 600 steps, against 847 at the worst step with `SelfCollision` off.

## Sleeping of soft bodies

A body sleeps when the **smoothed net speed** has stayed under `SleepThreshold` for `TimeToSleep` (and no moving rigid or kinematic contact, no skin pose change). The net speed is the mass-weighted mean square of `(x_end - x_start) / dt` over the free particles for the whole step; `StepSpeed2 += (that - StepSpeed2) * 0.1` each step (a time constant of about ten steps). The last substep's velocity is not used: an XPBD pile keeps a velocity noise of tens of mm/s per particle (the last substep swings around a position that barely moves), which the fastest particle's velocity never let settle (`soft-pile-2d`: 2 of 120 asleep at 600 steps, mean fastest particle 0.11 m/s). `StepSpeed2` is state (it decides rest) and is in the snapshot.

An awake body does not pass through a sleeping one: a sleeping body whose grown bounds touch an awake body's is **passive** for the step: it keeps its state, takes no part in the solve, and its particles read inverse mass 0 in the particle pairs and surface contacts (an immovable obstacle). It wakes when it is touched by an awake soft body whose `StepSpeed2` is above `(8 * SleepThreshold)^2` (0.4 m/s at the default), or by an awake rigid body as before. Before this rule an awake body passed through a sleeping one, and every awake neighbour woke the sleepers again (measured in `soft-pile-2d`: 120 of 120 asleep at 600 steps with it; 12 to 18 awake at a time with a wake threshold of the sleep threshold; 80 with 6 times).

## Tearing

`SoftBodyDef.TearStrain` (edge length against rest length, at the end of a step, after the solve) and `TearForce` (the XPBD multiplier of the last substep over `h^2`, newtons) turn tearing on; 0 is off. Both are measured at the end of the step, in constraint order, from the float32 working positions; an edge that meets either limit breaks. Zero-compliance edges never pass the strain limit, so they tear by force. A skinned body cannot tear (`SoftBodyException` in `Validate`).

A tear is a **broken edge**, a persistent sorted key `smaller << 20 | larger` (`SoftBodyData.Broken`, state): its constraint does not exist, and a bend across it (hinge on the broken shared edge) does not exist. Where broken edges separate the elements around a vertex, the vertex is **split** (`SoftTopology.Split`, shared by both dimensions):

* two elements around vertex v are joined when they share an unbroken edge through v (triangles), or share a face through v with at least one unbroken edge (tetrahedra: a face is cut when all three of its edges are broken); the components are the classes of the joined relation (union-find, smaller element wins);
* the component holding the lowest element keeps v; every other component gets a new vertex, numbered after the last, in the order of the vertices they split; the elements of that component use it;
* a new particle starts at the position, previous position, velocity and pin flag of the particle it split from; masses are shared again from the rest shape (a body keeps its total mass: `Mass` and `ParticleRadius` are frozen into the definition at the first tear so the same definition rebuilds the same body); rest values changed by plasticity follow their edge (a new edge takes the rest length of the edge it came from); a closed body that tears open, or a skin that loses a segment, loses its pressure constraint (`InternalPressure` set to 0);
* an edge stays in `Broken` only while it still has as many elements as the edge it came from (nothing was pulled apart along it) or the old edge had one element; an edge whose two sides were separated is a free edge again and gets its constraint back.

**The body stays one body**, with more particles; pieces that come apart are separate parts of its particle set (no new handle). `SoftBody.ParticleCount`, `Mesh` (the current topology), `BrokenEdgeCount` and the raw arrays describe the current state. Tears are applied after the step's rigid reactions and before the sleep update; a torn body does not sleep that step. Momentum is conserved through a tear: positions, velocities and masses are carried over, and nothing is applied (test: a strip thrown apart with a drift, total momentum equal to 2e-3 relative over 240 steps in both dimensions).

**Events.** The tears of a step, in body slot order and, within a body, ascending edge key, are in `world.SoftTearEvents` (`Body`, `ParticleA`, `ParticleB`: the numbers before any split of the step, and `ParticleCount` after) until the next step, and are raised through `world.SoftBodyTorn` after the step unless callbacks are suppressed. They are derived from the step, not state: not in a snapshot (events raised by the steps after a restore are the same as the original's).

**Limits.** At most one rebuild per body per step (the step's breaks are applied together); a world whose particle count would pass 2^24 stops tearing. A rebuild of a shape-matching body (2D `Both` model) rebuilds its clusters from the rest shape, so the plastic memory of its clusters restarts.

## Plasticity

`PlasticYield` (strain; 0 is off), `PlasticCreep` (0 to 1, default 0.5) and `PlasticMax` (default 0.5) make the rest shape follow large deformations (Müller, Keiser, Nealen, Pauly, Gross and Alexa 2004, rest-shape update; O'Brien, Bargteil and Hodgins 2002 for the yield, creep and limit). At the end of every step, in index order, from the end-of-step positions, for each constraint with rest value `r` and original value `r0`: the strain is `(l - r) / r` for an edge (`l` its length), `(A - r) / r` for a 2D triangle (doubled areas) and `(V6 - r) / r` for a 3D tetrahedron (sixfold volumes); with `e` the strain beyond `+-PlasticYield` (0 inside), `r' = r (1 + PlasticCreep e)`, clamped to `r0 (1 -+ PlasticMax)` and never below `r0 / 1000`. 2D shape matching: where a member is further from its goal than `PlasticYield` times the cluster's reach, its rest offset moves by `PlasticCreep (1 - yield / strain)` of the deviation (in the frame of the cluster's best rotation), is limited to `PlasticMax` of the reach around the original offset and re-centred by mass, and the quadratic fit is refitted. Bends, pressure and skin limits have no plasticity. The strain is measured after the solve, so zero-compliance edges and areas never yield: a plastic body needs compliance (the `plastic-2d` scene uses `EdgeCompliance` 2e-4).

State: the public rest arrays (`RawEdgeRestLengths`, triangle or tetrahedron rest values, 2D cluster offsets) are saved when a rest value has changed (`PlasticTouched`); the solver copies are derived from them. `r0` is recomputed from the mesh's rest positions and needs no saving. Tests: a bar bent into an arc and released keeps 85% of the bend of the tip with plasticity and returns to flat without it (2D), a sagged bar keeps its sag (3D).

## Snapshot (tearing, plasticity, rest)

2D section `SF2D` version 2 and 3D section `SOFT` version 3 are always written (the net speed is state). Per body, after the definition fields (and in 3D the skin block): `bool SurfaceCollision, f32 TearStrain, f32 TearForce, f32 PlasticYield, f32 PlasticCreep, f32 PlasticMax, i32 count, count x i64` broken edge keys (strictly ascending, smaller < larger < vertex count); after the sleep time `f32 StepSpeed2`; and at the end of the body `bool PlasticTouched` followed, when set, by `f32[]` edge rest lengths and triangle rest doubled areas (2D) or tetrahedron rest sixfold volumes (3D), and the 2D cluster rest offsets. The mesh written is the current one (vertices, triangles or tetrahedra after any split), so a restore rebuilds the same particles, constraints and blocks (`Build` is a function of the definition, the mesh and the broken set) and then overlays the rest values. The reader validates: key order and range, positive finite rest values, `StepSpeed2` finite and non-negative. Version 1 (and 3D version 2) snapshots are read with the new fields at their defaults (`SurfaceCollision` true, no tearing or plasticity, no broken edges, `StepSpeed2` 0). The hash covers all of it, being the snapshot's state in the same order; desync labels name `def.BrokenEdge`, `StepSpeed2` and the rest arrays. Fork copies the mutated rest arrays; derived arrays are rebuilt, never mutated.

**Debug geometry.** `DebugSoftBody.FirstBrokenIndex` / `BrokenEdgeCount` point at pairs of particle numbers (the edges that tore and still join two sides without a constraint) after the body's edges in `DebugGeometry.Indices`; -1 and 0 when there are none.

# Soft bodies (2D)

**Sweep direction (2D).** The edge, bend and triangle-area constraints of a region are solved in index order in even passes and in reverse index order in odd ones, where the pass number is `substep + pass` counted from 0 (the pressure constraint, shape matching, contacts and pairs are unchanged). A Gauss–Seidel sweep that always goes the same way round a closed outline gives the particles at its start a different correction from those at its end; a balloon or ring dropped exactly symmetrically then picked up about 0.7 rad/s of spin from that alone and rolled for ever. Alternating cancels the bias. Measured on a 24-segment balloon dropped on a floor: sum of particle angular momentum about the centre 1.7 after 60 steps and 4.3 after 300 (never asleep), against 0.004 after 60 and 0.41 at 300, asleep before step 400 with the rule.

`PhysicsWorld2D` has the same soft bodies as the 3D engine, with the same architecture and API shape: particles in their own store (not rigid bodies, not in the islands or the colouring), solved by extended position-based dynamics in small steps after the rigid step. The 2D code is a separate scalar implementation, not shared with 3D, so no 3D result depends on it. Public types are `[Experimental("OPHYS0003")]`: `SoftBody`, `SoftBodyDef`, `SoftBodyMesh`, `SoftMaterial`, `SoftBodyModel`, `SoftBodyException`, `SoftBodyOutOfBoundsException`.

## Meshes and masses

A mesh is either triangles (an area body) or a closed outline (a skin). `FromTriangles` repairs a clockwise triangle by swapping its last two vertices and refuses degenerate, unused or out-of-range input; `FromOutline` needs a counter-clockwise loop of positive area; `FromPolygon` triangulates a simple polygon by ear clipping; `Box`, `Disc` and `Ring` generate meshes. The outline of a triangle mesh is its boundary when that is one closed loop (then `IsClosed`).
Mass is shared among the particles in proportion to a third of the area of each triangle's rest area (a skin: half of each of the two segments at a vertex). `Mass` 0 means 1000 kg per square metre of rest area for a triangle mesh and 10 kg per square metre of enclosed area for a skin. Particle radius 0 means a quarter of the mean edge length; a particle collides as a disc of that radius.

## Models and constraints

Every constraint is an XPBD constraint (compliance alpha / h^2 with h the soft substep, Lagrange multipliers zeroed at the start of every substep, damping time gamma / h), so stiffness does not depend on the substep or pass count (Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016; Macklin et al. 2019).
- **Edge:** distance on every distinct edge of the triangles (or of the outline), in `(smaller, larger)` order.
- **Area:** `C = 2A - 2A0` per triangle (twice the signed area), the 2D analogue of the 3D volume constraint.
- **Bend:** only for a mesh without triangles: the distance between the two outline neighbours of every outline vertex keeps a skin from folding. A triangle mesh gets no hinge bend because the area constraint already resists shear.
- **Pressure:** `C = 2A - 2 p A0` on the doubled area enclosed by the outline (shoelace sum about the first outline vertex), p = `InternalPressure`, with the area compliance.
- **Shape matching** (Müller et al. 2005), with XPBD's multiplier: per cluster, the best rotation of the rest offsets onto the current offsets (the polar factor of the 2 x 2 moment matrix, as a unit vector from `(a00 + a11, a10 - a01)`, no trigonometry) gives goals `g_i = c + R q_i`; the quadratic mode also fits the 2 x 5 matrix over `(q, qx^2, qy^2, qx qy)` and aims at `blend * (fit) + (1 - blend) * (rigid goal)`. Each membership is a soft constraint `x_i = g_i` with multiplier update `dmu = -((x_i - g_i) + a mu) / (1 + a)`, `a = MatchCompliance / h^2` (seconds squared); the correction is the same for every member, so it is momentum-neutral inside the cluster. Clusters are bisection cells of the rest shape grown by 25% of their longer side, at most 256, of about 32 particles (one cluster up to 64); a pivot below 1e-9 of the largest entry of the 5 x 5 moment matrix falls back to the linear fit.
`SoftBodyModel` selects network, shape matching, or both.

## A step

After the rigid step (rigid poses are kept from before it, so a body that moved far is met where it was), `SoftSubsteps` (default 4) substeps of `SoftIterations` (default 3) passes each. Per substep: predict (gravity times `GravityScale`, velocity damping `1 / (1 + damping h)`), find contacts, solve, derive velocities, restitution zero, friction. A pass is: the interior constraints of every block (one task per block of at most 80 particles, at most 32 blocks per body, cut by recursive bisection of the rest shape), the boundary constraints plus pressure plus clusters of every body (one task per body), the contacts with shapes, the particle pairs, then the target limits. Tasks come from the mesh alone, so the result is identical on any thread count. All arithmetic is IEEE float32 on positions relative to the middle of the body's bounds (the subtraction is done in double), with the double positions stored in the world.

## Contacts, friction, coupling

Particle discs collide with every non-sensor shape the filters allow (circle, capsule, polygon, segment, chain and height-field segments with their one-sided rule and ghosts) through the same manifold routines the rigid engine uses, continuously for a particle that moves farther than half its radius in a substep. Particle pairs of different bodies collide (and of one body when `SelfCollision` is set, except neighbours joined by an edge or a hinge), and so do particles and the boundary edges of bodies with `SurfaceCollision` (see Surface contacts (2D) below); particles of different bodies interpenetrate more than rigid shapes do, because each is a small disc on a mesh whose edges are longer than the discs. Friction is Coulomb as a position correction with the geometric mean of the body's and the shape's coefficients. A dynamic rigid body in contact is part of the position solve (generalised inverse masses) and receives one impulse at the end of the step, so momentum is conserved (tests: 2% over 120 steps). A body sleeps when every particle has been slower than `SleepThreshold` for `TimeToSleep`; an awake rigid body near it wakes it.

## Targets, pins

`SetTarget(i, position, maxDistance)` gives a particle a target, `SetTargetPose(at, angle, maxDistance, teleport)` targets every particle at the rest shape placed in the world (the 2D counterpart of skinning). Targets interpolate linearly from the previous step's across the substeps; maximum distance 0 makes the particle kinematic (it sits on the target and takes no contacts), otherwise a hard distance limit is the last constraint of every pass. Pins give a particle infinite mass.

## Soft sensors

`OverlapSoftBodies(shape, position, angle, results)` is a stateless query: the soft bodies, by ascending slot, with a particle disc touching the shape. There are no soft events.

## Snapshot section

Optional section id `0x53463244` ("SF2D"), version 2 (version 1 is still read; see Snapshot (tearing, plasticity, rest) above for what version 2 adds), written only once the world has made a soft body, so worlds without them keep their bytes and hashes; not part of the 3D section. Layout (little-endian, in this order): `int32 version`, `int32 SoftSubsteps`, `int32 SoftIterations`, the handle table, then per slot (live or not, entity-described) for a live body: vertex count and `float32` vertices, triangle count and `int32` indices, position (`float64` x 2), angle, scale, linear velocity (x 2), angular velocity, mass, particle radius, model (byte), edge compliance and damping, bend compliance and damping, area compliance and damping, internal pressure, match compliance, quadratic flag, match blend, cluster size, friction, linear damping, gravity scale, self-collision flag, allow-sleep flag, category, mask, group, layer, asleep flag and sleep time, positions and previous positions (`float64`), velocities (`float32`), pin bytes, a targets flag followed by target flags, maximum distances, targets and previous targets. Constraints, blocks and clusters are rebuilt from the mesh and definition on restore and never saved. Restore validates exactly (ranges, finiteness, consistency with the mesh) and refuses a snapshot whose triangles the mesh would repair. The hash covers the same bytes.
The replay live-options payload is 128 bytes, or 136 when `SoftSubsteps` or `SoftIterations` differ from their defaults (4 and 3), the extra eight bytes being the two values.

## Surface contacts (2D)

The same design as 3D with one feature type: a particle against a boundary edge. The boundary of a body is the edges that belong to exactly one triangle, or the unbroken segments of an outline, **directed so that the material is on the left** (the outward normal is on the right); a particle on it is a surface particle. The search (`SoftCellGrid`, edge boxes grown by the body's radius plus twice its substep travel, an exact point-to-segment distance test below `r_p + r_e + 2 (travel_p + travel_e)`, ascending particles then cell order then edge number) lists `(particle, edge, s)` at the start of every substep (the particle queries run in tasks of 64 surface particles, each into its own list, joined in task order, so the order is the serial one); `s` is the side the particle starts on (the sign of the cross product of the edge direction and the particle's offset from its start), 0 (open) when the particle projects outside the segment. Each substep and pass, where the projection lies inside the segment, `C = s n . (p - a) - (r_p + r_e) >= 0` with `n` the unit right-hand normal; the correction is clamped to the thickness `r_p + r_e` per pass, split by barycentric inverse-mass shares, and friction is Coulomb as in 3D. Exclusions inside one body: `SelfCollision` required, the particle is not an endpoint, and the rest distance from the particle to the edge is at least `2.1 (r_p + r_e)`. Sleeping (net speed, passive obstacles, wake speed) is as in 3D. Measured: `soft-pile-2d` deepest overlap of two particle discs of different bodies 0.093 before, 0.0075 after; 120 of 120 asleep at 600 steps (2 before); maxSpeed 18.4 (12.6 before).
