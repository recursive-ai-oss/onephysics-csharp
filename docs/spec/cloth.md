# Cloth (3D) and strips (2D)

Normative for `ClothDef`, `ClothParams` (`SoftBodyDef.Fabric`), seams and attachments in `PhysicsWorld3D`, and `StripParams` (`SoftBodyDef.Strip`) in `PhysicsWorld2D`. A fabric is a soft body (see `softbody.md`) with a few more constraints and forces, all deterministic, saved, hashed and forked with the body. The 3D surface is `[Experimental("OPHYS0002")]`, the 2D one `OPHYS0003`.

## What a fabric adds to a soft body

All of it applies to a triangle mesh; none of it exists for a body without `Fabric` (or `Strip`), whose state, bytes and hashes are unchanged.

1. **Weave axes and edge classes.** `WarpAxis` and `WeftAxis` (mesh frame, roughly perpendicular, normalised on use). A solver edge whose rest direction has an absolute cosine of at least 0.9 with the warp axis is a *warp* edge, else with the weft axis a *weft* edge, else a *shear* edge (the diagonals of a square grid). With any of `WarpCompliance`, `WeftCompliance`, `ShearCompliance` set (a negative value means `EdgeCompliance`), the stretch constraint of an edge uses its class's compliance; the constraints are then solved scalar, in index order (no lane kernel). 2D: `AlongAxis` and the perpendicular across it (`AlongCompliance`, `AcrossCompliance`, `ShearCompliance`), same cosine rule.
2. **Strain limit** (Provot 1995; Goldenthal et al. 2007). `StrainLimit` s > 0: after the contacts and pairs of every pass no edge may be longer than (1 + s) times its current rest length. A limit pass is a hard projection: for each edge over its limit, both particles move along the edge by their inverse-mass shares (momentum is conserved; a pinned particle stays). Edges are visited in **limit order**: by breadth-first distance (over the edge graph) from the pinned particles (inverse mass 0), outward, ties by solver edge number; sweeps alternate forward and reverse with `(pass + sweep)` parity; a pass makes 4 sweeps, and the last pass of a substep up to 24 (it stops after a sweep that corrects nothing, once 4 sweeps have run). With no pinned particle the order is the solver order. The limit order is derived from the pins and rebuilt when they change (it is not state). Measured: a 1 m × 1 m cloth of 40 kg/m² hanging by one edge holds 5 % to 6 %; under strong wind or load it can exceed the limit (a pinned 30 × 20 flag in 6 m/s wind: 12.6 % at a 5 % limit): the projection converges slowly on a large mesh, which the limit pass trades for momentum conservation. Velocity follows the correction (the arrest of the stretch is a real change of velocity).
3. **Dihedral bend** (`DihedralBend`, Bridson, Marino, Fedkiw 2003; Müller et al. 2007). The bend constraint of a hinge is `C = δ`, the signed dihedral angle error `θ − θ0` (radians) between the two triangles sharing an edge. With `e = x_b − x_a`, `N1 = e × (x_c − x_a)`, `N2 = (x_d − x_a) × e` (c and d the far corners in the hinge's stored order), `cos θ = n1·n2`, `sin θ = (n1 × n2)·ê`; the rest angle (sine and cosine) comes from the rest mesh. The error is `atan2(sinθ cosθ0 − cosθ sinθ0, cosθ cosθ0 + sinθ sinθ0)` by `ClothMath.Atan2` (octant reduction and a polynomial, error under 1e-5 rad, only `+ − × /`). Gradients: `∇x_c θ = −|e| N1/|N1|²`, `∇x_d θ = −|e| N2/|N2|²`, `∇x_a θ = −((1 − t_c)∇x_c + (1 − t_d)∇x_d)`, `∇x_b θ = −(t_c ∇x_c + t_d ∇x_d)` with `t_c = (x_c − x_a)·e/|e|²` (checked against finite differences by convergence of the kernel). The XPBD update is the usual one with `α = BendCompliance` per radian (not metres per newton) and the same damping. A mesh whose rest shape is curved (`ClothDef.RestCurvatureX`: the rest mesh is the flat sheet bent into a cylinder of equal arc length about an axis parallel to z, curling towards +y; the particles start flat unless `StartCurved`, through `SoftBodyDef.StartVertices`) holds that curvature; the isometric bend (`|m|² = |m0|²`) has no force at a flat start, so it cannot curl a sheet.
4. **Aerodynamics** (`Drag` > 0). Per triangle and substep, before the prediction step: the area A and unit normal n from the substep-start positions; the flow `u = wind(centroid) − v` with `v` the mean of the corners' velocities and the wind the sum of the area fields' `Flow` (as for rigid surfaces); `u_n = u·n`, the tangential part `u_t` with direction `ĉ` (zero when `|u_t| ≤ 1e-9`). `AeroCoefficients.Force(|u_t|, u_n, A, ρ, …)` of a flat plate (`Plate(2)` with `PlateDrag = Drag`, `MinDrag = min(0.01, Drag)`, `LiftSlope`) gives the chord and normal components; `F = f_c ĉ + f_n n`. The velocity change of the corners (a third of F each, `Δv = w F h / 3`) is capped so that `|F| h` never exceeds `|u|` times the triangle's mass (the sum of the corner masses): a light cloth ends the substep moving with the flow, not past it. Air density is the world's. 2D: each boundary edge (material on its left) presents its length times a unit depth; the windward face (flow into it) takes the plate force, the lee face none, skin friction along the edge acts on both; the same cap applies.
5. **Tethers** (Kim, Chentanez, Müller 2012). `TetherAnchors` (particle numbers, de-duplicated, ascending) and `TetherStrain` t: every other particle is held to within (1 + t) times its rest distance (Euclidean, rest mesh) of its nearest anchor (ties to the lower particle number) by the limit pass, after the strain limit, in particle order. `ClothDef.Tethers` takes the pinned particles as anchors. Derived from the definition; after a tear it is rebuilt from the rest mesh.

Order in a step: the aero velocity change, then the usual prediction; per pass: constraints, shape contacts, particle pairs and surface pairs, seams and attachments (with the pairs), then the limit pass (and the skin limits).

## Presets

`ClothMaterial` holds the constants; `ClothDef.Material` defaults to `Cotton`. Stretch compliances are the inverse of the membrane stiffness in N/m of a square weave (an edge is a spring of stiffness about E·t); mass is `ArealDensity × Width × Depth` unless `Mass` is set. Values are starting points:

| preset | kg/m² | warp / weft / shear compliance (m/N) | bend | strain limit | friction | drag | damping time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Cotton` | 0.15 | 3e-3 / 5e-3 / 5e-2 | 2e-3 | 5 % | 0.5 | 1.2 | 0.02 s |
| `Denim` | 0.4 | 6e-4 / 8e-4 / 1e-2 | 5e-4 | 3 % | 0.6 | 1.3 | 0.02 s |
| `Silk` | 0.06 | 8e-3 / 1e-2 / 8e-2 | 8e-3 | 6 % | 0.25 | 1.1 | 0.02 s |
| `Leather` | 0.9 | 2e-4 / 2e-4 / 1e-3 | 1e-4 | 2 % | 0.7 | 1.4 | 0.05 s |
| `RubberSheet` | 1.5 | 1.2e-3 / 1.2e-3 / 2.4e-3 | 1e-3 | 30 % | 0.9 | 1.4 | 0.01 s |

**Damping and rest.** The damping time β of a constraint (Rayleigh damping, γ = β/h) divides the correction of every pass by 1 + γ; at β = 0.05 s and 240 Hz substeps a constraint is 13 times softer in one pass. A fabric that must hold its shape against gravity uses a damping time of a few milliseconds.

**Convergence.** XPBD with 4 substeps of 3 passes does not hold a long chain of constraints against gravity (a 24-cell strip hanging from one end stretches by 38 % without a strain limit), which is why every material but rubber has a strain limit and why the limit pass above exists.

## Seams and attachments (3D)

State of the world, in the cloth-link section (below). `AddSeam(a, pa, b, pb, compliance)` joins a particle of one soft body to a particle of another (or of the same); `AddSeam(a, spanA, b, spanB, compliance)` stitches two rows pairwise. `AttachCloth(cloth, particle, body)` holds a particle to the point of a rigid body it is at (stored in the body's frame relative to its centre of mass); `AttachClothEdge` does a row. `RemoveClothLink(number)`; numbers are reused lowest first. A link goes with its soft body (when it is destroyed) or its rigid body (at the next substep that finds it dead). A seam wakes its sleeping side when the other is awake.

A **seam** is three scalar equality constraints (one per world axis) with XPBD multipliers zeroed at the start of every substep: `Δ = x_a − x_b` (the difference of the double positions between bodies), `λ = (−Δ_c − α̃ λ_acc)/(w_a + w_b + α̃)` per axis `c` with `α̃ = compliance/h²`, `x_a += w_a λ`, `x_b −= w_b λ`. An **attachment** is the same with the anchor `p(t) + q(t) r` of the rigid body at the end of the substep (the pose interpolated through the rigid step as for contacts), no compliance, the particle's inverse mass shared with the body's generalised inverse mass along each axis, and the reaction applied to the body as for contacts (one impulse at the end of the step plus the virtual displacement). Both are solved after the surface pairs of every pass, in link-number order, in the serial pair task.

## Snapshot

* **Soft section `SOFT`, version 4 while any 3D body has `Fabric`**, else 3 as before (so worlds without fabric keep bytes and hashes); readers accept 1 to 4. Per body, after the broken edges: `bool hasFabric`; if set: `f32 × 3` warp axis, `f32 × 3` weft axis, `f32` warp, weft, shear compliance, `f32` strain limit, `bool` dihedral bend, `f32` drag, `f32` lift slope, `f32` tether strain, `i32` anchor count and `i32` anchors. The reader validates ranges and anchors through `ClothParams.Validate`. Everything else (limit order, tethers, edge classes, rest angles) is rebuilt from the mesh and these.
* **2D `SF2D` version 3 while any body has `Strip`**, else 2; per body, after the broken edges: `bool hasStrip`; if set: `f32 × 2` along axis, `f32` along, across, shear compliance, `f32` strain limit, drag, lift slope.
* **Cloth-link section `CLNK` (id `0x4B4E4C43`), optional**, written only while a link exists, registered after the rail section: `i32` version (1), the handle table, then per live link in slot order `i32 kind` (0 seam, 1 attachment), `i32` soft slot, `i32` particle; seam: `i32` slot B, `i32` particle B, `f32` compliance; attachment: `i32` rigid body, `i32` generation, `f32 × 3` anchor. The reader refuses unknown kinds, particles that do not exist, a body that is not alive, a seam of a particle with itself and a compliance outside 0 to 10^6.
* `StartVertices` is used at creation only and is not saved (the particles' positions are).

The world hash covers all of it, being the snapshot's state in the same order. Fork copies the link tables; the derived arrays are shared (never mutated).

## Debug geometry

`DebugContent.Cloth` (65536) fills `DebugGeometry.ClothLinks` (seams and attachments by link number, then tethers by soft body and particle: both ends in double world coordinates and the tether's longest length) and `DebugGeometry.ClothWind` (per triangle of every aerodynamic cloth, by soft body and triangle: its centre and the aerodynamic force in newtons computed from the state between steps). `DebugContent.All` is 131071.

## Scenes and thresholds (600 steps, own thresholds: 1.5 × the worst of the x64 and arm64 values)

See `scenes.md` → "Fabric". `cloth-flag-3d`, `cloth-character-3d`, `cloth-throw-3d`, `cloth-garment-3d` and `strip-2d` have no reference engine. Triangle crossings of one body are counted every tenth step with a brute-force segment-triangle test, pairs sharing a vertex excluded; the thresholds ask for none.
