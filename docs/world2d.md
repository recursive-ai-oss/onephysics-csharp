# The 2D world

Normative, language-neutral description of `src/OnePhysics/Physics2D/`: what a 2D world does in a step and which choices a port must reproduce to get the same bits. It sits on the shared core (`architecture.md`), the 2D collision code (`collision2d.md`) and the broadphase (`broadphase.md`). All arithmetic is IEEE `+ − × ÷ sqrt` on float32 (positions and broadphase boxes are double); no fused operations.

## Bodies, shapes, mass

* A body's **core position is its centre of mass**; the body origin is `com − R·lc` where `lc` is the local centre (float, stored per body). Shapes keep their authored coordinates in the origin frame. `Body.Position` is the origin.
* Mass data per shape and unit density comes from `collision2d.md`. For a dynamic body: `m = Σ density·area`, `lc = Σ density·area·centroid / m` (double, rounded to float), inertia about the centre of mass `I = Σ density·InertiaAbout(lc)`. Shapes with density 0 add no mass. No mass at all gives mass 1, inertia 1, `lc = 0`. `FixedRotation` gives inverse inertia 0. Adding or removing a shape recomputes this and moves the centre-of-mass position by `R·(lc_new − lc_old)` so the origin stays put.
* `maxExtent` = largest distance from `lc` to any core vertex plus the radius; `minExtent` = smallest of: radius (circle, capsule, segment), smallest `n·(v − centroid)` over polygon edges plus radius.
* Rotation is stored as (cos, sin). Angles in definitions are converted with onemath's correctly rounded `SinCos` (double, rounded to float); `Body.Angle` uses its `Atan2`.
* Bodies created with `StartAsleep` are put to sleep when created.

## Pair table

The world keeps one entry per pair of shapes `(a, b)`, `a < b` by shape slot, whose broadphase boxes overlap; it holds the contact constraint while the shapes touch. Entries get the lowest free index first (free list is a stack, snapshotted).

1. **Proxies.** Every proxy is the shape's tight box in world space (double: `position + float box relative to the centre of mass`), created with the shape. At the start of a step each awake body's proxies are moved with displacement = the body's displacement over the previous step. Margins: 0.1 for moving proxies, 0 for static (broadphase defaults, an option).
2. **New pairs.** `UpdatePairs` returns candidates in its sorted order. A candidate is accepted when the shapes are on different bodies, `CanCollide` holds (group rule, then category/mask), the optional pair predicate agrees, and it is not in the table.
3. **Which pairs update.** A pair is updated when either body is an awake dynamic body, or a kinematic body with non-zero velocity.
4. **Narrowphase**, in parallel over fixed chunks of 64 updated pairs, in ascending pair index: if the two stored boxes no longer overlap the pair is dropped. Otherwise the pose of B in A's frame is built from the origins `com − R·lc` subtracted in double and then converted to float, and `Collide2D.Collide` runs with the speculative distance `4 × 0.005`. Contact arms: `rA = qA·(p − lcA)`, `rB = rA + float(comA − comB)`; normal `qA·n`.
4a. **Recycling.** Before the narrowphase runs for a pair that already has a touching contact constraint, the pair may reuse its previous manifold (see "Manifold recycling"). A recycled pair skips `Collide2D.Collide`, and step 6 writes only the normal, arms, separations and materials.
5. **Apply**, serially in ascending pair index, over the pairs the narrowphase stage listed (a constraint to add or remove, a pair to drop, a sensor pair, or a sleeping body in a touching pair); every other pair is final after step 4 (a recycled manifold is written there, in parallel, unless a material mixer is set): no points → constraint removed (if any) and the pair stays; boxes apart → constraint and pair removed; points → constraint added if needed (kind Contact, flag ReportPersist, bodies and shapes in pair order) or updated. A sleeping body in an existing touching contact with a moving kinematic body is woken. Because constraints exist only while shapes are within the speculative distance, contact begin/end events mean "touching (within 0.02)".
6. **Manifold payload** (layout in `architecture.md`): normal x, y; mixed friction, restitution, rolling resistance; the rolling impulse is kept from the previous manifold; per point the arms, separation, and normal and tangent impulses taken from the old point with the same feature id (0 if none); max normal impulse 0. Whenever a fresh manifold is written, the payload also keeps the recycling frame (below).

## Manifold recycling

A manifold is a set of points fixed to the two bodies, so while the bodies move almost rigidly together the same points are still the right ones and only their separations change. Catto's published notes on Box2D 3.2 describe the idea (a touching contact keeps its manifold until the shapes have moved about 5 cm or turned about 11 degrees relative to each other). Our bound is much tighter, because the looser one failed the mixed-terrain resting penetration threshold: turning about one contact sinks the other vertices by `extent * sin(angle)`.

A pair is recycled when all hold:

* it has a Contact constraint with at least one point, and the payload's owner slot is exactly 1 or 2 (0, which a snapshot taken before recycling existed holds, and any other value mean "never recycle");
* with `d` the change of B's centre of mass relative to A, in A's frame, since the manifold was made, and `(dc, ds)` the cosine and sine of B's rotation relative to A since then: `dc > 0` and `|d| + |ds| * maxExtent(B) <= 0.003` (all float32);
* every recomputed separation `s` satisfies `s <= 0.02` (the speculative distance) and `s >= -0.02`; NaN fails.

The new manifold is the stored body-fixed one moved with the bodies: the normal is rotated by the rotation of the body that owns it, each arm is the stored local arm rotated by its body, and `s = s_made + ((comB + rB) - (comA + rA)) . n` using the new arms (centre-of-mass difference taken in double and then float, as for a fresh manifold). Point count, feature ids, impulses and rolling impulse are untouched, so warm starting continues. The materials are mixed again, so a friction change applies at once.

**Frame stored with each fresh manifold** (payload floats left unused by the 2D layout; no snapshot format change; the payload is snapshotted and hashed, so the frame is too):

| floats | content |
|---|---|
| 2, 7 | normal in the owner's frame |
| 32 | owner: 1 shape A, 2 shape B, 0 none |
| 8, 11 | centre of mass of B in A's frame when made |
| 14, 18 | cosine and sine of B's rotation relative to A when made |
| 20, 33, 34, 35, 36 | point 0: arm on A in A's frame (2), arm on B in B's frame (2), separation when made |
| 37, 38, 39, 23, 26 | point 1, same order |

The owner is the body whose face made the normal: A when the manifold normal equals one of A's face normals in the world (dot product above 0.99999), else B when it is minus one of B's, else 0 (circles, capsule ends, vertex against vertex), which is never recycled. `Engine2D.RecycleManifolds` (default true) turns recycling off, for comparison; it is not part of the snapshot.

Against a fresh narrowphase on large-pile (steps after 300): about 1 % of recycled points differ by more than 2 mm in separation, 0.02 % have a normal more than 3 degrees away (corner contacts where the reference face flips), and 0.6 % have one point fewer (the fresh manifold has a second speculative point).

**Materials.** Default combine rule: friction `sqrt(fA·fB)`, restitution `max`, rolling resistance `max`. `WorldOptions2D.MaterialMixer` replaces it (it sees the first shape's material, the lower slot, then the second). Mixing happens in step 5.

## Step

The core pipeline (`architecture.md`) with these stage bodies; `h` is the substep time, `invH = 1/h` computed once.

* **UpdateContacts**: step 1 above for awake bodies; then every live body gets `start pose = pose`, `Δp = 0`, `Δq = (1, 0)`; then steps 2–5.
* **Prepare** (per active constraint): `hertz = min(ContactHertz, 0.25·invH)`; soft coefficients (below) with `hertz` and with `2·hertz` (used when either body has inverse mass 0), damping ratio `ContactDampingRatio`, over `h`. Per point: effective masses `1/(mA + mB + iA·(rA×n)² + iB·(rB×n)²)` for the normal and likewise for the tangent `t = (ny, −nx)`; adjusted separation `s − (rB − rA)·n`; relative normal velocity from the current velocities; the stored max normal impulse is reset. Rolling mass `1/(iA + iB)`.
* **Soft coefficients** (Catto, soft step): `ω = 2π·hertz` (float constant 6.2831855), `a1 = 2ζ + hω`, `a2 = hω·a1`, `a3 = 1/(1 + a2)`; bias rate `ω/a1`, mass scale `a2·a3`, impulse scale `a3`. Zero hertz gives bias 0, scales 1 and 0.
* **IntegrateVelocities** (dynamic bodies): `v += h·(gravityScale·g + invMass·force)`, `w += h·invI·torque`, each multiplied by `1/(1 + h·damping)`; linear speed clamped to `MaxLinearSpeed` by scaling; `|w|` clamped to `MaxRotationPerSubstep/h`.
* **SolveBatch**: the kernel below. A batch of colored constraints is run as one 8-lane group; the overflow set is run one constraint at a time in list order. The same arithmetic runs at 1, 4 or 8 lanes.
* **IntegratePositions** (awake dynamic and kinematic): `Δp += h·v`, `position += (double)(h·v)`; if `w ≠ 0`, `Δq` and the body rotation each advance by angle `h·w` (`c' = c − a·s`, `s' = s + a·c`, divide both by `sqrt(c'² + s'²)`).
* **FinalizeBodies**: forces and torque of dynamic bodies are cleared; continuous collision (below).
* **BodyMotion**: float.MaxValue if a touching constraint partner is a moving kinematic body, else `|v| + |w|·maxExtent`. Sleeping is the core's.
* **StoreResults**: nothing; impulses live in the payloads.

### Kernel (per point, per lane)

Gathered per constraint: velocities and Δp, Δq of both bodies; normal, friction, restitution, rolling, inverse masses and inertias, soft coefficients; per point arms, adjusted separation, masses, relative velocity, impulses.

* **Warm start**: apply `n·λn + t·λt` at the arms for both points; then rolling impulse as angular impulse on the bodies.
* **Solve** (bias) and **Relax** (no bias), in this order: rolling resistance (limit `rolling · (λn0 + λn1)`); friction of point 0 then 1 (limit `friction·λn`, tangent impulse accumulated); normal of point 0 then 1:
  `s = ((Δp_B − Δp_A + Δq_B·rB − Δq_A·rA)·n) + adjustedSeparation`;
  if `s > 0`: `bias = s·invH`, scales 1 and 0 (speculative: close the gap in one substep);
  else when biased: `bias = max(biasRate·min(s + slop, 0), −PushOutVelocity)` with the constraint's mass and impulse scales; else (relax) `bias = 0`, scales 1 and 0.
  `Δλ = −normalMass·massScale·(vn + bias) − impulseScale·λn`, accumulated impulse clamped at 0, maximum remembered.
* **Restitution**, once after the last substep: for points with relative velocity `≤ −RestitutionThreshold`, max normal impulse > 0 and restitution > 0: `Δλ = −normalMass·(vn + e·relVel)`, accumulated clamp at 0.
* Maximum and minimum are compare-and-select (`max(a, b) = a > b ? a : b`), negation is `0 − x`; the kernel has no divisions and no cross-lane operations. Only dynamic bodies are written.

### Continuous collision

For each awake dynamic body at the end of the step: movement `m = |com_end − com_start| + |sin(Δangle)|·maxExtent`; if `m ≤ 0.5·minExtent` nothing happens. Otherwise each shape's swept box (union of its start and end boxes) is looked up in the static tree; for each static shape that passes the filter, `TimeOfImpact` (target gap 0.005) between the static shape and the mover. For segments and chain segments a hit counts only if the mover's centre approaches the segment line by at least `0.25·minExtent` over the step, and (one-sided chains) started on the solid side. The smallest fraction `t` with `0 < t < 1` wins; the body's centre of mass goes back to `start + t·movement` and its rotation to the normalized lerp. Velocity is unchanged: the next step finds a speculative contact. Bullets (`Body.IsBullet`, `BodyDef.IsBullet`) are described next.

### Bullets

A dynamic body flagged as a bullet skips the per-body pass above and is handled after every other body has finished the step (core hook `IWorldStages.AfterFinalize`, serial, after `FinalizeBodies` and before the store stage), so it sees the final pose of everything that moved. The pass is Catto's continuous collision (GDC 2013): the bullet is swept from its start pose to its end pose against every other shape, and goes back to the earliest time of impact. Bullets never sweep against each other, so each bullet's result depends only on non-bullet bodies and on its own motion; bullets are processed in parallel by index and each writes only its own pose.

1. `m` and the `0.5·minExtent` test are as for any fast body; a bullet that moved less is left alone.
2. Reach: `pad` is the largest, over awake non-bullet bodies, of the centre's displacement during the step plus `|sin(Δangle)|·maxExtent`; the moving trees are queried with each shape's swept box grown by `pad·(1+1e-6) + 1e-4`, static shapes with the plain swept box. Candidates pass the usual filters (sensors never count).
3. For every candidate the time of impact (target gap 0.005) is computed between the bullet's shape moving from its start to its end pose and the candidate with a moving candidate sweeping linearly from its pose at the start of the step to its pose at the end (centre and rotation interpolated); a Failed result counts as a hit; other bullets are skipped, with the segment and chain rules of the static pass. The smallest fraction `t` with `0 < t < 1` wins; equal fractions go to the lower shape slot. The bullet's centre of mass goes back to `start + t·movement`, its rotation to the normalized lerp; its velocity is unchanged and the next step resolves the contact. The other body stays at its end pose, so the two may overlap slightly at the start of the next step; the soft contact pushes them apart.

A non-bullet body that is fast enough can still tunnel through a thin moving body; that is by design and why the flag exists. A body's bullet flag is saved in the body flags.

## Defaults

Substeps 4, solve iterations 1, relax iterations 1, contact hertz 36 (0.15 of the substep rate; the sleep-wake final penetration is chaotic, 0.10 to 0.22 across settings from 30 to 42, and 35 to 38 all measure under 0.15 while the pyramids sink less than at 30), damping ratio 10, push-out 3 m/s, restitution threshold 1 m/s, contact slop 0, max speed 400 m/s, max rotation per substep π/4, sleep: 0.5 s below 0.05 m/s, broadphase margin 0.1. Default gravity (0, −9.81).

## Events and callbacks

Events are the core's; contact begin/end carry the shape handles, in the core's sort order. After a step, `OnCollisionEnter/Stay/Exit` handlers run in that order, first for body A then body B of each event. Persist events are recorded only when the options' event mask includes `ContactPersist` (it also sets the stay callbacks' availability). A handler can destroy bodies: events already delivered are not changed.

## Snapshot section

Section id `0x32445747`, after the broadphase section. Version (u32; 1, 2 or 3, see below); tunables (ContactHertz, ContactDampingRatio, PushOutVelocity, RestitutionThreshold, MaxLinearSpeed, MaxRotationPerSubstep as f32, ContinuousEnabled as u8, ContactSlop f32); body count n, then arrays for all n slots: local centre (2n f32), mass, inertia, max extent, min extent, linear damping, angular damping, gravity scale (n f32 each), force (2n f32), torque (n f32), flags (n u8), shape head, shape tail (n i32), last-step displacement (2n f32); shape count, per slot a live byte and for live slots the shape (kind u8, count i32, radius f32, flags u8, centroid, ghost 1, ghost 2, then 8 vertices and 8 normals as f32 pairs), proxy key i32, friction, restitution, rolling, density (f32), category, mask (u32), group, next shape (i32); pair slot count, per slot a live byte and for live slots shape A, shape B, constraint (i32); free pair count and the free stack (i32). **Section versions.** The version says which optional blocks follow the free pair stack. A world with no joints, no characters and default filter, layer, sensor and hit-threshold state writes version 1 and no block, byte for byte as before any of those features existed. Version 2: the services block only. Version 3: at least one live joint; a flag byte (u8, 1 when a services block follows), the joints block, then the services block when flagged. Version 4: at least one live character; a presence-flags word (u32: bit 0 joints block follows, bit 1 services block follows, bit 2 characters block follows, which is always set), then the flagged blocks in the order joints, services, characters. Later optional blocks take the next bits of the version 4 word, so no further version is needed for them. The writer picks the lowest version that can hold the world (characters, else joints, else services, else none), so every state has exactly one encoding. A reader accepts 1 to 4 and rejects with `SnapshotFormatException` any other version, a version 3 with no joint records or a flag other than 0 or 1, a version 4 whose flags word has bits beyond 2 or lacks bit 2, a joints block with no joints, a services block holding only default state, a characters block with no characters (the writer would not have written any of these), and every truncation, with the world left unchanged. **Services block** (versions 2, 3 and 4): the 32 layer-matrix rows (u32), the hit speed threshold (f32), the filter dirty flag (u8), and for every shape slot a flags byte (bit 0 sensor) and a layer byte (both 0 for dead slots). The world options a snapshot restores are the core's plus these tunables; `BroadphaseMargin`, thread count and world id are construction-time. Delegates (material mixer, pair predicate, collision handlers) are never saved.

## Layers and filters

Whether two shapes may collide is decided in this order; the first rule that answers decides.

1. Two sensor shapes never collide.
2. **Layer matrix.** Each shape has a layer 0..31 (`ShapeDef.Layer`, default 0). `LayerMatrix[a]` is a 32-bit row whose bit b says that layer a may collide with layer b; setting a pair sets both rows; default all ones.
3. **Group.** Shapes with the same non-zero group index collide iff the group is positive.
4. **Category and mask.** Both `(mask_a & category_b)` and `(category_a & mask_b)` must be non-zero.

Rules 1-4 are the pure part (`CanCollide`, safe in the parallel pair search). After them, and only for pairs the pure part accepts: the optional `WorldOptions2D.PairFilter` delegate, then the optional struct `IContactFilter` installed with `SetContactFilter`, each called serially with the shapes in slot order.

**Re-filtering.** The engine keeps a *filter dirty* flag. It is set by changing a shape's layer, category, mask or group, by changing the layer matrix, by installing or clearing the struct filter, by `RefilterPairs()`, and when `Options.PairFilter` is a different delegate object than at the previous step (compared by reference at the start of the contact stage; the first step compares with null). At the start of the next contact stage, if the flag is set (and the flag cleared): every live pair in the table is evaluated again in ascending pair index; a pair that is no longer allowed has its constraint removed (a contact end or sensor exit event, islands woken as any removal does) and leaves the table; then every proxy is touched in the broadphase so the step's pair search reports every overlapping pair, and newly allowed pairs enter the table in the usual sorted order. Delegates and filters are never saved: after a restore the host installs them again and calls `RefilterPairs()` if they now answer differently. The flag itself is saved.

Changing a body's `Layer` changes all its shapes.

## Sensors

A shape created with `ShapeDef.IsSensor` produces no contact response, contributes no mass or inertia, and is ignored by continuous collision. A pair of shapes on different bodies with at least one sensor (and passing the filters) is in the pair table like any pair, but instead of a contact constraint it has a **sensor constraint** (kind Sensor: never solved, colored or linked into islands, never wakes anything). The narrow phase is the contact manifold; the shapes **overlap** when a manifold point has separation at most 0. The constraint exists exactly while they overlap: it is added when they begin to overlap (sensor enter event) and removed when they stop, when the pair's boxes stop overlapping, or when either shape or body is destroyed (sensor exit event). A sensor pair is evaluated when either body is awake and moving (the contact rule), and additionally once in the step in which the pair is first found, so a sleeping body already inside a sensor gets its enter event. Sleeping and waking never end an overlap. Destroying a body or shape queues its exit events; they are delivered at the end of the next step.

## Events

Kinds and order are the core's (`architecture.md`) with one addition: kind 8, **ContactHit**, sorted after Wake, enabled by `EventMask.ContactHit` (not in `EventMask.Default`; the snapshot accepts mask bits up to 0x1FF). `Value` is the approach speed. It is emitted in the store stage, per active contact: the largest of `-relativeNormalVelocity` over manifold points (the velocity measured when the step was prepared) among points whose accumulated maximum normal impulse is positive; the event fires when that exceeds `WorldOptions2D.HitSpeedThreshold` (default 1 m/s, at least 0).

After the step, handlers on bodies run over the delivered list in order: contact begin, persist, end and hit call `OnCollisionEnter`, `OnCollisionStay`, `OnCollisionExit`, `OnCollisionHit` first on body A then on body B (normal flipped for B); sensor enter and exit call `OnTriggerEnter` / `OnTriggerExit` the same way, with `Trigger.SelfIsSensor` telling which shape is the sensor; sleep and wake call `OnSleep` / `OnWake` on the body. Collision data is read from the contact as it is at the end of the step: points are the manifold points (world position of the anchor on A's centre of mass plus the arm), `Separation`, and `NormalImpulse` / `TangentImpulse` equal the stored accumulated impulse of the last substep times the number of substeps (an estimate of the impulse over the step). A contact that has ended, or began and ended inside the step, has no points. Polled form (middle tier): `Events` (all), `GetEvents(kind)` (one kind's slice), `GetCollision(event)` and `GetTrigger(event)`.

## Queries

Queries read the world and change nothing (a world that was queried hashes the same as one that was not). All numbers: positions are double, within +-2^22 of the world origin; directions, lengths, sizes and radii are float.

* **Frame.** For each candidate shape the query is moved into the shape's body frame like the contact stage does: body origin = `com - R*lc` in double, `(float)(point - origin)` rotated by the inverse body rotation; results are rotated back and added to the origin in double. A ray's translation is the unit direction (float `sqrt` and `/`) times the length; length infinity or above 2^23 becomes 2^23.
* **Narrow phase.** Ray: `Shape2D.RayCast` (origin inside a convex shape: hit at fraction 0, zero normal). Shape cast: `Distance2D.ShapeCast` of the query shape against the target. Overlap: `Distance2D.Compute` with radii, overlapping when the distance is at most 0. Closest point: `Distance2D.Compute` against a point.
* **Broadphase.** Static proxies are tested against the exact box (grown by 1e-4); kinematic and dynamic proxies against the box grown by 1e-4 plus a *pad*: the largest amount by which any non-static shape's current tight box exceeds its stored fat box (proxies are refreshed at the start of a step, so between steps a moving body may be ahead of its box). The pad is computed lazily once per step (and after a restore or origin shift) and never changes state. Rays and casts use the tree's ray traversal clipped to the worst kept hit; overlaps use the box query; the closest point searches a box that doubles from 1 until it holds a hit within its half width or reaches the maximum distance.
* **Order.** Every hit has a key: fraction for rays and casts, distance for closest point, the centre distance for the closest form of an overlap, the shape slot for the all form of an overlap. Results sort by (key, shape slot). Closest forms keep one; all forms keep the best N for a buffer of N; any forms stop at the first accepted shape (which one is unspecified).
* **Filter.** A shape is a candidate if it is not a sensor (unless `IncludeSensors`), its layer is in `LayerMask`, `(category & MaskBits) != 0` and `(CategoryBits & mask) != 0`, and the optional predicate accepts it; evaluated in that order, before the narrow phase.
* **Errors.** Non-finite or out-of-range origin, positions, directions, a zero direction, a non-positive or NaN length, a negative or NaN radius, a non-positive box size or capsule radius, an empty `Shape2D`: `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` or `ArgumentException` naming the argument. Nothing is changed.
* **Scratch.** Results are collected in per-thread scratch (allocated once per thread), so queries with caller buffers allocate nothing in steady state and may run concurrently on several threads while the world is not being modified. A filter predicate that queries again gets fresh scratch.
* **Batches** (`PhysicsQuery2D`): inputs are validated first; then fixed chunks of 64 queries run on the world's executor, each writing its own output slots; results do not depend on the thread count.

## Character mover

A character is an upright capsule (axis along +y, centred on the body origin) on a kinematic body with a fixed rotation. `Move(displacement, dt)` moves it by collide-and-slide and then sets the body's velocity to the realized displacement divided by `dt` and its pose to the new position, so dynamic bodies see it as a kinematic body in motion. Everything below is deterministic: candidates are sorted by shape slot, ties go to the lower slot, and the move reads the world as it stands at the call (poses of the last step), so calls made in character-slot order give the same result on any thread count.

**Definition** (`CharacterDef`): radius (0.3), height (1.8, end to end), slope limit in degrees (45), step offset (0.3), skin (0.01), layer mask for what the mover collides with (all), push strength (0 = never pushes), and the layer, category and mask bits of the capsule's own shape. Up is +y. Limits: the displacement per call at most 1e5 long, positions within ±2^21, `dt` positive; violations throw `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` naming the value.

**Planes.** For every shape within range of the capsule (sensors, the character's own body and shapes outside the layer mask are skipped) the mover computes the manifold of capsule against shape out to `2·skin`, and keeps one plane per shape: the contact normal and the smallest separation. A plane whose normal is within the slope limit (`n.y ≥ cos(slopeLimit)`) is walkable ground and keeps no skin; every other plane holds `skin` of clearance (its separation is reduced by `skin`). Another character's body is never walkable and is never pushed out of (see below). A plane's push limit is the most the solver may move the capsule to undo an overlap: `0.5·radius` per move for static and kinematic shapes, 0 for dynamic bodies and other characters. A character spawned inside geometry, or squeezed by a moving platform, is therefore eased out over several moves instead of being thrown across a thin wall.

**Plane solver** (`Mover2D.SolvePlanes`, after the published write-ups on character movers): find the vector closest to the wanted movement `t` that does not move into any plane further than that plane's separation allows, i.e. `n·v ≥ −separation` for every plane, where a plane that is overlapped (negative separation) asks for a push-out of at most its push limit. Solved as a projection onto the intersection of half-spaces by Dykstra's alternating projection in plane order (at most 50 sweeps, tolerance 1e-5), starting from the target; an empty feasible set (a notch narrower than the capsule) returns the zero vector. `ClipVelocity` clips a velocity against the planes the same way (planes closer than a contact distance only). Each plane that ended up active records how much it pushed; a plane with a positive push is a hit.

**Move.** `ride`: when the character was grounded, the ground body's velocity at the character's position times `dt` is added to the displacement (a platform carries its passenger). The displacement is split into the horizontal part and the vertical part, and the horizontal part is applied first.
- *Slide* (`MvSlide`): up to five rounds of gather planes, solve for the clipped movement, shape-cast the capsule along it (the same shape cast as the queries; a shape already touching and not approached by at least 1% of the cast is left to the planes), move to the first hit, and continue with the rest of the movement.
- *Step up*: if the horizontal slide met a wall and made less progress than asked, and the step offset is above 0, the mover tries: raise by the step offset (a ceiling stops the raise early), slide forward the wanted distance, drop. The attempt succeeds only if it made more progress than the plain slide, the drop lands on walkable ground (not a character) and the landing is at most `stepOffset + 1e-3` above where the move began. A short move may leave the capsule's round bottom behind the edge of the step, so a second attempt moves `radius + skin` forward. Otherwise the plain slide stands.
- *Vertical*: upward movement is a plain slide. Downward movement first resolves overlaps and checks whether the character already rests on walkable ground (separation ≤ 1e-3), in which case the downward movement is absorbed (no sinking, no sliding down a gentle slope). If not, and the character was grounded last move and the caller is not moving up, it casts down by `max(step offset, movement)` and snaps to walkable ground (down stairs and slopes); steeper surfaces are slid along with the remaining distance.
- *Ground probe*: a settle pass pushes out of remaining overlaps; the character is grounded when a walkable plane (not another character) has separation ≤ `2·skin` after the settle; the ground normal is the walkable plane with the largest `n.y`, ties to the lower shape slot. `CharacterContact` flags: Sides (a wall plane pushed), Above (a ceiling plane pushed), Below (grounded).
- *Pushes*: with push strength above 0, for every dynamic body the character pushed (a hit whose normal is not walkable ground) the wanted speed along the push direction is `displacement·(−n)/dt`; the body is brought up to it: an impulse `mass·(want − have)·pushStrength` at the contact point along `−n` if `want > have`, else nothing.
- Hits are recorded once per shape, in plane order, and the `OnControllerColliderHit` handlers run inside `Move`, after it finished.

**Characters and each other.** Another character is an obstacle for the cast and the planes, so two characters walking toward each other stop at `2·radius`; it is never walkable (a character cannot stand on, or be carried by, another) and never pushed out of (two characters that overlap stay where they are instead of shoving each other: with push-out between them, a crowd on one layer crept at tens of metres per second). Overlapped characters can still leave by moving apart. To let characters pass through each other, give them a layer that ignores itself (`IgnoreLayerCollision`) or a layer mask without it.

**State and snapshots.** Per character: body slot and generation, the definition, grounded flag, ground normal, ground body (slot and generation), realized velocity. They are saved in the characters block (below) and covered by the world hash; the registry (a handle table) is saved first. Destroying the body destroys its character. Handlers, like all delegates, are not saved.

**Characters block** (section version 4, last block): the handle table of characters (as the core writes handle tables), then per live character in slot order: body index (i32), body generation (i32), radius, height, slope limit, step offset, skin (f32), layer mask (u32), push strength (f32), layer (i32), category, mask (u32), grounded (u8), ground normal x, y (f32), ground body index, generation (i32; −1 and 0 when none), velocity x, y (f32). Restore validates the definition like a new one, requires every character's body to be live, of the named generation and kinematic, and finite state.

## Debug geometry

`GetDebugGeometry(content, buffer)` clears and fills a `DebugGeometry` (plain structs in `RecursiveAI.OnePhysics.Debug`, shared by the 2D and 3D worlds; z is 0 in 2D). Content flags: Shapes (1), Bounds (2), BroadphaseBounds (4), Contacts (8), Bodies (16), Joints (32), Characters (64). Orders: shapes, bounds and broadphase bounds by shape slot; contacts by constraint index then manifold point; bodies by body slot. A shape is world-space points (circle: centre; capsule: the two axis ends; polygon: the vertices in stored order; segment and chain segment: two points) plus the rounding radius; points are `origin + (double)(R * v)`. Bounds are the shape's tight box, broadphase bounds the stored fat box. A contact point is the world position of the anchor on A's centre of mass plus the arm, with the normal from A to B, the separation and the stored normal impulse of the last substep. A body entry holds its type and sleep state, origin, centre of mass and island id (-1 for static and kinematic bodies). A joint entry (by constraint slot) holds the joint handle, both bodies, the anchors in world coordinates (`origin + (double)(R * local anchor)`; a mouse joint reports its target as anchor A) and `Axis`, the world unit axis of a prismatic or wheel joint (zero for the others). A character entry (by character slot) holds the character's body, the capsule centre (the body position), radius and height, the grounded flag, the unit ground normal (zero when not grounded) and the ground body (default handle when not grounded); the capsule itself is also a shape entry of its kinematic body.

## Bodies leaving the supported range

Before any change, `Step` checks every non-static body's shape boxes, stretched the way the broadphase would store them (margin plus twice the last step's displacement). A box that is not finite or reaches beyond 2^22 in either axis raises `BodyOutOfBoundsException` (a `PhysicsException`) naming the body handle and its position, lowest body slot first. Nothing has been changed when it is raised, so the world hash is the same before and after, and every later `Step` raises it again until the host moves the body back, destroys it (`PhysicsWorld2D.GetBody(handle).Destroy()`) or shifts the origin. `Restore` applies the same test to every body (static included) and to the restored tunables, and rejects the snapshot with `SnapshotFormatException`, leaving the world unchanged. A snapshot may declare at most 2^22 bodies and 2^22 shapes, and the body count must fit in the bytes that remain.

## Origin shifting

After the core shifts positions, every shape's proxy is destroyed and re-created in ascending shape slot order; the pair table is unchanged. The next step reports all overlapping pairs again, of which the existing ones are ignored.

## Joints

Normative description of `src/OnePhysics/Physics2D/Joints/`. A joint is a core constraint of kind Joint between two different bodies, at least one dynamic; it takes a color, links islands, wakes and merges them like a contact, and is solved in the same batches. All arithmetic is float32 `+ − × ÷ sqrt`, except the few calls named below, which are onemath's correctly rounded double functions. Joints are solved scalar (no lanes): a batch holds at most eight constraints of one color, which share no dynamic body, so the order inside a batch cannot matter.

### Parameters and state

* **Record** per joint, kept by constraint slot, saved in the snapshot: type (u8, 1 revolute … 7 mouse), flags (bit 0 limit, 1 motor, 2 spring, 3 collide-connected), local anchors A and B (4 × f32, measured from the body **origins**, converted to centre-of-mass offsets every step so later mass changes cannot move a joint), local unit axis A (2), reference angle (1), lower and upper limit (2), motor speed, maximum motor force or torque, maximum motor torque of a motor joint (3), spring hertz and damping ratio, second hertz and damping ratio (weld angular) (4), rest length, motor-joint offset x, y and angle, motor-joint correction factor (5), mouse target (2 × f64), break force and break torque (2 × f32; infinity never breaks). Field use per type: revolute: limits are angles (radians, within −π..π, lower ≤ upper), motor torque in the first maximum, spring on the angle; prismatic: limits in metres along the axis, motor force, spring along the axis; weld: hertz and damping are the linear spring, the second pair the angular spring (0 hertz = rigid); wheel: axis, limits along it, spring along it, the motor is angular (torque); distance: rest length (≥ 0.005), limits are the minimum and maximum length (minimum ≥ 0, maximum ≥ minimum), spring along the joint, motor along it; motor: offsets and maximum force and torque; mouse: anchor on B, target, hertz, damping ratio, maximum force.
* **Validation.** Every float is finite (break thresholds may be infinity, not NaN or negative; a distance maximum may be large); anchors and offsets within ±2^21; hertz, damping, maxima ≥ 0; correction factor within 0..1; the mouse target finite within ±2^21; an axis must have a length above 1e-12 and is normalized once at creation (`x / sqrt(x² + y²)`). Same body twice, two non-dynamic bodies, lower above upper, and a zero axis raise `InvalidJointException`; non-finite or out-of-range numbers raise `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` naming the field; dead, foreign or default bodies raise the handle exceptions. Nothing changes when a definition is refused; edits through a `Joint` revalidate the whole record first.
* **Reference rotation.** `(refC, refS)` is `(cos, sin)` of the reference angle, computed with onemath's `SinCos` in double and rounded to float; a motor joint uses its angular offset instead.
* **Payload** (accumulated impulses, one slot per row, the core's float payload): 0 and 1: the point impulse (revolute, weld, motor, mouse), or the perpendicular row and the angle row (prismatic), or the perpendicular row (wheel), or the rigid length row (distance, slot 0); 2: the angular row (weld, motor joint); 3: spring; 4: motor (the wheel's angular motor too); 5: lower limit; 6: upper limit. They are the impulses of one substep and persist as the warm start. Turning a limit, motor or spring off through the API zeroes its slot.
* **Rule order for a pair of shapes.** (1) different bodies; (2) the layer matrix, then group, then category and mask bits, and sensor-versus-sensor (`CanCollide`); (3) no joint without collide-connected between the two bodies; (4) the pair predicate; (5) the contact filter. A collide-connected joint therefore leaves the decision to (2), (4) and (5): it never forces a collision the filters refuse. Refiltering after a filter change uses the same order, so lifting a layer ignore never defeats a plain joint.
* **Collision.** Unless collide-connected is set, shapes of the two bodies never form a pair: the broadphase filter rejects a candidate pair when a joint without the flag joins their bodies (it walks the dynamic body's constraint list). Creating such a joint removes the existing contacts and pair entries between the two bodies' shapes (contact end events as usual). When such a joint disappears (break, destroyed joint or body) the engine `Touch`es every proxy of the surviving bodies so the next step finds their overlaps again. This reconciliation runs at the end of every step, when a joint or a body is destroyed through the world, and at the start of a step (for removals through the raw tier), scanning constraint slots in ascending order, so a snapshot never holds a pending effect.

### Step

* **Prepare** (per active joint, with the contact preparation; `h` the substep, `invH = 1/h`): the anchors `rA = qA·(localAnchorA − lcA)`, `rB` likewise, at the start-of-step rotations; `d0 = (float)(comB − comA)` from the double centres; inverse masses and inertias; the axis `qA·axis`; the relative rotation of B in A's frame with the reference removed, `conj(qA)·qB·conj(ref)`; the reciprocal of the sum of inverse inertias (0 if 0); `maxMotorImpulse = h·maxMotor` and `h·maxTorque`; and
  * **rigid-row softness** with `MakeSoft` (above) at `hertz = min(JointHertz, 0.25·invH)`, damping ratio `JointDampingRatio` (defaults 60 and 2);
  * **spring coefficients** `(stiffness, massScale, impulseScale)` for each user spring (`MakeSpring`): zero hertz gives none. Otherwise `wh = min(2π·hertz·h, π)`, `zh = ζ·wh`, determinant `D = exp(−2·zh)`, trace `T = 2·exp(−zh)·cos(wh·sqrt(1 − ζ²))` for ζ < 1 and `exp(x − zh) + exp(−x − zh)` with `x = wh·sqrt(ζ² − 1)` otherwise (doubles; onemath `Exp`, `Cos`), `massScale = 1 − D`, `impulseScale = D`, `stiffness = (2 − massScale − T)/h`, all rounded to float. Reason: the soft-contact coefficients are implicit Euler and add damping of about `ω·h` to a spring (measured: 4 Hz at ζ = 0.05 decayed like ζ = 0.15); these coefficients make the one-substep map `v' = (1 − massScale)·v − stiffness·x`, `x' = x + h·v'` have exactly the eigenvalues of the continuous oscillator, so frequency and damping ratio come out as asked (checked within 2%);
  * for a mouse joint the target relative to B's centre, `(float)(target − comB)`; for a motor joint the offset rotated by A's rotation.
* **Per substep** the joint phases are called with the contacts': **WarmStart** applies the stored impulses (anchors, axes and normals rebuilt from the accumulated body motion Δp, Δq as for contacts: `r' = Δq·r`, `d' = d0 + Δp_B − Δp_A`, relative rotation `ΔqB·conj(ΔqA)·rel0`, `rel0` being the start-of-step relative rotation with the reference removed); **Solve** (with bias) and **Relax** (without) solve the rows below in their order, the rigid rows last so they win; **Restitution** does nothing for joints.
* **Two angles.** `ExactAngle` = `(float)atan2(s, c)` with onemath's double `Atan2` of the current relative rotation, used where the value matters (limits, an angular spring). `SmallAngle` = `2s/(1 + c)` with `1 + c` raised to at least 1e-4 and the result clamped to ±π: `2·tan(θ/2)`, equal to θ to third order, with no transcendental call, used by rigid angle rows and by the motor joint (whose reference rotation is the offset, so the value is the error).
* **Rows.** `Cdot` is the relative velocity along the row; "apply" adds the impulse to both bodies at the current anchors.
  * *Soft rigid row:* `bias = useBias ? rateJ·C : 0`, `ms = useBias ? massScaleJ : 1`, `is = useBias ? impulseScaleJ : 0`; `Δλ = −m·ms·(Cdot + bias) − is·λ`. Two-row blocks use `K⁻¹` of the 2×2 effective-mass matrix (`det` inverted once, 0 when singular) in place of `m`.
  * *Spring row* (only in the biased pass, once per substep): `Δλ = −m·(massScale·Cdot + stiffness·C) − impulseScale·λ`, accumulated without clamping.
  * *Motor row:* `Δλ = −m·(Cdot − speed)`, accumulated impulse clamped to ±`maxMotorImpulse`.
  * *Limit rows* (lower: `C = x − lower`, `Cdot` as is; upper: `C = upper − x`, `Cdot` negated, impulse applied reversed): speculative when `C > 0` (`bias = C·invH`, `ms = 1`, `is = 0`), soft rigid coefficients when `C ≤ 0` and biased, none in the relax pass; accumulated impulse clamped at 0.
  * Order: **revolute**: spring (on `ExactAngle`), motor, limits (on `ExactAngle`), then the 2×2 point block `C = d' + rB' − rA'`. **Prismatic**: spring, motor, limits along the axis (`C` = translation of B's anchor from A's along `axis'`), then a 2×2 block of the perpendicular row (`C = perp'·(d' + rB' − rA')`) and the angle row (`SmallAngle`, `K22 = iA + iB`, 1 if 0). **Wheel**: motor (angular, `maxTorque`), spring and limits along the axis, then the perpendicular row alone. **Weld**: angle, then point; each part is a rigid row (soft-rigid coefficients, both passes) when its hertz is 0 and a spring row (biased pass only) otherwise. **Distance**: with the spring on and `min < max`: spring (when hertz > 0) and motor along the joint, otherwise the rigid length row `C = |d| − length`; then limits on `|d|` when enabled and `min < max`; the axis is `d/|d|`, zero when `|d| ≤ 0.005`. **Motor joint**: angle then point, both velocity targets with a pose-error term `correction·invH·error` in both passes, `error` = `SmallAngle` and `d' + rB' − rA' − Δq_A·offset`; the angular impulse is clamped to ±`h·maxTorque`, the linear impulse vector to a length of `h·maxForce` (scaled down). **Mouse**: only B moves; a spring on `C = Δp_B + rB' − target` with the 2×2 mass of B alone, the accumulated impulse vector clamped to a length of `h·maxForce`, biased pass only.
* **Mixed batches.** A batch with joints and contacts solves the joints one by one and passes the contacts, in batch order, to the lane kernel (any number of lanes ≤ 8 gives the same bits); the overflow set is solved one constraint at a time in list order. A batch without joints is exactly as before.

### Reaction and breaking

After the last substep each active joint's reaction is computed from its payload: linear impulse `l` (revolute, weld, motor, mouse: the vector of slots 0 and 1; prismatic and wheel: `(slot 0, axial)`; distance: `slot 0 + axial`), where `axial = spring + motor + lower − upper`; torque (revolute: `axial`; weld, motor joint: slot 2; prismatic: slot 1; wheel: motor; others 0). Force = `sqrt(lx² + ly²)·invH`, torque = `|torque|·invH`. A joint whose force exceeds its break force, or else whose torque exceeds its break torque, sets `BreakRequested` with that value; the core removes it after the step in ascending constraint order and queues one JointBreak event (joint handle, bodies, value), which sorts by the core's rules. A joint breaks once, because it is gone afterwards. `Joint.OnJointBreak` handlers run after the step in the same loop as the body callbacks, at the break event's place in the sorted event order (after contact and sensor events, before sleep and wake), once per joint. Reaction readouts are not state: they are not saved and read 0 after a restore until the next step.

### Snapshot

Joints block (section version 3, see "Section versions" above; after the flag byte):  `JointHertz`, `JointDampingRatio` (f32), the number of constraint slots `n` (i32) and per slot a live byte (u8) and, if live, the 110-byte record in the order listed above (type, flags, anchors, axis, reference angle, lower, upper, motor speed, maximum motor, maximum motor 2, hertz, damping, hertz 2, damping 2, length, offset x, y, angle, correction, target x, y (f64), break force, break torque). Restore validates every record like a definition (axis within 1e-3 of unit length) and requires records exactly for the live joint constraints; the reference rotation is recomputed. The hash covers it. The two joint options are saved only here; a world without joints keeps the defaults.

### Defaults and API

`JointHertz` 60, `JointDampingRatio` 2 in `WorldOptions2D`. Seven definition types (`RevoluteJointDef` … `MouseJointDef`) with documented defaults feed `CreateJoint`; `AddHinge`, `AddSlider`, `AddFixed`, `AddSpring`, `AddDistance`, `AddWheel`, `AddTarget` (a mouse joint; the first body is any other body, usually static) and `AddRelative` (a motor joint holding the current relative pose) are the one-liners; they take world anchors and build the local ones from the bodies' current poses (`ToLocal`: double difference to the body origin, then rotated into the body frame), and the reference angle from the current relative rotation with onemath's `Atan2`. A `Joint` is a (world, constraint slot, generation) struct; stale and default handles throw before anything else.

## Core additions this relies on

`IWorldStages.FinalizeBodies` (default no-op, called over chunks of awake bodies after the restitution pass and before `StoreResults`) and `WorldCore.Sleep(BodyHandle)`. Neither changes snapshot format version 1. `IWorldStages.AfterFinalize` (default no-op) runs once, serially, after `FinalizeBodies` and before `StoreResults`; bullets use it.

## Performance

None of this changes how a body moves: the poses of every 2D scene equal those of the previous solver bit for bit, and each lane of a wide kernel computes what its scalar twin computes. The world hash does change, because the broadphase tree (part of the hash) is now maintained by enlarging and rebuilding instead of reinserting.

- **Solver layout.** Per step the contacts of each batch are copied into groups of eight lanes (36 fields of eight floats), and body velocity and the motion accumulated in the step live in 8-float rows indexed by body (row 0 is all zeros and is what unused lanes read). A kernel gathers the rows of its lanes with a transpose, does the soft-step arithmetic in the scalar twin's order, and scatters velocities only to dynamic bodies. Group size is fixed at eight lanes whatever the kernel width, so batches and colors never depend on the width. Impulses are copied back into the payloads after the last substep.
- **Joints.** Revolute joints with no spring, motor or limit and no stored limit impulse use a wide kernel over the same groups (anchors, masses and soft coefficients come from the scalar preparation); every other joint, and every joint in the overflow set, stays scalar. Joints have no restitution pass.
- **Joint preparation.** A plain revolute joint is prepared straight into its wide group (anchors turned by the body rotations, masses, position difference); the soft coefficients are computed once per batch. Only the other joints build the scalar scratch row.
- **Pair finding.** Tight boxes and the test against the stored fat box run in parallel; the tree update and the pair merge run serially in body order, and pairs are sorted, so tree shape never reaches a result. The pair table is open addressing with no allocation. A pair whose boxes are further apart than the speculative distance plus 0.005 skips the narrowphase. Manifold writes run in parallel unless a material mixer is set. A moved proxy enlarges the boxes of its ancestors instead of being reinserted (`docs/broadphase.md`). The separating-axis search stops at the first face that already separates the shapes by more than the radii plus the speculative distance, which is exactly the case in which the manifold is empty.
- **Checks.** The bounds test before a step skips dynamic bodies that are far inside the range (position, extent, last displacement and margin all below 2^19, so the stretched box stays below the 2^22 limit); sleep detection looks at a body's contact partners only while some kinematic body moves.
- **Unchecked access.** The contact kernel reads and writes its group fields and the body rows through `Mem` (`Physics2D/Mem.cs`), the one place without bounds checks; offsets are in range by construction (field index below 36 plus a lane offset below 8, body rows written by the preparation), and the `Checked` configuration checks every access. Measured on large-pile and pyramid-100 at one thread: about 3 % of the step.
- **Threads.** One shared pool of worker threads spins briefly, then sleeps; a call falls back to running inline when the pool is busy or on the browser. Work is split by index and combined in index order. The substeps of the solver run as one staged call (`TaskExecutor.ForStages`): the threads stay inside it from the first integration to the restitution pass and cross the stage boundaries (integrate, then each color of each pass) by polling counters. Each thread owns one contiguous block of every stage and runs it first, then helps with what is left of the other blocks, so a constraint group is solved on the same core in every pass. A staged call whose stages all have fewer than four tasks runs on the calling thread. Tasks of a stage touch disjoint data, so which thread runs a task never matters.
- **Where the time goes** (ms per step, the previous merge to now, medians of per-step minima over three interleaved runs, one thread, a shared 32-core machine at load 5; the staged solve line is all substeps, passes and integrations together):

  | stage | large-pile before | after | joint-grid before | after |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | contact update | 3.97 | 2.16 | 2.99 | 1.16 |
  | of which tight boxes | 0.27 | 0.26 | 0.58 | 0.30 |
  | of which proxy moves | 0.79 | 0.06 | 0.76 | 0.06 |
  | of which pair finding | 0.32 | 0.39 | 0.50 | 0.43 |
  | of which manifolds | 1.77 | 1.15 | 0.56 | 0.15 |
  | prepare | 0.37 | 0.15 | 4.08 | 0.86 |
  | solver, all passes | 2.36 | 1.37 | 3.77 | 2.47 |

  The "before" column was taken with the same instrumentation on the previous merge on a busier machine, so read it as a ratio, not as exact milliseconds. At eight threads the solver region of large-pile went from about 1.14 ms (separate fork and join per color) to about 0.86 ms, and joint-grid's step from 3.1 to 1.7 ms.
- **JIT-shaped code.** Three rules found by reading the generated code, each worth 5 to 30 %: (1) a struct with more than four fields (a group of lane vectors) stays in memory, so the kernels keep their vectors in plain locals; (2) the JIT's inlining budget is per method, and a method with a few hundred lane operations runs out, leaving the operators as calls, so the friction and the non-penetration passes are methods of their own, and the warm start, the solve and the restitution pass are separate methods, as are the joint kernel's; (3) writing scalars into a struct and then copying the whole struct with wide loads stalls on store forwarding, so the narrowphase fills its polygons in place (`Poly.Load`, `Poly.LoadTransformed`) and handles two discs without building polygons at all. Solved velocities go back with a transpose and one 16-byte store per body (slot 3 of a body row is padding), a batch remembers on its first constraint whether it holds only contacts or only wide revolute joints (`Engine2D.BatchSimple`), and a batch with no contacts does not clear its contact group.
- **Tiered compilation.** With the default tiered JIT a short scene of a small world finishes before its hot methods leave the unoptimised first tier (chain 0.47 ms per step against 0.03 ms optimised, bullets 1.9 against 0.24, pyramid-20 0.034 against 0.009), and large scenes lose about 5 %. The bar project therefore runs with tiered compilation off, which is also what a host sees after warm-up; an application that cares about its first frames can do the same in its runtime configuration.
- **Narrowphase, second pass.** The separating-axis search takes the other core's points four at a time as x and y lanes (`Collide2D.FindMaxSeparation`; polygons of three or more points, a point or segment keeps the two-dot loop): every lane computes `nx * (ox - vx) + ny * (oy - vy)`, lanes past the point count are masked out with compare and select, and the minimum of finite values does not depend on the order it is taken in, so the manifolds are bit for bit the scalar ones (checked over 10 shape pairs of 4096 random poses). The face clipper no longer recomputes the reference face's separation: it is the value the search just returned. Searching the other core's face normals with lanes was measured and was slower (the branchy scalar loop is cheap for four or five faces), so it stays scalar. Shape bounds are a branch-free running minimum of (x, y, -x, -y).
- **Pair finding.** The broadphase filter drops a pair that already has a table entry before it is sorted with the new pairs (the entry was going to make the serial loop skip it anyway): tumbler 11 % faster. Shape moves are collected per box task and applied in task order, the start poses and the update list are built in parallel blocks (each block writes its own slots; a serial pass closes the gaps in block order), and so are the awake list and the active-constraint lookup of the coloring. None of this changes an order or a result.
- **Unchecked writes.** The manifold write and the group preparation go through `Mem` as well; the `Checked` configuration now checks every `Mem.At`.
- **Participants.** A staged call takes only as many threads as the stages can keep busy (two tasks per thread in the average stage), and a parallel call wakes no more workers than it has extra tasks. Which thread runs a task never reaches a result, so this only changes speed. At 32 threads on a 32-core machine every thread crossing every stage boundary costs more than the extra thread brings, which is why both references are slower at 32 threads than at 8.
- **Where the time goes now** (ms per step, one thread, load about 12, so read ratios): large-pile contact update 1.77 to 1.67 (manifolds 0.96 to 0.87, tight boxes 0.15 to 0.12), solver 0.83 unchanged; step time of large-pile 3.1 to 2.8, tumbler 2.5 to 2.2, mixed-terrain 1.12 to 1.04, sleep-wake 2.24 to 2.08 (interleaved runs, medians).
- **What is left.** Large-pile, pyramid-100, tumbler, sleep-wake and mixed-terrain are about 3 to 4 times Box2D v3 at one thread, joint-grid and chain are level with it. The narrowphase costs about 50 ns per pair beyond the shape code (reading the proxies, bodies and shapes of a pair touches about ten cache lines), the separating-axis search and the face clipper are still 20 to 25 % of a large-pile step, the contact kernel is about 8 ns per constraint pass, and at eight threads the serial parts are the tree update, the pair merge, the pair apply loop and island sleep, plus about a hundred stage boundaries per step in the solver.

- **Measured and not kept.** A sampled profile of large-pile at one thread (share of all samples, self time): the pair glue in `Engine2D.Collide` 9.6 %, the face clipper 6.9 %, the separating-axis search 5.5 %, the contact kernel passes about 19 % together, batch preparation 5.6 %, manifold writes 5.4 %, tree queries about 6 %. Stage times per step (ms, one thread, a loaded machine, so ratios only): manifolds 1.5, solver passes 1.3, pair finding 0.5, preparation 0.5, manifold writes 0.3 to 0.4, tight boxes 0.2, finalize and store 0.2. A scene has about 10.7k pairs per step of which 5.9k have a manifold (1.5k two-point), and the shape routine itself costs 45 ns for two boxes when run alone. Four changes were tried against the same binary, interleaved, five or six runs each, and none moved a scene by more than the run-to-run noise (about 3 %), so none was kept: skipping the fat-box overlap test for pairs where neither stored box changed in the step; sorting the pairs of a step by the kinds of their two shapes before the narrowphase (each result keeps its own slot, so no result changes); a broadphase displacement multiplier of 0 or 1 instead of 2 (fewer pairs, 10.7k to 9.6k in large-pile and 13.4k to 9.9k in tumbler, but no consistent step-time gain); and the kernel at 4 lanes (1.4 times slower than 8 lanes in the solver stage) or scalar (about 4 times slower). A 16-lane kernel would have to load two 8-lane groups as one vector, which means a group-pair layout and a staged call that hands the kernel two batches at once; on a machine with 256-bit execution units the expected gain is under a tenth of the solver stage, so it was not built.
- **Kernel width by platform.** `Core/KernelWidthChoice.cs` picks the default lane width of the wide kernels: 8 lanes where `Vector256` is a hardware vector, otherwise 4 lanes, so an 8-lane batch runs as two halves on arm64 and in the browser, whose vectors are 128 bits wide (a 256-bit vector there costs several instructions per operation). The separating-axis search loads shape B's points as four scalars per lane vector on the same platforms. Both are speed-only: batches stay eight lanes, every lane computes what the scalar code computes, and the hash matrix (including the runs with 256-bit vectors switched off, which take exactly these paths) shows the same hashes at every width.
- **Step audit and manifold recycling.** Counts per step on large-pile at one thread, taken from Box2D 3.2.0's own profile and counters (black box, through its documented API): 9650 contacts of which 91 % recycled (about 8800), 6550 touching; step 1.5 ms of which pair finding 0.27, collide 0.42, solve 0.83. Ours before recycling: 13.8k pairs, 8.9k narrowphase calls, 7.1k active manifolds, about 9.8 colors, 135 solver stages; pairs exceed Box2D's because boxes are stretched by twice the displacement. Both engines keep nearly all bodies awake in large-pile and tumbler, so sleeping is not the difference. The solver does the same number of passes, so the rest is constant factors. Recycling cuts narrowphase calls on large-pile from about 8.9k to 5.5k per step; interleaved one-thread runs against the previous merge (indicative, loaded machine, median of five): large-pile -6 %, mixed-terrain -13 %, pyramid-100 -17 %, tumbler +5 % (its contacts move too much to be recycled, so the attempt is overhead), sleep-wake +3 %.
- **Round 6: what was measured.** Per step on large-pile at one thread (ms, loaded machine): box tasks and proxy moves 0.15, pair finding 0.27, narrowphase stage 0.8, serial apply 0.09, manifold writes 0.2, preparation 0.16, solver passes 0.7, finalize/store/sleep 0.15. Box2D's own profile for the same scene has the whole solver (preparation included) at 0.83 ms, so the solver is level with it and the remaining gap sits in the contact update (about 1.5 ms against about 0.7). Pair counts barely matter: the broadphase margin from 0.03 to 0.2, or a displacement multiplier of 0, 1 or 2, moves large-pile and tumbler by less than 3 %, because the extra pairs are rejected by a cheap box test and fewer pairs mean more proxy moves and new pairs. Rebuilding the dynamic tree less often (threshold of a half or a quarter of the proxies instead of an eighth) changed nothing; rebuilding on every move was twice as slow on tumbler. Of the touching pairs of large-pile, 43 % are recycled; the rest fail on the motion bound (22 %), have a normal that is no face normal (20 %), or sank past the depth gate (15 %).
- **Kept in round 6.** Inside a color the contacts with one manifold point come first (a stable partition in `Engine2D.GroupSinglePointFirst`; colors holding a joint are left alone). The constraints of a color share no dynamic body, so grouping them differently cannot change a result, and a group of single-point contacts skips the second point in the warm start, solve, relax and restitution passes (`Engine2D.GroupPoints`). Recycled manifolds are written inside the parallel narrowphase stage, and that stage lists the pairs that need the serial apply loop, which then visits about a tenth of the pairs. An island is put to sleep after checking its bodies only up to the first one that has not rested. All of it is hash-neutral on every 2D scene.
- **Round 7: data layout was tried and is not the lever.** Per step on large-pile at one thread, the pair loop handles about 10.8k pairs: 3.4k are dropped by the box gap test, 2.5k recycled, 4.8k reach the narrowphase (3.3k of them with an existing constraint after a failed recycle test, 1.4k with no points). Repeating the whole narrowphase stage a second time on the same data, so that everything it reads is already cached, took 0.80 ms against 0.85 ms for the first run; the stage is computation, not waiting on memory. A per-shape record (fat box, tight box, step stamp, owner, material) and a per-body record (position, rotation, extent, sleep flag), both filled before the pair loop and read as one or two cache lines per shape and body in place of about ten scattered reads, left every pose bit-identical and moved step time by under 2 % on large-pile, tumbler, pyramid-100 and mixed-terrain at one and eight threads, so it was not kept. Unchecked array reads in the pair loop and dropping the 80-byte clear of the result slot were also within noise. A sampling profile of a settled large-pile puts the whole pair loop at about 30 % of the step, of which the narrowphase proper is 11 %, the recycle test 4 % and the pair loop's own code 15 %; the rest of the step is flat (solver kernels 20 %, preparation 6 %, manifold writes 6 %, pair finding 4 %).
- **Kept in round 7.** (1) *Clearance.* `Collide2D.Collide` can also report, for an empty manifold, a proven lower bound on the gap between the shapes beyond the speculative distance: the face value of the separating-axis search that ended it, minus the radii (for two discs the exact gap). A pair without a constraint stores B's pose in A's frame and that clearance (`Engine2D.SkipFrame`), and while B's origin has moved by less than the clearance minus the chord of its turn (the square root of two minus twice the cosine of the turn) times the distance of B's farthest point from its origin, minus a slack of 0.001 plus a millionth of the pose's size, the narrowphase is skipped, since no point can exist yet. The result is the one the narrowphase would give, so it is hash-neutral; the table is not part of a snapshot (the first step after a restore runs the narrowphase once) and a recycled pair slot starts cleared. On large-pile it removes 1.1k of 4.8k narrowphase calls per step. (2) *Write-through.* A pair that already has its constraint writes a fresh manifold inside the parallel stage, like a recycled one, while its payload is still cached from the recycle test; the serial stage only ever wakes bodies for such a pair. Pairs without a constraint, sensors and runs with a user mixer still write after the serial stage. Together about 3 % at one thread on large-pile, tumbler and mixed-terrain, nothing measurable at eight threads or on pyramid-100 and joint-grid; hash-neutral on every 2D scene.
- **What is left of the gap to Box2D v3 at one thread** (large-pile, ms per step on a loaded 32-core machine): contact update about 1.6 against about 0.7 for its pairs plus collide. Box2D's profile reports 91 % of contacts recycled; we recycle 43 % of touching pairs, and the failures are the normal that is no face normal (20 %: circles, capsules, vertex regions), the motion bound (21 %) and the depth gate (15 %). Each is a stability trade, not a layout problem: loosening the motion bound to count only sliding along the face (the part along the normal is tracked exactly by the separations) recycled 2 % more pairs and no more. Remaining candidates: recycling manifolds with a disc or vertex-region normal, fusing the body-pose reads into the pair-finding stage, and the solver's preparation (6 % of the step).
